# Posterior · `rec:posterior:incremental-model-updates`

This record owns the maintenance of the posterior: the bounded incremental update, the factorisation that keeps it sound and the verdict its refusal is, the policy that decides when to rebuild, and the marginalisation correction that may decline to run. It realises the update choice (`dec:numerics:incremental-update`), the cascade choice (`dec:numerics:repair-cascade`), the recomputation choice (`dec:numerics:recomputation-trigger`), and the marginalisation choice (`dec:numerics:marginalisation-correction`).

It is the densest record in the set. It reproduces none of the mathematics: every result it relies on is the specification's and is cited, and every mutation it performs is the substrate record's and is cited there (`dec:substrate:no-raw-access`).

**Decision (Read, decide, mutate — in three separated steps)** · `dec:posterior:three-step-update`

The update is three steps in a fixed order. The quantities the policy needs are read; the update the policy permits is decided; only then is the model mutated (`src/model/bayesian.rs`).

The separation exists to make the third step unconditional. A step that both inspects and mutates has to be able to undo itself, and undoing a rank-one update means reconstructing state the mutation destroyed — which is not the same computation run backwards but a second, weaker one. Deciding first means the mutation is always the one that was intended, so there is nothing to revert and no reverted state to be subtly wrong.

**Decision (Time and label decay combine into one factor)** · `dec:posterior:combined-factor`

Elapsed-time decay and label decay are combined into a single factor computed once per model per label, and applied once. They are not applied in sequence as two separate multiplications.

Combining them is what makes double application impossible rather than merely avoided. Two factors applied at two sites is an ordering problem and an idempotence problem at every site that touches a model; one factor computed at one point is neither. The factor's temporal half comes from the clock record's funnel and is bounded there (`dec:clock:shared-functions`); where in the label path it is applied is the ordering record's (`dec:ordering:decay-at-head`).

**Decision (An algebraic identity replaces a matrix-vector product)** · `dec:posterior:identity-substitution`

One matrix-vector product per model per update is replaced by an algebraic identity that produces the same quantity from values the update has already computed (`src/model/update.rs`).

The substitution is exact rather than approximate, which is why it is a decision about cost and not about accuracy. It is worth recording because it is the kind of optimisation that looks like a transcription error to a reader comparing the code against the specification's statement of the update (`alg:update:sherman-morrison`): the code computes something the written form does not name, and the reason is an identity rather than a divergence.

**Decision (Leverage is bounded before the update, never repaired after)** · `dec:posterior:leverage-before`

The observation's leverage is bounded by policy before the update is applied. The effective weight is reduced to satisfy the bound, and the update is then applied at that weight — there is no path on which a full-weight update is applied and subsequently corrected (`src/model/update.rs`).

The bound is what stops a single observation dominating the posterior, and the specification states both the bound (`prop:update:leverage-bound`) and the fact that the update is exact per observation rather than approximate (`inv:guarantee:per-observation-exactness`). Its interaction with the importance weighting is the specification's too (`disc:weighting:leverage-interaction`). Bounding before is the same reasoning as (`dec:posterior:three-step-update`), applied to the one quantity most likely to need it.

**Decision (Factorisation is attempted once, and its refusal is a verdict)** · `dec:posterior:repair-cascade`

Factorisation is attempted plainly. Its refusal is not retried under a diagonal shift: the refusal is the answer, and it says that the matrix in hand is not positive definite in the working arithmetic (``src/linalg/bridge.rs``).

The retired second phase added a diagonal shift, factored the shifted matrix, and returned that matrix's inverse. The arithmetic was sound and the report was not: the returned quantity is the inverse of a matrix the caller did not supply, offered under a variant the caller had to inspect to distinguish from an exact answer — and beneath the shift the backend's per-pivot backstop rewrote whatever pivot the shift had not reached, so what came back was the inverse of a third matrix again. What made this untenable was not the risk of a consumer forgetting to inspect but the frequency: the shift was reached routinely rather than exceptionally at feature widths above a thousand (`rep:assayer:synchronisation-drift-study`), so a mechanism justified as exceptional and observable was neither.

What survives that argument is its premise and not its second phase. The distinction between a healthy matrix and a repaired one is what everything downstream reads, and a refusal preserves that distinction better than a shift does, because a refusal is unambiguous where a success carrying a flag is a result the consumer must remember to interrogate.

The same shift is retained, at a different site and with a different meaning. Applied at the rebuild it is a declared floor on the posterior's spectrum, with a stated effect on every eigenvalue and a magnitude derived from the model's own reading (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`); applied inside the factorisation it was a claim about which matrix had been inverted. The arithmetic is identical and the epistemic status is opposite, which is why the move is a repair of this record rather than a weakening of it. What a refusal produces on the maintenance path is the numerics face of the structural posture rather than a second decision about failure (`rem:degradation:numerics-face`).

**Decision (The maintenance path never fails the call; the restore paths do)** · `dec:posterior:cascade-never-fails`

A factorisation refused on the periodic recomputation path does not fail the call. The terminus retains the maintained covariance and flags it; the call returns. A factorisation refused while restoring a model from a persisted artefact does fail the call, and the error reaches whoever asked for the restore.

The split is the error partition rather than an exception to it (`dec:degradation:error-partition`). A refusal on the maintenance path is the engine's own conditioning outrunning its arithmetic on state the engine built from valid input, and a party handed that error has no repair available — which is the ground the fallible assessment was rejected on (`disc:degradation:fallible-alternative`). A refusal on a restore path says the artefact does not carry a positive definite precision matrix, which is a defect of whoever wrote the artefact and one that can be acted on: an earlier checkpoint, a refusal to start, a rebuild from the journal.

A refusal on a restore path is the factorisation's own, and there is one factorisation to take it from (`dec:posterior:repair-cascade`). The disposition this record used to have to state separately — that an answer reached only under the diagonal shift is refused with the rest, because it is the inverse of a matrix the artefact does not carry — is now a property of the utility rather than a rule about how to read its answer. There is no shifted answer left to refuse: a restore either holds a matrix a factorisation accepts or holds nothing, and the refusal carries the pivot it stopped at.

All three restore entries take the verdict, and the disposition differs at each because the caller does. The restore the numeric checkpoint performs has the engine for its caller rather than a host, so its disposition is the label path's stop (`dec:surface:async-label`): the engine has judged its working copy corrupt, cannot rebuild it, and stops learning rather than continuing on the state it judged corrupt. The restore from a whole-state checkpoint has a host for its caller and happens while the instance is being constructed, so its refusal is a variant of the builder's error and construction fails (`dec:construction:eager-validation`). What that buys is the choice: a host holding the refusal can restore an earlier checkpoint, rebuild from the journal, or start clean, and it makes that choice knowingly rather than having the engine cold-start on its behalf and discard every learned parameter without saying so. An unreadable or structurally incompatible checkpoint keeps its cold start, because a deployment with no state to resume loses nothing it had; a checkpoint that was read and whose structure agreed is a different case and is not absorbed into that one. The restore of a hibernated block has the registration for its caller, and its refusal joins the enum that path already carries: the registration extends at the prior, which is a definite block by construction, so the fallback the algorithm names is also the fallback the verdict needs (`alg:registry:hibernation`).

Each verdict is a plain factorisation and not a spectrum. A factorisation is the cheapest available witness of definiteness, and no restore has one of its own to reuse; the two entries that install a matrix rather than inverting one pay exactly one for the matrix they install. What a diagonal reading would cost instead is the whole content of the repair: a strictly positive minimum diagonal is necessary for definiteness and is not sufficient for it (`req:gaussian:positive-definiteness`), so the cheap test admits artefacts the real one refuses, and both of the entries repaired here had nothing but the cheap test standing between a persisted matrix and the posterior.

What this record no longer claims is that the flag keeps the specification's precision statement a bound rather than a precondition. The statement it referred to was a spectral claim the replenishment floor does not support (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`), so the flag was standing in for a guarantee that was not there. The guarantee is made where it belongs, by a floor on the spectrum rather than by a note attached to a repaired result (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`).

**Decision (One shared inversion utility, with the disposition of a refusal left to the caller)** · `dec:posterior:one-inversion-utility`

Every site that needs an inverse from a factorisation calls one utility (``src/linalg/bridge.rs``). There is no second implementation, and in particular no second set of tolerances — the utility takes no numerical configuration at all, which is the strongest form of that rule rather than an exception to it, because a knob a call site could set is the second implementation this record forbids, reintroduced as a parameter.

What a call site does decide is the disposition of a refusal, and it decides it outside the utility. The utility reports that the factorisation was refused and which pivot refused it; whether that report becomes a retained-and-flagged covariance or a hard error is the caller's, because the answer depends on where the matrix came from and the utility cannot know that (`dec:posterior:cascade-never-fails`). The call-site tag the utility takes is diagnostic and selects no behaviour, which is the line worth keeping: varying the disposition by provenance is not varying the arithmetic by call site, and it is the arithmetic this record exists to keep single.

The alternative is what the corpus had. Two records specified this utility's interface incompatibly while each certified its own side as verified, which is the failure a shared utility with two owners produces — not a wrong tolerance but two right ones that disagree. The interface is now one interface, recorded at (`reg:posterior:collapsed-interface`).

**Decision (The cadence and the cheap check trigger a measurement, and the measurement decides whether to rebuild)** · `dec:posterior:recomputation-trigger`

What the counter arm of the per-label check fires is a *visit*, and a visit measures before it rebuilds. The measurement is the held pair's drift, the part of it the replenishment clamp put there, the residual left over, and the resolution of the reading those two were taken between — one matrix product for the drift and one for the resolution, and no spectrum and no factorisation. If the residual is good, nothing is rebuilt: the reading is recorded, the counter resets, and the interval recovers as a clean outcome always did. If it is bad, the rebuild runs, and what the rebuild's own measurement then says about its answer is the adoption test unchanged (`dec:posterior:measured-adoption`).

The split is a repair of a measured fault rather than a refinement. Under the arrangement it replaces, every arm fired the rebuild directly and the rebuild measured on its way through; the schedule's operational and sister models therefore paid a cubic factorisation every hundred labels, had nine in ten of them declined, and were held at the cadence's floor by those declines alone — with a residual three orders under their own threshold throughout (`rep:assayer:declined-rebuild-cadence`). The declines were correct and the models were healthy. What was wrong was that the question "is this model drifting" was being answered by an operation that only needed running once the answer was yes.

Recomputation is decided per model, not globally, and every arm of the per-label check is relative to the record the last visit wrote. The counter reaches the interval that visit's outcome set; the cheap diagonal ratio outgrows the ratio that visit recorded, by a configured factor (`alg:gaussian:condition-adaptive-recompute`); the count of dimensions resting at the replenishment floor moves off the count that visit recorded (`req:monitoring:replenishment-floor`). A measurement resets all three as a rebuild does, which is what keeps the counter arm from trapping now that most visits stop at the measurement — an arm relative to a record only a rebuild could write would fire on every label of a model the measurements keep finding healthy.

**Only the counter arm defers to the measurement, and which arms do is decided by what each of them detects.** The counter says that labels have been absorbed and the pair should be looked at — a question about drift between the two matrices, which is exactly the question the measurement answers. The conditioning-growth arm and the floored-count arm say that the precision matrix has moved away from the state the last rebuild's spectrum was read from, and that is a question about the matrix rather than about the pair. The measurement cannot answer it: a pair can be synchronised to the last bit while the matrix it is a pair of marches toward the edge of the definite cone. Nor can a measurement repair it, because what the staleness threatens is the spectral floor, and the floor is derived from that spectrum and applied at the rebuild and nowhere else (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`). So those two arms go to the rebuild whatever the measurement says, and the degenerate arm — a precision diagonal entry that is not finite or not positive — is the limiting case of the same argument and needs no separate one.

That disposition was measured rather than reasoned into place, and the first arrangement of it was wrong. With all three cheap arms deferring to the measurement, the width sweep's outcome-axis model — whose reading is almost entirely its replenishment clamp's, so its residual is always good — took no rebuild at all: its prior-induced reading climbed four orders past the base run's, its precision matrix took no floor for thousands of labels, and at a width of 1150 the factorisation was refused at pivot 1013 and the label path stopped after 3124 labels. The arm that had been firing for that model throughout was the floored-count arm, which was reporting precisely the staleness the floor exists to answer, and was being told that the pair looked fine. What the restructure set out to remove is untouched by the correction: the models the reading found pinned at the cadence's floor were pinned there by the counter arm, since nothing rests at their replenishment floor and their diagonal ratio grows slowly enough that the growth arm fires a handful of times over thousands of labels (`rep:assayer:declined-rebuild-cadence`).

The measurement is still taken on every one of these paths, because what it reads is the evidence the record and any alarm publish, and a non-finite reading is itself the report; it is the verdict that is not consulted. The factorisation is then attempted and its refusal is the answer, which retains the maintained pair and flags it (`dec:posterior:repair-cascade`).

Relative is the whole of the decision, and the alternative it replaces is what makes the case. The retired arm tested the diagonal ratio against a fixed threshold. The replenishment clamp puts the least diagonal entry at the floor on every label (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`), so once any dimension rests there the ratio is pinned above any threshold the floor implies and the arm is satisfied permanently. Worse, the rebuild it calls for recomputes the covariance from the precision matrix and never touches the precision matrix, so the quantity the arm tested is one the rebuild cannot move: the engine spent a cubic factorisation per label, for ever, to no effect. Substituting the true eigenvalue ratio for the diagonal one would have trapped identically, because the trap is a fixed threshold on a matrix property the rebuild does not change, not the crudeness of the proxy.

A test the rebuild resets cannot trap. That is why the growth arm compares against a recorded ratio rather than a constant, and why its rate is what recommends it: a rebuild writes the ratio it has just measured, so the arm fires at most once per multiplication of the ratio by the factor, which is logarithmic in how far the matrix has grown rather than proportional to the labels absorbed.

The counter's own justification survives unchanged and the conditioning arm's does not. The counter catches slow accumulation that no conditioning reading reveals. The retired arm was justified as catching a single bad update, and no such update exists: the label update's three mutations are a positive scaling, a positive-semidefinite diagonal addition and a positive-semidefinite rank-one addition, so a definite precision matrix cannot leave the definite cone by absorbing a label, and a weight that is not a non-negative number is refused before any mutation (`dec:posterior:three-step-update`). What remains for conditioning to say is that the matrix has moved away from the state the last rebuild certified, which is what the growth arm and the floored-count arm each say about their own quantity.

A model with no record — a fresh one, or one restored from a checkpoint written before the record existed — has nothing to be relative to, so only the counter and the absolute arm are available and the first rebuild establishes what the others need.

Divergence between the maintained factorisation and a fresh one is monitored at the rebuild rather than by this check (`alg:gaussian:synchronisation-monitor`), against the measure the specification fixes.

**Decision (Down fast, up slowly)** · `dec:posterior:adaptive-cadence`

A visit that found work to do shortens the effective interval, subject to a floor. Restoring it requires several consecutive visits that found none, after which the configured interval returns.

A visit reaches the cadence as one of four answers. A **measurement** found both components of the reading under the model's width-scaled threshold and nothing was rebuilt; the interval is unchanged. A **rebuild the residual called for** found drift the arithmetic had accumulated and removed it; the interval halves, subject to the floor, because drift that was really there is drift the cadence should visit sooner for. A **rebuild the replenishment clamp called for** found the residual under the threshold and the clamp's own contribution over it; the rebuild absorbs that contribution and the interval is *unchanged*. An **alarm** — a rebuild that was needed and left the model no better, or one the factorisation refused — takes the interval to the floor in one step and keeps the pair the model holds.

The third answer is the one that keeps the second from becoming the trap this record retired, and the distinction it rests on is the same one the residual rests on. The clamp's contribution scales with the interval and no factorisation reduces it below one interval's worth, so an interval that shortened on it would converge to the cadence floor and stay there. What is different from the retired arrangement is that the rebuild is not wasted: an adopted rebuild takes the covariance from the precision matrix the clamp has already raised, which is the one operation that absorbs the contribution and returns the reading to zero. So the clamp calls for the work that answers it and not for the shortening that does not, and the recovery run counts a visit of this kind as a visit that found no drift — which is what it is.

Three properties of that arrangement are worth stating because each replaces something the reading found wanting. **Recovery is spent out of visits that found no drift**, so a model that needed one rebuild can climb back to its configured interval without paying for three more; under the arrangement this replaces the run had to be earned in rebuilds, which is the cost the interval exists to avoid. **Only a rebuild the residual called for shortens**, so the cadence responds to drift rather than to having been asked; the two models the reading found pinned at the floor were shortening on rebuilds that were never called for, and there is no longer any such rebuild to shorten on (`rep:assayer:declined-rebuild-cadence`). And **the alarm goes to the floor rather than halving**, because halving is the proportional response to drift that is being removed and an alarm is precisely the case where none is: the model is looked at as often as the cadence permits until a measurement comes back good.

The threshold no longer appears in the cadence, and its absence is the point rather than a simplification. The verdict on the residual is taken at the measurement, where the resolution of the reading is also in hand, and by the time an outcome reaches the cadence the comparison has been made and the outcome names its answer. That is the monitor's own reading feeding the cadence rather than a second policy (`alg:gaussian:synchronisation-monitor`), and it is the same threshold the monitor already fixes.

The third input is a residual rather than a whole reading, and the reason is measured rather than assumed. The drift reading carries a component the prior put there and not the arithmetic: the replenishment clamp raises a diagonal entry whenever a coordinate has decayed below the floor, the maintained covariance does not follow that addition, and the pair therefore disagrees by the clamp's contribution seen through the covariance (`def:monitoring:synchronisation-error`). That component scales with the interval and not with the width, so a cadence acting on the whole reading shortens to its own floor on a model with coordinates resting at the floor and stays there, reacting to a quantity shortening cannot remove — paying a cubic factorisation at its maximum rate against a prior rather than against rounding. The behaviour was safe, the component being conservative in direction because the covariance is wider than the inverse exactly where the model has no evidence, and it was wasteful. The repair taken is the subtraction: the rebuild computes the component from the clamp mass accumulated since the last adopted rebuild, and the cadence is handed the remainder. The component the model tracks over its life is the wrong quantity to subtract and would be worse than subtracting nothing, because a rebuild absorbs everything the clamp added before it; a lifetime figure overstates the disagreement by that much, and an overstated component drives the residual to zero and blinds the cadence to rounding on exactly the models that carry a clamp. The alternative was to move the clamp to the recomputation as the spectral floor now is, which would make the pair consistent by construction and need no subtraction at all. It is refused on the requirement: the clamp is mandated after each forgetting step (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`), and deferring it to the recomputation lets a coordinate decay by the forgetting factor compounded over an interval below the floor the requirement exists to hold it at. The subtraction keeps the requirement intact and costs one $O(p^2)$ product per visit, where a rebuild is $O(p^3)$. The adoption test is unaffected and stays on the measured reading (`dec:posterior:measured-adoption`): after a rebuild both components are at zero together, so there is nothing there to separate.

The subtraction has a resolution, and the residual is published against it (`def:monitoring:synchronisation-error`). Where the prior's component is a modest share of the reading the remainder is a well-defined quantity; where that component dominates by many orders, the two operands agree to every digit the arithmetic could deliver and what is left is the rounding of the subtraction rather than any drift. That is worth reporting and it is not a verdict, and the difference was settled by measurement. The refinement was proposed as a verdict — an at-resolution residual would be good however far above the threshold it stood — and the falsifier vetoed it. The resolution is computed from the magnitudes of the two matrices the product was formed from, so a pair that has drifted apart by orders reports a resolution large enough to excuse any residual at all: the schedule's outcome-axis model reached a residual of `3.0e2` against a threshold of `1.2e-3` with the flag still true, and every measurement in that state called for nothing while the pair it held ceased to be an inverse pair. The flag is kept because what it says is true and a reader wants it; the threshold keeps the verdict.

The clamp's own contribution is read against the same threshold, and that arm was put there by the same falsifier. The contribution is what the replenishment clamp has added since the last rebuild seen through the covariance, and an adopted rebuild is the only thing that removes it — a measurement cannot, and waiting makes it worse, because it compounds as the covariance grows while the precision decays. With the residual alone deciding, the schedule's outcome-axis model ran its reading from `1.7e2` to `2.0e10` across a single rebuild, took no spectral floor for thousands of labels, and stopped the label path outright with a refused factorisation. Reading both components against the threshold the monitor already fixes costs no new constant and leaves untouched the models the restructure was built for, whose clamp contribution is exactly zero.

The asymmetry is the decision. Shortening on one bad outcome and restoring on one good one would let the cadence oscillate with the noise; requiring a run of good measurements to recover means the interval only lengthens on evidence that the condition has actually passed. This is the layer's only adaptive policy, which is why it is separated from the cascade: the cascade is a fixed procedure and this is a policy that learns.

**Decision (The precision matrix carries a spectral floor derived from its own spectrum)** · `dec:posterior:spectral-floor`

The posterior precision matrix's least eigenvalue is held at a floor, the floor is the least value that keeps the matrix factorisable in the working arithmetic rather than a figure the configuration carries, and it is applied at the recomputation rather than at the label. A floating-point Cholesky factorisation of a symmetric positive definite matrix runs to completion when its condition number satisfies the success condition stated in the width and the unit roundoff, so that condition rearranges into a bound on the least eigenvalue given the largest — and the largest is measured at every recomputation. The floor is therefore evidence rather than configuration: the recomputation reads the spectrum, derives the floor from the largest eigenvalue, adds the amount the least eigenvalue is short of it, factors the floored matrix, and installs that matrix together with the covariance taken from it.

Where the floor is applied is not a detail of the implementation but the decision itself, and the alternative was measured rather than reasoned away. An addition to the precision matrix that the covariance does not follow is read by the synchronisation monitor as drift, exactly and immediately: the residual gains the addition scaled by the covariance, and at a magnitude derived so that the least eigenvalue sits near the floor, that term is of order one. A per-label increment would therefore manufacture the drift the monitor exists to detect, and the adaptive cadence would read the model's own declared prior as a fault and shorten the interval in response to it. Correcting the covariance to match would cost a further cubic operation per label, which is the cost the whole cadence exists to avoid paying per label. The recomputation is the one place the covariance is being rebuilt anyway, so it is the one place an addition to the precision matrix costs nothing to follow — and there the pair comes out consistent by construction rather than by a correction.

Two consequences follow and neither is hidden. The floor is in force at every point at which the matrix is factorised, which is what factorisability actually requires, and between recomputations the decay scales the spectrum by a positive factor — which preserves definiteness exactly and costs only margin, bounded by that factor and absorbed by the next recomputation's own measurement. And the floor has nothing to say about a matrix that is already outside the definite cone: the amount that would carry a negative eigenvalue into the cone is not the least value the arithmetic requires but a repair of whatever went wrong, and a refused factorisation is a verdict rather than a condition to floor. Such a matrix goes to the factorisation and to the measurement of its answer unchanged.

Two rules make the floor a statement about the arithmetic rather than a ratchet on the model. It is set afresh at every recomputation and never carried forward: evidence that returns lowers the largest eigenvalue, which lowers the floor, and a floor that could only rise would be asserting a confidence the returning evidence had just refuted. And it is the least value that makes the arithmetic sound and never more: a measured least eigenvalue already above the floor asks for nothing at all, because the floor exists to keep the factorisation defined and not to assert anything about where the evidence has put the spectrum.

The floor is anti-conservative, and the size of that is why it is derived rather than chosen. Raising an eigenvalue of a precision matrix narrows the posterior along that direction, so every unit of floor is confidence the evidence did not supply. Deriving the least sufficient value bounds that claim at the smallest quantity for which the model's own factorisation is defined at all — of order a hundred-millionth at a width near a thousand, five orders below the coordinatewise replenishment floor (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`) — and publishing the share of the least eigenvalue the floor is carrying makes the claim readable rather than implicit (`dec:health:tiered-queries`). The coordinatewise floor and this one answer different questions and their magnitudes are not interchangeable: the shipped replenishment magnitude applied per label at this site would settle the least eigenvalue far above the prior precision, which is a modelling assertion no arithmetic requires.

The model tracks how much of its precision the two floors have supplied, because a floor whose size is known and whose contribution is not is only half a declaration. The spectral floor's contribution is identity-shaped, since every amount it adds is a multiple of the identity, so one scalar carries it — taking each recomputation's addition and decaying with the matrix between them; the replenishment clamp's contribution is coordinate-shaped, so a vector of the model's width carries it. Both are shares of the precision matrix and are scaled whenever it is scaled, and the coordinate-shaped one rides alongside it through every structural change — extended with zeros where a coordinate has just arrived and had no clamp applied to it, gathered under a permutation, and restricted to the coordinates a marginalisation keeps. With the two in hand the share of the posterior precision along any direction that the prior rather than the evidence is holding up is `(f · ‖φ‖² + Σ_j c_j φ_j²) / (φᵀ B φ)`, which is the aggregate degradation reading resolved to a direction. The identity-shaped scalar is uniform across coordinates and therefore slightly overstates the share on a coordinate that arrived after some of it had been added; the overstatement is bounded by the floor's own magnitude, orders below the prior precision a new coordinate starts at, and it errs toward reporting more prior than there is, which is the safe direction for a reading a consumer treats as a source of uncertainty.

The arrangement this replaces left the diagonal shift inside the factorisation, applied to a temporary while the model kept the unshifted matrix. That leaves the precision matrix and the covariance permanently not inverses of one another and reports the discrepancy as a persistent synchronisation error, which is a fault the monitor cannot recompute away because it is not drift (`alg:gaussian:synchronisation-monitor`). The same arithmetic, applied to the matrix the model will hold and followed by the covariance taken from it, is a declared floor with a stated magnitude that a host reads; applied to a temporary, it was a claim about which matrix had been inverted. The device is unchanged and its epistemic status is opposite, which is why the move is a repair of the record rather than a weakening of it.

**Decision (A rebuilt covariance is adopted on measurement, not on the factorisation's verdict)** · `dec:posterior:measured-adoption`

A rebuild offers a covariance, and the model adopts it only if the drift measured after the rebuild is no worse than the drift measured before it and is below the threshold the synchronisation monitor already fixes (`alg:gaussian:synchronisation-monitor`). Both readings are taken against the same precision matrix the model holds, never against a shifted or otherwise modified copy. A rebuild that fails the test leaves the maintained covariance in place.

The test's arithmetic is unchanged and what has changed is when it runs: only where a measurement has already found the drift worth acting on (`dec:posterior:recomputation-trigger`). That changes what a failure means. A rebuild used to run whether or not one was called for, so a decline was the ordinary answer on a healthy model — nine in ten on two of the schedule's four, every one of them correct, and each of them shortening an interval that had nothing to shorten for (`rep:assayer:declined-rebuild-cadence`). A rebuild now runs only against real drift, so a rebuild that leaves the model no better is a model whose drift is real and whose fresh inverse is worse than the pair it holds. That is the **alarm**: the pair is kept, the count is a count of alarms rather than of declines, a health event names the model and both drifts, the interval goes to its floor, and the detail tier carries it. There is no longer any decline that is not an alarm, because there is no longer any rebuild that was not needed.

The drift-before operand comes from the measurement rather than being retaken, which leaves the rebuild a function of the precision matrix alone: read its spectrum, floor it, factor it, and measure the answer against the matrix that was factored. The covariance the model currently holds does not enter the rebuild's arithmetic at all. That is not an economy — the reading was going to be taken either way — but it is the check on the split: a rebuild that still needed the maintained pair would be a rebuild the measurement had not fully separated from.

The verdict a factorisation returns is not a measurement of its answer's quality, and the two come apart wherever a device offers an answer about some other matrix. The retired regularised phase was such a device: it shifted the matrix, factored the shifted one, and returned that matrix's inverse as a success by verdict, and beneath the shift a per-pivot backstop fabricated an inverse of neither the supplied matrix nor the shifted one. Measuring the drift after the rebuild reads that directly, and costs one further product on a path that has just paid for a factorisation, so such an answer is refused by arithmetic rather than by a policy naming the device that produced it. The device is retired (`dec:posterior:repair-cascade`) and this test is not, because what it excludes is a class rather than a mechanism: any rebuild whose covariance is not the inverse of the matrix the model will hold fails it, whatever produced the covariance.

Adopting on a reading no worse than the previous one, rather than on a strictly better one, is deliberate. A rebuild of a matrix that has not moved reproduces the covariance the model already holds, so its two readings are equal; refusing that would refuse the healthy fixed point and shorten the interval on a model that is behaving. What the test must exclude is a rebuild that makes the disagreement worse, and a reading that is equal has not.

**Decision (The marginalisation correction uses a half-solve)** · `dec:posterior:half-solve`

When a dimension leaves, the correction is computed through a half-solve against the Gram matrix rather than a full solve, over the Schur complement the specification defines (`def:gaussian:schur-complement`) and realising the marginalisation result (`thm:gaussian:marginalisation`).

The half-solve produces the same correction for roughly half the work, by stopping at the intermediate the correction is a product of rather than completing the solve and multiplying back. It is worth naming because the saving is invisible in the output: the two paths agree to rounding, so nothing about the result reveals which was taken, and a maintainer restoring the full solve would see no test fail.

**Decision (The correction is infallible)** · `dec:posterior:infallible-correction`

Marginalisation does not fail. Where the correction cannot be computed, the uncorrected block is used and the operation returns (`src/model/marginalise.rs`). There is no error path out of a dimension leaving the model.

The fallback is sound rather than merely convenient: the uncorrected block is a valid, conservative posterior over the surviving dimensions, not a damaged one. What it loses is the sharpening the correction would have supplied. The positive-definiteness the specification requires (`req:gaussian:positive-definiteness`) holds on both paths, which is what makes declining an option at all — and it holds on the fallback path by interlacing, because a principal submatrix of a positive definite matrix is positive definite. That premise is the parent's definiteness, which the model now guarantees rather than assumes (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`), so the fallback's soundness is a consequence of the invariant rather than a separate hope.

**Decision (A conditioning guard skips the correction)** · `dec:posterior:conditioning-guard`

Before the correction is attempted, the conditioning of the block it would be computed from is estimated. If the estimate crosses the guard threshold the correction is skipped and the uncorrected block is taken (`src/model/marginalise.rs`).

The guard declines a computation it expects to fail rather than attempting it and handling the failure. The difference is not performance: an ill-conditioned solve does not reliably fail, it returns a number, and the failure modes that produce a plausible wrong correction are the ones no downstream check would catch. Skipping on the estimate is the only point at which the distinction is still available. The guard's threshold lives in the code and is not transcribed here.

The premise this makes explicit is that the same reasoning governs a factorisation, and for a time the corpus applied it here and not there. A factorisation retried under a diagonal shift does not fail either: it returns a number, and the number is the inverse of a matrix nobody supplied. That is this guard's own failure mode at a neighbouring site, and the phase that produced it has been retired on exactly this argument (`dec:posterior:repair-cascade`).

**Decision (The full solve is retained as a debug-only oracle)** · `dec:posterior:debug-oracle`

The full solve is kept, and in debug builds it recomputes the correction independently and compares it against the half-solve's result (`src/linalg/bridge.rs`). It is compiled out of release builds and costs nothing there.

Retaining it gives the cheap path something to be wrong against. A hand-derived optimisation whose only check is that its output looks reasonable is unverified in the way that matters, because the quantity it produces has no independently known value. The oracle supplies one. This is the same discipline the specification's results are held to, applied to an implementation shortcut the specification does not describe.

The tolerance the comparison is made at is derived from what the two routes compute rather than chosen to fit them. Both routes solve against the same Cholesky factor, so the factorisation's own error is common to them and cancels out of their difference: what separates the two answers is the error of the substitutions and of the products and nothing else, which is what makes a bound on their difference derivable rather than estimated. Each substitution is componentwise backward stable, so its error carries the factor's componentwise conditioning, and that conditioning is evaluated through the comparison matrix's entrywise bound on a triangular inverse — one forward substitution whose every operation is an addition or a division by a positive pivot, so no inverse is formed and the auxiliary solve contributes no cancellation of its own. To that is added each product's own rounding, at the standard growth factor for a sum over the factor's order, and the two routes' bounds are summed. Every term is stated against the operands the products actually round rather than against the answer they produce, which is the whole of what makes the bound hold where the two earlier forms did not.

Those two were retired in an order that is itself the argument. The first was a fixed absolute distance, `1e-12`, which falls below a single representable step once an entry's magnitude passes about `4.5e3` and is therefore a bound no correct arithmetic can meet there; a correction near `5.09e3` whose two forms stood two ulp apart tripped it, reporting agreement to the last bit the format carries as a fault (`claim:linalg:the-oracle-holds-where-a-fixed-distance-falls-under-one-ulp`). The second set a plain relative term beside that floor, and was short in two independent ways: it dropped the substitution's conditioning entirely, which is precisely the term the coordinatewise replenishment floor creates, since that floor is where the factor's smallest pivot comes from (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`); and it put the computed answer where the sum of the terms' magnitudes belongs, understating that sum by however much the entry cancelled. Stating the bound against the operands repairs both at once and retires the absolute floor with them, because an absolute floor was needed only to stop a bound written against the answer from shrinking to nothing when the answer does.

What the derived bound is worth at the geometry the engine spends its time in is rigorous rather than tight, and the falsifiers measure that gap rather than assert it. At a removed block whose near-collinear features leave every eigenvalue but the leading one at the replenishment floor, an eight-wide factor and two hundred kept dimensions, the two correct routes disagree at their worst entry by `7.84e-16` of that entry's own magnitude — three and a half ulp — while the bound allows `1.733e-7` of it, a factor of `2.2e8` between what the arithmetic does and what the bound permits. That factor is the comparison matrix being the worst case over every factor with those magnitudes rather than the realisation, which is a known pessimism of the bound on an ill-conditioned triangular factor and not an error in it, and its consequence is worth stating plainly: at the floor this oracle audits nothing finer than one part in ten million (`claim:linalg:the-derived-bound-admits-the-rounding-a-floored-factor-produces`). Every one of the five failures recorded against the retired relative term is admitted, and admitted even under the assumption that flatters that form most, where the narrowest margin — on the entry of magnitude `2.329e2` — is 1.4 per cent, and far wider at the conditioning actually present.

What that price leaves is still an oracle, because the divergence an oracle exists to report is not a last-bit one. A solve against a block regularised a hundred times more heavily, a coupling gathered one column out, a correction assembled from the coupling block where the half-solve's own transpose belongs: each moves an entry by a share of its own magnitude rather than by its last bits, and at the floored geometry the three of them move their worst entry by 0.81 to 0.99 of its own size, which is `2.3e7` to `4.9e7` times what the bound allows there (`claim:linalg:the-derived-bound-still-refuses-a-faulted-route-at-the-floor`). The conditioning term that admits the rounding therefore refuses the driven faults by seven orders of magnitude. A bound tighter than the error of the arithmetic it audits would report the arithmetic rather than the algorithm; what the floor's conditioning costs is sensitivity to divergences below a part in ten million, and what an oracle exists to report is parts in one.

The same discipline governs the oracle's own budget as governs the shortcut it audits. A tolerance chosen to cover the disagreements that had been seen would carry the defect the oracle exists to remove — a quantity with no independently known value — so the budget is the least width the arithmetic makes necessary and no more, computed at each comparison from the operands in front of it rather than carried as a constant. That is the standing the model's own floor is held to, derived at each rebuild from a measured spectrum rather than configured (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`). None of it reaches a release binary: the profile that compiles the oracle out has no tolerance to compute, and none a subscriber can switch on (`claim:linalg:the-release-profile-has-no-oracle-for-a-subscriber-to-switch-on`).

**Decision (The covariance is always refactored)** · `dec:posterior:always-refactor`

After a marginalisation the covariance is recomputed by factorisation. It is never taken from the block extracted during the correction, even where that block is numerically the same quantity (`src/model/bayesian.rs`).

Taking the extracted block would inherit whatever the correction did — including the uncorrected fallback and any regularisation the factorisation applied — into a matrix that the assessment path reads on every request (`dec:substrate:covariance-only`). Refactoring costs one factorisation per lifecycle event, which is rare, and buys a covariance whose provenance is one operation deep rather than a chain of conditionals.

**Register (The one interface and the one signature this record collapses)** · `reg:posterior:collapsed-interface`

Two legacy records shared the inversion utility and specified it incompatibly. One passed a context struct; the other passed a bare call site and documented the divergence as a departure from a sketch, without amending the record holding the sketch. The same pair gave the marginalisation entry point two different signatures and its failure type two different shapes, both citing a third record as their source.

Both records' guarantee tables certified their own side as verified, so the corpus held two mechanically unfalsifiable certifications of contradictory states. The census recorded it with its evidence (`reg:assayer:adr-contradictions-records`). The code adjudicates, and there is now one utility (`dec:posterior:one-inversion-utility`) with one signature, stated once.

**Discussion (The rejected mutate-then-inspect-and-revert)** · `disc:posterior:revert-alternative`

The alternative was to apply the update, inspect the result, and revert if the policy was violated. It is the shorter arrangement and it puts the check where the evidence is most complete, since the post-update state is the thing the policy is really about.

It fails on the revert. Reverting a rank-one update to a factorisation is not the update run backwards: the factorisation has been modified in place and the information needed to restore it exactly is gone, so a revert reconstructs an approximation of a state that was previously exact. The arrangement would trade a policy check for a silent loss of exactness on precisely the paths where the policy fired.

**Caveat (The posterior can become ill-conditioned)** · `cav:posterior:conditioning`

Nothing here claims the posterior stays well-conditioned. It can degrade, and the specification records that it can (`cav:limitation:conditioning`). Three landed devices are what the system does about that, and naming them is not a claim that they prevent it. One factorisation is attempted plainly and its refusal is the answer rather than a prompt to retry under a shift (`dec:posterior:repair-cascade`). A per-label check decides when the pair is measured, every arm of it relative to the record the last visit wrote, so no arm can be satisfied permanently by a quantity a visit does not move, and the covariance is rebuilt only where that measurement finds drift worth acting on (`dec:posterior:recomputation-trigger`). And a floor derived at each rebuild from the matrix's own largest eigenvalue holds the least one where the working arithmetic can still factorise it (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`). Each is a response to ill-conditioning; none is evidence that it will not arrive.

The distinction matters for a reader deciding what to trust. A degraded posterior produces assessments that are conservative rather than wrong — the flags travel with them — but the sharpening a well-conditioned model would supply is absent, and nothing in an assessment's own numbers separates a model whose confidence its evidence earned from one whose spectrum its floors are holding up. What separates them is carried beside those numbers rather than in them: the degradation state that accompanies the result, the share of the least-confident direction the spectral floor is supplying (`dec:health:tiered-queries`), and, per request and per direction, the share of the precision behind that verdict that the models' floors rather than their evidence account for (`dec:risk:evidence-only-uncertainty`). A consumer that does not read those reads a number it cannot tell apart from a sharper one.

**Entry (Schur correction skip counter)** · `entry:posterior:skip-counter`

Completed, and the register entry this deferral migrated from is retired at (`entry:assayer:defer-schur-skip-counter`). The working copy carries a ledger of what marginalisation has cost; the helper that marginalises every full-dimension model folds each model's diagnostics into it, and the health report's skipped-correction field is read from that ledger rather than filled with a literal. The completion trigger this entry set is met, and passed: what a host reads is not only how many corrections were skipped but what approximation each event committed, labelled as a measurement or as a bound (`req:gaussian:marginalisation-error-reported`). **Entry (Synchronisation-error visibility)** · `entry:posterior:sync-visibility`

Completed, and the register entry this deferral migrated from is retired at (`entry:assayer:defer-sync-error-visibility`). The specified Frobenius residual is retained per model at recomputation and travels with each operational, sister, anchor and outcome-axis precision-health entry — and it no longer travels alone. Each entry carries three figures where it once carried one: the measured reading the definition fixes, the part of that reading the replenishment clamp put there rather than the arithmetic, and the residual left when that part is taken out (`def:monitoring:synchronisation-error`). Each entry also carries the resolution of the reading those figures were taken between and whether the residual stood at or below it, its current effective interval, and the lifetime counts of the three dispositions a visit can take: the visits that measured and rebuilt nothing, the rebuilds paid for, and the alarms. The compact health summary reports the maximum of each of the three across the live models rather than a constant, so a reader of the compact tier can tell a model leaning on its prior from a model whose arithmetic has drifted without descending to the detailed one.

The shortening rule is derived without a tuning choice, and what it reads is the residual against two bars rather than one. The synchronisation definition and configuration definition fix the strict threshold at $10^{-6} \cdot p$ (`def:monitoring:synchronisation-error`), (`def:config:synchronisation-threshold`). A visit whose residual *and* whose prior-induced component are both at or under that model-width threshold rebuilds nothing and retains the existing three-visit recovery to the configured interval. A residual over the threshold rebuilds and, where that rebuild is adopted, halves the interval; a prior-induced component over it rebuilds too, because that is the one operation which absorbs it, and leaves the interval alone; and a rebuild that is not adopted goes to the existing floor of one hundred (`dec:posterior:adaptive-cadence`). The resolution the reading carries is published beside the residual as a reading and is not a third bar. The threshold is unchanged and so is the arithmetic that derives it — what changed is the quantity it is applied to, on the ground the cadence record argues: the prior's component of the reading scales with the interval rather than with the width, so an arm reading the whole figure shortens against a quantity shortening cannot remove. The whole reading remains what this surface publishes under the definition's name, and is not what the arm tests.

Focused tests prove the helper rule and the live model both halve, reset and count on a rebuild that was needed and adopted (`claim:bayes:a-needed-rebuild-shortens-the-interval-and-restarts-recovery`), (`claim:bayes:the-live-model-shortens-and-restarts-recovery-on-a-needed-rebuild`), and that a visit which found nothing to do leaves all three alone. The export test distinguishes four model identities, proves the aggregate is their maximum, and proves the detailed tier preserves each model's own error, interval and shortening count (`claim:wellness:a-maintained-synchronisation-error-reaches-both-export-tiers`).

The completion trigger is met: the measured reading is surfaced per model and in aggregate, the component the prior contributes and the residual are surfaced beside it at both tiers, and a residual over both of its bars shortens the interval.
