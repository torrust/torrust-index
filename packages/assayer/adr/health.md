# Health · `rec:health:diagnostic-publication`

This record owns what the package may be asked about its own condition: how that condition is published, how it is queried, and the single discipline that governs all of it — it reports, and it never acts. It realises the health choice (`dec:contracts:health-surface`) and the reporting half of the convergence choice (`dec:operational:convergence-diagnostic`).

Eight deferrals migrated here, the widest gap in the set between what a record decides and what the implementation had built. That count was a measurement rather than a defect: this is the surface where the package describes itself, and a description is the last thing to be finished. It has since been worked down to one. Six of the eight are completed and state the code that closed them; one is retired as superseded rather than built; and the single entry still open states the gap, the code that proves it is still open, and what would close it.

Twelve decisions stand against those eight, and three of them fix figures. That is a departure from this corpus's usual posture on values, taken for the reason the degradation record gives at length where it takes the same one (`dec:degradation:guard-magnitudes`): the figures below have no authority anywhere else, so a record stating them is the original rather than a second copy. Everything they cost is a reading, because convergence is reported and never enforced (`dec:health:reports-never-gates`), and that is what makes a shipped operating point an acceptable answer where no derivation is available.

**Decision (Health publishes independently of the model snapshot)** · `dec:health:independent-publication`

Health state reaches readers through its own swap, on its own cadence, so neither publication waits on the other. The swap discipline and the independence are the retention record's (`dec:retention:health-independent`); what this record adds is why the independence is worth its second allocation.

A health summary that published with the model would be exactly as stale as the model, which is the opposite of what a monitor wants. The reader most in need of health is the one watching a system that has stopped publishing — and to that reader a health value welded to the publication cadence answers by falling silent at the moment it was wanted. Independence is what lets health report *about* the model rather than *with* it.

**Decision (Events are pushed through one bounded channel that drops with a counter)** · `dec:health:bounded-events`

Health events go to consumers through a single bounded channel. On overflow the channel drops and increments a counter (`src/health/events.rs`); it does not block the producer and it does not grow without bound.

Both refusals matter and they are refusals of different things. Blocking would put an observer on the critical path, so a slow consumer of diagnostics could slow the label path that produces them — the monitor becoming the fault. Growing without bound would trade a bounded loss for an unbounded one. Dropping with a counter keeps the cost fixed and keeps the loss *visible*, which is the only property that makes a dropped event acceptable: a consumer can tell that it missed something, and how much.

**Decision (Queries are tiered by cost)** · `dec:health:tiered-queries`

There is more than one way to ask, and they cost different amounts: a cheap summary suitable for frequent polling (`schema:output:health-snapshot`) and a fuller report for a caller that wants everything (`src/api/health.rs`).

The tiering exists because a single query would have to be priced for its most expensive reader. A monitor scraping every few seconds and an operator investigating an incident want different things, and collapsing them forces the first to pay for the second's detail on every poll. The alternative — one report with the expensive parts made optional by argument — is the same tiering with a worse interface, since the caller would have to know which arguments were the costly ones.

Two consequences of the tiering govern how a per-model reading is published, and both are visible in the precision readings. The first is that a reading a model has not taken is absent in the detailed tier rather than filled with a stand-in: the true condition number and the spectral floor's readings exist only from a model's first recomputation, and a field promising a measured quantity must carry its absence rather than a number that would be indistinguishable from a measurement (`def:monitoring:condition-number`). The second is that the compact tier has one number per quantity and therefore cannot carry that absence; its aggregate is a maximum across models, and a model with no reading contributes nothing to it, which is the identity for a maximum rather than a claim that the model is healthy. A caller that needs the difference asks the tier that keeps it.

A quantity whose reading has two meanings is published as both of them and as the reading itself, and the synchronisation error is the case that settled the shape. The measured reading is what its definition fixes (`def:monitoring:synchronisation-error`) and is published under that name unchanged. Beside it go two more figures per model: the part of the reading the replenishment clamp put there, and the residual left when that part is taken out. The compact tier carries the maximum of each of the three across models, and the maxima are taken separately rather than derived from one another, because the model leaning hardest on its prior and the model carrying the most rounding need not be the same model. Publishing only the residual would hide a model propped up by its prior, which is a convergence-state signal a host wants; publishing only the measured reading is what the surface did before, and it cannot distinguish a model that is drifting from one that is merely exhausted. The two are read differently and neither substitutes for the other: a large prior-induced component is conservative rather than wrong, since the covariance is wider than the inverse exactly along the directions the model has no evidence for, while a rising residual is drift the maintenance has accumulated. Only the residual is acted on, by the cadence (`dec:posterior:adaptive-cadence`); the other two are reported and nothing downstream moves on them. A fourth figure is published beside the three and is neither a component nor a derived ratio: the resolution the reading carries, and a flag saying whether the residual stood at or below it. It says how much of the residual the arithmetic that produced it could have manufactured, and a reader cannot reconstruct it — it is read off the magnitudes of the two matrices the product was formed from, and neither of those is on any tier (`def:monitoring:synchronisation-error`). It is a reading and not a verdict: the cadence judges both components against the threshold and this figure does not excuse either of them, which is a distinction the surface has to keep because the two look alike on a row.

One reading is carried on both tiers and is the exception the tiering allows for rather than a breach of it. The label path's stop is a condition and not a measurement: it says the engine has stopped applying labels, which is the one thing a frequent poller must not have to buy the expensive tier to discover, so the compact tier carries it as a flag. What the compact tier cannot carry is why — the model whose reconstruction was refused, the pivot, and how far into the label stream it happened are a cause rather than a scalar, and what a host does about the stop depends on all three (`dec:surface:async-label`). So the report carries the cause whole, and its presence is the report's own flag: a second boolean beside it would be two descriptions of one position, which is the shape this record's own aggregates avoid.

The derived readings are published beside the raw ones they are derived from, not instead of them. The share of a model's least-confident direction held up by the spectral floor rather than by evidence is a ratio of two published quantities — the least eigenvalue measured at the last recomputation and the floor then in force (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`) — so a host that disagrees with the derivation can compute its own from the same tier. Publishing the ratio alone would make the package's arithmetic the only arithmetic available, which is the shape a health surface should avoid wherever the inputs cost nothing extra to carry.

A reading whose meaning is a comparison is published with the figure it is compared against, and it stays on the tier that can carry both. The detailed precision health carries three readings of a model's last rebuild — the drift measured after it, what the factorisation answered, and whether the offered covariance was adopted — beside the drift that stood before it, which the surface already carried. The pair is the point: a rebuild is declined either because it was no better than what the model already held or because it was better and still over that model's width-scaled threshold (`dec:posterior:measured-adoption`), and a surface carrying only the earlier figure and a count of declines leaves a reader able to see that rebuilds are being discarded and unable to see which of the two conditions is discarding them. The three share one absence and are absent together until a rebuild has run, for the same reason the spectral readings are. What does not follow them onto the compact tier is the after-drift, and the reason is the tiering's own: that tier's aggregate is a maximum across models, and a maximum would pair one model's after-drift with another's before-drift and with a threshold scaled to a third model's width, which is a figure no reader can act on without descending to the tier that keeps the readings together. The verdict and the adoption flag are not quantities a maximum is defined on at all.

**The last measurement and the last rebuild are separate groups on that tier, and separating them is what keeps either group readable.** Every visit to a model takes the drift readings; only a visit that found them bad rebuilds (`dec:posterior:recomputation-trigger`). So the readings above — the measured drift, the prior-induced part, the residual, the resolution and its flag, and the labels the model had absorbed when they were taken — belong to the last *visit*, and the three readings of a rebuild belong to the last *rebuild*, which on a healthy model is a long way further back. Publishing them as one group would date the whole group to whichever event was older, which on the models this arrangement exists to help is exactly the group a reader wants current. The labels-at figure is what places the first group: the same residual read four labels after a rebuild and a thousand labels after one are different statements about the same model.

**The counts are three and they are disjoint.** A visit measured and rebuilt nothing, or it rebuilt and the result was adopted, or it rebuilt and the result was not — the alarm. Their sum is the number of visits, and their ratio is the reading a host actually wants: a model accumulating measurements with its rebuild count flat is one the cadence is visiting and not rebuilding. The alarm count is the one that used to be a count of declines, and its meaning moved with the flow around it rather than with any change to the adoption test: a decline on a rebuild that was never called for is a routine outcome and a decline on one that was is a condition (`rep:assayer:declined-rebuild-cadence`). Nothing here becomes a metric. The catalogue carries one precision counter, the rebuilds at which the spectral floor repaired the matrix, and its meaning is untouched; the three counts are read from the tiers by a caller that has asked for them (`dec:metrics:catalogue-membership`).

**Decision (Discrimination metrics are computed once at refit and cached)** · `dec:health:cached-discrimination`

Discrimination is computed when the calibration is refitted and cached until the next refit, rather than recomputed for each query. What refit produces is the calibration record's (`dec:calibration:pure-refit`), and this record decides that its by-products are retained.

The reason is that the quantity does not change between refits. Discrimination is a property of the fitted calibration, so recomputing it per query would spend a scan over the buffer to obtain a number that provably has not moved, and would make the cost of monitoring scale with how often anyone looked. What the specification requires be visible is fixed elsewhere (`inv:guarantee:discrimination-visibility`); caching is how it is afforded.

**Decision (The report type is non-exhaustive)** · `dec:health:non-exhaustive-report`

The report type is declared non-exhaustive, so a field may be added without breaking a host that matches on it (`src/health/summary.rs`).

This is the one place the record designs for its own incompleteness, and the eight deferrals below are why. The realistic future of this surface is that fields arrive, one at a time, as the computations behind them land — and a host that had to be recompiled for each arrival would make the deferrals expensive to discharge. Non-exhaustiveness converts each of those arrivals from a breaking change into an addition, which is what makes the deferral load below a schedule rather than a debt.

**Decision (Each process carries its own concrete tracker)** · `dec:health:concrete-trackers`

Every converging process owns a concrete convergence tracker of its own — the calibration tracker (`src/health/platt_tracker.rs`) and the identity tracker (`src/health/identity_tracker.rs`) among them. There is no shared trait over trackers and no global convergence state machine.

The processes converge on different evidence and at different rates, and a shared trait would have to name the intersection of what they all report — which is smaller than what any of them knows. A global state machine is worse in a second way: it would make convergence a thing the system *has* rather than a thing each process has, and the composite below shows why that is the wrong shape. Concrete trackers keep each measurement in the units its process actually produces.

**Decision (The composite stage is computed on demand and may regress)** · `dec:health:on-demand-composite`

The overall stage is derived from the individual trackers when it is asked for (`src/health/composite.rs`), over the stages the specification fixes (`tab:warmup:stages`). It is not maintained incrementally and it does not ratchet: after a lifecycle change it may report an earlier stage than it did before.

Regression is the honest answer rather than a flaw. Registering a new entity genuinely returns part of the system to an earlier stage, and a composite that ratcheted would report a maturity the system no longer has — permanently, since nothing would ever lower it. Computing on demand is what makes the regression possible: a stored stage would have to be explicitly walked backwards by every path that could invalidate it, and the milestones this reads against (`tab:warmup:milestones`) are the specification's.

**Decision (Convergence is reported and never enforced)** · `dec:health:reports-never-gates`

No path is blocked, no output is withheld, and no behaviour changes because a tracker has or has not converged. The package reports its condition and acts on none of it (`inv:monitoring:report-only`).

This is the record's central discipline and the reason it can be read independently of every other. A health surface that gated would be a control surface wearing a monitor's name, and its every measurement would acquire a second meaning — not *what is true* but *what will be permitted*. It would also make health a dependency of correctness, so a defect in a diagnostic could withhold a result. What a reader may trust before the first refit is the specification's (`rem:warmup:pre-calibration`), which is a statement about maturity precisely because nothing here enforces it.

**Decision (The standardisation transition is reported, not inferred)** · `dec:health:standardisation-phase-reported`

The cold ramp's phase and its accepted-observation count are reported quantities. They travel both on the compact summary riding every result and in the full report this record's swap publishes, so a host reads the same two facts wherever it is looking — with the compact pair describing the coordinate state that result was actually scored in rather than the ramp's position at the moment of reading. The inline carrier belongs to the surface record (`dec:surface:inline-health`) and the two objects remain the distinct things this record keeps them (`rem:health:inline-versus-queried`); what is decided here is that the transition appears on both of them.

Reported rather than inferred is the substance, because inference is available and is wrong. A reader given only the moments can estimate the phase by counting how many variances still sit at the floor, and that estimate fails in both directions: it cannot separate a position whose observed variance is genuinely small from one the ramp has not yet moved, and it says nothing whatever about how far along the ramp has come. A quantity a reader must reverse-engineer from the state it is about is not a diagnostic but a guess wearing one's clothes, and it is the kind of guess that ends up written into a host's alerting and then quietly contradicted.

Two refusals of a second field follow. Maturity is the reported count against the ramp's horizon and is not stored beside it (`dec:vector:prior-mass-ramp`), because a stored fraction and a stored count are two descriptions of one position and this corpus has paid enough for what happens when two descriptions of one thing are maintained apart. And the coordinate state a result was computed in is named by the model snapshot version the compact summary already carries, because every accepted advance publishes a snapshot (`dec:ordering:score-before-evolve`) — so the join key the health stream already has *is* the coordinate version, and a second version namespace would be a second authority over one fact. None of this gates anything: the phase is read and the package acts on none of it (`dec:health:reports-never-gates`).

**Decision (A dimension's stability is read on two coordinates calibrated to agree)** · `dec:health:identity-stability-cuts`

The identity tracker (`dec:health:concrete-trackers`) puts each registered dimension on a five-stage ladder, and it decides the stage from two smoothed statistics of the competitive set's movement: the overlap between consecutive sets, and the fraction of the set that changed. Each statistic has a volatile cut and a stable cut. The reading is the pessimistic one — a dimension is volatile if either coordinate says so, and eligible for the top only if both do — and a dimension that has cleared both cuts still waits out an age gate before it is called settled.

The two coordinates are not two independent tests, and the record's substantive claim is that they are calibrated against each other. The churn cuts are the choice; the overlap cuts are derived from them. Under the movement the tracker is built to read — cells replacing cells at roughly constant set size — a churn rate of one twentieth produces an overlap of about nine tenths, and a churn rate of three tenths produces an overlap of about eleven twentieths; the overlap cuts are those two figures taken to one decimal place. So wherever the set turns over, the disjunction is a redundancy in which both coordinates say the same thing. It separates only where the set is growing or shrinking instead, which is exactly the movement the two statistics genuinely disagree about and the movement a single coordinate would read wrongly.

Rounding to one decimal leaves the churn coordinate marginally the tighter of the pair at both cuts: an overlap of nine tenths corresponds to a churn a little over one twentieth, and an overlap of one half to a churn a third rather than three tenths. Under pure replacement it is therefore churn that speaks first and overlap that confirms. The asymmetry follows from the derivation rather than being a further choice, and it is stated so that a reader who recomputes the correspondence and finds it inexact knows the discrepancy is the rounding.

What the ladder is not is anywhere else. These figures are absent from the construction contract's parameters (`tab:construction:parameters`), no chapter of the specification names them, and nothing a host may set reaches them — a claim that needs the caveat below to be read honestly, because a host-settable surface named for exactly these stages does exist and does nothing. The table below is where the figures are fixed.

**Caveat (One age gate stands against a deployment-dependent arrival)** · `cav:health:identity-maturity-arithmetic`

The gate now follows the specification's convention and the caveat that remains is a different one. The budget counts *eligible* labels and the specification divides it by the rate at which eligible labels arrive, naming that as the single convention used wherever a convergence figure appears (`bound:resource:convergence-budget`); at the standard deployment twice six hundred and thirty-eight parameters is one thousand two hundred and seventy-six eligible labels, the reference rates deliver a hundred and twenty of them a day, and the arrival is ten and two thirds days. The gate is the whole day above that, eleven, and the rounding is as tight as the figure it replaced was — the gate clears the arrival by a third of a day rather than padding it.

What it replaced divided the same budget by the raw two hundred labels a day, for a little over six days, and took a week as the round figure above that. The consequence was that a dimension at the reference deployment read settled about three and a half days before the weights behind it had their labels. That is repaired rather than recorded, because the gate answers to the specification's convention and a gate that clears the arrival is the whole point of having one, and the arithmetic above is pinned by a test that recomputes it from the specification's figures and refuses the raw-rate division.

The residual is that the arrival is a property of the deployment and the gate is one constant. The specification's own table runs from about four and four fifths days at the minimal deployment to about fifteen days at the large one, and eleven days covers the minimal and the standard while falling some nine hours short of the standard-plus and some four days short of the large. The standard-plus is the one worth spelling out, because the shortfall there is small enough to have been overlooked and was: its six hundred and eighty-two parameters make one thousand three hundred and sixty-four eligible labels, which at a hundred and twenty a day is two hundred and seventy-two and four fifths hours against a gate of two hundred and sixty-four. A host at either size still reads a dimension settled before its weights have their labels, and the reason that costs nothing is unchanged — the stage is a reading and only a reading (`dec:health:reports-never-gates`), no path is gated on it, no output is withheld, and the sister's own convergence is reported separately and honestly. Making the gate track the deployment would mean the tracker reading a parameter count it is not given, which is a change to what the tracker is handed rather than to a figure, and it is not taken here.

**Caveat (The convergence surface a host could set changed nothing, and is gone)** · `cav:health:dead-convergence-thresholds`

The convergence threshold configuration carried twelve fields and two of them were live: the calibration movement bound and its refit count, which reach the composite through the label path. The other ten were consumed by nothing, and were validated at construction anyway — the check compared each stage's figures with the previous stage's and refused a configuration that did not increase, so a host set a surface named for exactly the stages this ladder reports, had its settings checked against each other, and changed nothing whatever. Validation is what made that costly rather than merely dead: a configuration that is checked reads as a configuration that is used.

The ten are deleted and the two live figures are all that remain. The eight stage figures went with the cross-stage check that was their only consumer, and no mapping was invented in their place. The sufficiency threshold went to the Companion record that owns it (`dec:challenge:sufficiency-floor-owned-here`) at the value it shipped with, its value question deliberately left open rather than answered in passing. That question was answered afterwards on evidence of its own, at the specification's twenty (`cav:challenge:sufficiency-threshold-open`). The minimum-samples figure was deleted outright, having named no operation anywhere in the package.

The repair first attempted was to wire the ten to their specified consumers, and it could not be executed for a reason worth keeping: **there are no specified consumers**. The specification names none of the twelve fields, carries no convergence-threshold table in its configuration chapter, and has no Warming, Converging or Converged stage anywhere — its warm-up ladder is the six stages of (`tab:warmup:stages`), which are the composite's and were already built. Wiring would therefore have meant inventing the semantics rather than following them, which is why the study that sized the question recommended deletion and why deletion is what this record carries.

The evidence base is the commissioned study, and it is package prose rather than specification: [the specification record](../docs/reports/convergence-thresholds/spec-study.md) for the line-cited comparison of ladders and fields, [the sensitivity analysis](../docs/reports/convergence-thresholds/sensitivity-analysis.md) for what the live cuts actually separate, and [the decisions](../docs/reports/convergence-thresholds/decisions.md) for the options, costs and risks the deletion was chosen among.

Two premises this caveat itself asserted were wrong, and the study found both. It described the eight stage figures as minimum cells, observations and graph importance at each of three stages, which enumerates nine; the eight are three cell minima, three observation minima and two importance minima, there being no warming importance figure. And it said no per-dimension graph importance was aggregated anywhere in the identity layer, from which it drew that six of the eight figures had nothing to compare against. Graph importance is aggregated — the graph snapshot returns whole-graph energy and the assessment path already reads it per dimension — so the figures lacking a quantity anywhere in the package were the three observation minima, and the figures lacking one on the tracker itself were five. There was no reading under which the number was six. Recording that here is the point of the correction: the count was the part of this record that made the gap look smaller and more mechanical than it was.

What this record fixes is what the shipped ladder is actually read against (`tab:health:identity-stability-cuts`), and the honest statement of that is now simpler than it was. The configuration a reader looks at first no longer describes the identity ladder at all, because it no longer pretends to. A host still cannot tune identity convergence — the figures that decide the stages are the private constants tabulated above — but that limitation is now visible as an absence rather than disguised as a surface. Making it settable is a later decision and wants a specification naming the ladder and quantities it would control, not a revival of the names deleted here.

**Decision (The alarm composite's two scales are fixed here)** · `dec:health:alarm-composite-scale`

The per-Sentinel alarm summary compresses one Sentinel's extraction into a composite by putting a peak z-score and a peak accumulator onto a common scale and taking the larger. What the composite *is* belongs to the specification (`def:runtime:alarm-summary`), which fixes that each is normalised and says nothing about the divisor; this record fixes the two divisors and nothing else about the quantity.

It is taken here on the ground that the choice is a reading choice and has no other consequence: the composite enters no model, so the scale changes what a host sees and nothing the package computes. The record is careful about which health this is — it is the inline kind and not the published kind, and the distinction is the one the remark below keeps (`rem:health:inline-versus-queried`) — but the scale of a diagnostic is a reporting judgment wherever the diagnostic travels, and there is no other record that makes reporting judgments.

The z-score half is principled. It saturates at five, which under a normal reference is a two-sided tail of about six in ten million: an observation at or beyond five standard deviations is as alarming as the composite has any need to distinguish, and a scale that kept resolving beyond it would spend its whole range on differences between extremes. (`const:assayer:alarm-z-saturation-scalar-5p0`)

The accumulator half is a shipped operating point, stated as one. It saturates at two, and what fixes it is its ratio to the z half rather than its own magnitude: an accumulator of two is two standard deviations of *accumulated* departure, which is a sustained drift, where a z of two is one unremarkable observation. The pair therefore encodes that sustained drift saturates the composite sooner than instantaneous extremity does, and that ordering is the judgment the record makes. The absolute figure is revisable; the ordering is the part a change should have to argue against. (`const:assayer:alarm-accumulator-saturation-scalar-2p0`)

**Decision (Calibration is called settled on movement and on count together)** · `dec:health:calibration-settled-cuts`

The calibration tracker reports converged only when both a movement bound and a refit count are met, and the composite calls calibration *converging* at a lower count on its own. Three figures fix that ladder: calibration counts as settled when the last refit moved the calibration parameter by no more than one hundredth, and when five refits have completed; and two completed refits are enough for the composite to report the stage below.

Requiring both is the decision, and the reason is that either alone is satisfiable by an accident. A single refit can move the parameter by less than a hundredth because the data it saw was thin rather than because the fit had settled, and a count of refits says only that the cadence has run, not that it converged on anything. Together they say that the fit has been exercised repeatedly and has stopped moving, which is what settled means.

The movement bound is a tenth of the bound that resets the drift accumulator, which is the relation worth stating: the figure at which the calibration is called settled is deliberately an order tighter than the figure at which it is called disturbed, so there is a wide band in which the calibration is neither. The two counts — five and two — are shipped operating points, and their relation is again the substantive half: the lower one exists so that a host watching the ladder sees the stage move before the fit has finished, rather than waiting at the previous stage through every refit and then jumping.

**Remark (What fifty samples buys an axis correlation)** · `rem:health:axis-correlation-evidence`

The per-axis correlation between stored predictions and reported values (`def:monitoring:axis-correlation`) is reported only once an axis has fifty samples. The specification fixes that a minimum stands and not what it is, and fifty is a shipped operating point set above the twenty the discrimination metrics require of each class (`tab:config:monitoring`) on the ground that a correlation asks more of its evidence than a two-class rank comparison does.

What fifty actually buys is worth stating plainly, because it is less than a reader might assume. The sampling standard error of a correlation near zero at fifty samples is about fifteen hundredths, so a reported figure at this gate resolves the sign and the rough size of the association and not its magnitude. A host reading the correlation as a measurement rather than as an indication wants considerably more than the gate demands, and the gate is set where it is so that the diagnostic appears early rather than so that it is precise when it appears.

**Caveat (A convergence event that cannot fire)** · `cav:health:tracker-event-defect`

The composite stage-change event can never be emitted. Its emitter compares an old stage against a new one and sends only when they differ (`src/health/events.rs`), but nothing on any runtime path calls it: publication computes the new stage alone, with no earlier value to compare against (`src/owner/label_path.rs`), and every call to the emitter is in a test.

It is carried here rather than concealed because the legacy record that owned it asserted the opposite in an event table and in a guarantee row, which is how a defect becomes documented as a feature (`obs:assayer:adr-visibility-drift`). This is the census's one finding that is a live code defect rather than a drift in prose, and the mechanism above is what a recomputation against the present code finds; the census described the same dead event by a different route. Repairing it is neither this record's business nor this campaign's. The record's business is that the next reader is not told the event works.

Repaired 2026-08-21 by the conformance repair campaign's ninth wave (`entry:assayer:wl-health-stage-event-dead`): publication reads the previous stage off the summary it is about to replace and hands the pair to the emitter, so the event now fires on genuine transitions from the one production site that holds both halves. The mechanism paragraph above is kept as the record of why it could not fire, and of how a thoroughly tested emitter stayed dead — covered at six call sites, every one of them a test.

**Remark (Inline health and queried health are distinct things)** · `rem:health:inline-versus-queried`

The compact summary riding on every assessment result and the state this record's swap publishes are different objects answering different questions. The first says what *this result* cost; the second says what the *package's condition* is. The surface record owns the inline one (`dec:surface:inline-health`).

They are named together here because the resemblance is the trap: both are called health, both carry degradation, and a reader who assumes one is a prefix of the other will look for per-result detail in a published summary that was never about a result. Keeping the distinction explicit is cheaper than the alternative, which is two records each describing a superset of the other.
