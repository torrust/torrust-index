# Degradation · `rec:degradation:infallible-failure-posture`

This record decides one posture and states its faces at the other layers, so that no other record has to argue for infallibility from first principles. It realises the failure-posture choice (`dec:structural:failure-posture`).

It is a short record on purpose. Four decisions and seven environments that follow from them are the whole of it; the length that would come from restating the posture at each site it governs is exactly what the citations below exist to prevent.

**Decision (Core assessment does not fail)** · `dec:degradation:infallible-core`

There is no error channel on the assessment call. The call returns an assessment, always — not a result that may be an error, and not an assessment carrying a sentinel value meaning failure. Anomalies are reported in band, travelling with the result in the structures the specification defines (`schema:output:assessment`).

The decision is about what the host can do, not about what can go wrong. Things go wrong; the question is whether the host is handed a numeric pathology it has no means to act on, and the answer is no.

**Decision (Matrix pathologies retain and flag)** · `dec:degradation:retain-and-flag`

When a numerical operation cannot be completed as intended, the affected state is left at the best available value and flagged, rather than the call being failed. The flag travels with the result, so a consumer that cares can see that the quantity it is reading was produced under degradation, and a consumer that does not care is not forced to handle an error to obtain a number it would have used anyway. The posture is the in-flight one and it stops at the persistence boundary: a refusal met while restoring a model from a persisted artefact is a structural violation by whoever wrote the artefact rather than a pathology arising in flight, and there is no last state known good to retain there, because the state a restore would fall back on is the one the restore was reached for in order to replace (`rem:degradation:numerics-face`).

Retaining is not the same as ignoring. The state is not silently wrong: it is explicitly the last state known good, and it is explicitly marked as such.

**Decision (Structural violations are hard; data-quality problems degrade)** · `dec:degradation:error-partition`

Where a call can fail at all, the partition is fixed and total. A structural contract violation — a caller supplying something the contract does not admit — is a hard error, because it is the caller's defect and the caller can fix it. A data-quality problem is not: it degrades the affected cell, reports the degradation, and lets everything else proceed.

The line is drawn by who can act. A host given a hard error for bad data would have to choose between discarding a whole batch and suppressing the error, and neither is what it wants. A host given a degraded cell for its own contract violation would never learn it had one.

**Decision (A guard's magnitude is fixed by the arithmetic of its own site)** · `dec:degradation:guard-magnitudes`

An infallible core (`dec:degradation:infallible-core`) has to return a number from arithmetic that has been handed one it cannot use: a probability sitting on a pole of the logit, an uncertainty that has decayed to nothing under a division, a squared distance so large that the kernel's denominator has stopped carrying the information the quotient would need. At each such site the input is clamped, or the output floored, before the operation — never tested for pathology after it.

Guarding before is what makes the guarantee cheap. A test afterwards has to recognise a non-finite value and decide what to put in its place, which is the same judgment made later and with less to make it from; a guard makes the pathological input unreachable, so the operation has one branch and its result is finite by construction. It is (`dec:degradation:retain-and-flag`) at a scale too small to flag, and it is the reason the flag is not raised: nothing was lost that a consumer could have acted on, because an input displaced by its guard carried no information at the resolution the guard sits at.

The magnitudes do not share a value and must not. Some are fixed by where double precision stops representing a distinction and some by where a statistic stops meaning anything, and one shared epsilon would be wrong at every site but the one it was chosen for. The table below fixes each, which departs from this corpus's general posture that a figure belongs in the code and a record names only where it lives (`obs:assayer:adr-visibility-drift`). The departure is deliberate and narrow. That posture forbids restating a figure whose authority is somewhere else, and these figures have no authority anywhere else: no specification fixes them, no configuration surface exposes them, and until this record they were choices carrying no stated reason at all. Here the record is the authority, so stating the figure is the original and not a second copy of it.

**Table (The guards, and the arithmetic that fixes each magnitude)** · `tab:degradation:guard-magnitudes`

Eight guards, by site, with the magnitude each stands at and the argument that puts it there. The posterior record's own table names four guards by site and purpose and transcribes no value at all (`tab:posterior:guards`); the leverage floor is the row the two share, and this table is where its magnitude is decided.

| Guard | Site | Magnitude | What fixes it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Probability pole clearance | ``src/resonance/derivation.rs`` | one in a million | Semantic, not numerical. Double precision would carry a probability far nearer the pole, so the clearance is a statement about evidence rather than about arithmetic: it bounds the logit the derivation will transform at just under fourteen in magnitude, and a calibrated probability resolved finer than one in a million is a fit reporting a confidence its evidence does not hold (`rem:warmup:pre-calibration`). (`const:assayer:probability-pole-clearance-scalar-1en6`) |
| Least admissible uncertainty | ``src/resonance/derivation.rs`` | one in ten thousand million | The classification bandwidth is a reciprocal of this quantity, so what the guard really fixes is the largest bandwidth the derivation will produce, and it is a semantic bound rather than a representation limit. It stands ten decimal orders below unit uncertainty, which is far outside any posture the model occupies: it bites only on an uncertainty an upstream defect has already reduced to zero, and it converts that defect into a very sharp tag instead of a division by nothing. (`const:assayer:least-credible-uncertainty-scalar-1en10`) |
| Kernel saturation cutoff | ``src/resonance/tags.rs`` | ten to the fifteenth | A representation limit, with one decimal order of headroom. The kernel divides by one plus the squared scaled distance (`eq:rendering:kernel`), and beyond two to the fifty-third — about nine times this cutoff — the unit addend has no representation in the sum at all. Cutting an order early keeps the guard on the side where the denominator is still exact. (`const:assayer:kernel-saturation-cutoff-scalar-1e15`) |
| Tag presence floor | ``src/resonance/tags.rs`` | ten to the minus three hundredth | Positive, because every tag carries positive probability at every posture (`prop:rendering:completeness`) and the field is normalised by dividing through the sum, so an exact zero makes a tag's probability undefined rather than merely small. Normal, because the value is arithmetic on: it stands about seven and a half decimal orders above the smallest normal double, which is the headroom a subsequent multiplication or division needs before it underflows to the zero the floor exists to avoid. (`const:assayer:tag-presence-floor-scalar-1en300`) |
| Blend variance guard | ``src/risk/blend.rs`` | one in a million million | Bounded by its effect on the weight rather than by representation. The guard is added to a variance the weight then divides by, so its whole influence is its ratio to that variance. At prior-level uncertainty — the variance the prior precision inverts to, which is ten (`tab:config:risk-model`) — that ratio is one part in ten million million, and it would take a variance of one in a thousand million before the ratio reached even one part in a thousand. No state between those two moves a weight a reader could observe. (`const:assayer:blend-weight-guard-scalar-1en12`) |
| Leverage division guard | ``src/model/update.rs`` | one in a hundred million | A shipped operating point, and the one guard here whose magnitude no argument pins to a particular decade. Its duty is to sit below every leverage carrying information and above the point the quotient stops being finite, and the range that leaves is wide: a unit-norm direction at prior-level uncertainty carries a leverage of about ten, so this guard is nine decimal orders below the informative scale and about three hundred orders above where the division fails. It is revisable anywhere in that band. (`const:assayer:leverage-division-guard-scalar-1en8`) |
| Rank tie tolerance | ``src/health/published.rs`` | one in a million million | The width of the band two calibrated scores are called equal within. Bare machine epsilon is too tight: the calibrated score is compared after intermediate arithmetic, so genuine ties separate by a few units in the last place, which at the top of the score's range is about four in ten thousand million million. This tolerance stands about three and a third decimal orders above that and far below any score difference the ranking is meant to resolve. (`const:assayer:rank-tie-tolerance-scalar-1en12`) |
| Recalibrated threshold floor | ``src/health/concordance.rs`` | one in a hundred | Semantic and not numerical, and the one row here that guards a statistic rather than an arithmetic. Each axis takes a percentile of its own window (`alg:feature:concordance-recalibration`), so a window whose scores all sit near zero recalibrates to a threshold near zero, and a threshold near zero is exceeded by any positive signal — which is calibration producing a detector that fires on everything. The floor is the smallest threshold that still discriminates, and it binds only while the window is degenerate. (`const:assayer:concordance-threshold-floor-scalar-0p01`) |

**Caveat (This table is authored and is not checked against the code)** · `cav:degradation:guard-transcription`

Nothing verifies that the magnitudes above are the magnitudes the code holds. The constant profile pins each of these values at its declaration and regenerates the pin when the value moves, so the code's own half is machine-checked; the table above now cites each pin, so a value change re-mints its pin, the citation dangles, and the check fails on it — what remains discipline is not the drift's detection but the reviewer's semantic re-approval of the changed value. A reader who needs to know what the code does should read the code, and this table is the argument for what it should do.

The exposure is stated rather than repaired because repairing it belongs to the profile and not to this record. It is the price of the departure the decision above makes, and it is a smaller price than the alternative it was weighed against, which is a figure standing in the source with no reason attached to it anywhere.

**Corollary (Degradation and health travel with the result)** · `cor:degradation:in-band-travel`

Because degradation and health travel inline on every result (`schema:output:health-snapshot`), the absent error channel of (`dec:degradation:infallible-core`) costs a consumer no information. Everything an error channel would have carried is present; what changes is that it arrives as data on a successful return rather than as a control-flow event. That is what makes infallibility affordable rather than merely convenient, and it is why the two decisions must be read together: either alone would be a worse design than the pair.

**Discussion (The rejected fallible assessment)** · `disc:degradation:fallible-alternative`

The alternative was a fallible assessment returning an error on numeric pathology. It is the conventional shape and it was rejected on what the host could do with it. A host receiving an error from an ill-conditioned posterior has no repair available: it cannot refactorise the matrix, cannot choose a different regularisation, and cannot decide that this cell should be trusted less. The only behaviours left to it are to retry, which will fail identically, and to log, which the in-band flag already achieves without discarding the assessment. The error would move the anomaly to a place with less capacity to handle it.

**Remark (The numerics face of this posture, and where it stops)** · `rem:degradation:numerics-face`

The refused factorisation on the posterior maintenance path is this posture at the numerics layer, not a second decision. It ends by retaining the maintained covariance and flagging it, which is (`dec:degradation:retain-and-flag`) applied to a factorisation. What it no longer does is repair: the phase that answered a refusal with the inverse of a shifted matrix is retired, and retaining and flagging is the whole of what survives it (`dec:posterior:repair-cascade`). Reading this as an independent choice is what previously let the corpus carry the same posture in several places with drifted wording; the record that owns the maintenance path decides how a refusal is reported, and inherits from here what happens when the state cannot be recomputed.

The posture stops at the persistence boundary. A factorisation refused while restoring a model from a persisted artefact is not a numerical pathology arising in flight but a structural contract violation by whoever wrote the artefact, and the partition sends that to a hard error rather than to a flag (`dec:degradation:error-partition`). The two dispositions are the partition doing its work rather than an inconsistency, on the same reasoning that sends observation-queue overflow to a degradation and label-queue overflow to an error (`rem:degradation:queue-face`).

Retaining and flagging is not available at that boundary, and that is what makes the line a boundary rather than a preference. What the maintenance path retains is a covariance the model built from valid input and can go on using; what a refused restore would retain is the state the restore was reached for in order to replace. Where the caller is the engine itself — the numeric checkpoint reverting a label it has judged corrupt — there is nothing left to retain at all, and the disposition is to stop rather than to flag and continue (`dec:surface:async-label`).

**Remark (The queue face of this posture)** · `rem:degradation:queue-face`

Observation-queue overflow degrades on the same reasoning. A full observation queue is a load condition rather than a contract violation, so by (`dec:degradation:error-partition`) it is not a hard error on the observing path. The label queue is the deliberate exception and is decided elsewhere: overflow there returns an error because a dropped label is a submitter's loss and the submitter can act on it (`dec:concurrency:no-silent-drop`). The two queues differ in who is owed the news, which is the partition doing its work rather than an inconsistency.

**Caveat (The checkpoint placement is definition, not decision)** · `cav:degradation:checkpoint-placement`

The corpus carries a placement of numeric checkpoints from the boundary inward. It is not a decision this record makes: it defines where the system takes its readings rather than choosing a posture about them, and the census marked it for absorption into the specification on that ground (`tab:assayer:adr-disposition-structural`). It is named here so that a reader looking for it in the degradation record learns where it went, and it is not restated here in any part.
