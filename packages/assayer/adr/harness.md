# Harness · `rec:harness:shared-testing-contract`

This record fixes the shared testing contract for Assayer: one vocabulary for constructing a subject, controlling it, observing it and deciding when its asynchronous work is complete. The contract binds the crate test tree, the integration-test binaries and the inline unit-test modules — all three caller surfaces (`obs:assayer:testing-architecture-three-surfaces`) reached by the testing architecture (`rep:assayer:testing-architecture`) — and the Criterion target that consumes the same scenario from outside them (`dec:harness:performance-benchmarks`).

The tests exercise the assessment interface, label pipeline and concurrency model without redefining them (`chap:spec:assessment-interface`) (`chap:spec:label-pipeline`) (`chap:spec:concurrency`). The structural and public-contract layers remain the sources of product behaviour (`spec:structural:architectural-commitments`) (`spec:contracts:public-contracts`), and the concurrency and surface records remain its decision owners (`rec:concurrency:snapshot-stewardship`) (`rec:surface:host-facing-contracts`).

This record decides about tests and changes no specification behaviour. It is the testing-only exception admitted on the ground that dividing a common contract among behaviour records would copy or mis-home it (`dec:assayer:record-harness-record`). Its choices form the standing discipline (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-ideas`); implementation order remains planning (`sec:assayer:testing-plan-roadmap`), and authored test claims remain governed by the test documentation policy (`dec:assayer:test-documentation-policy`). The campaign that executed these decisions is a separate plan, and its finished rosters are the inventory of what the contract now has in each tree (`plan:assayer:harness-implementation`) (`tab:assayer:harness-implementation-library-roster`) (`tab:assayer:harness-implementation-support-roster`).

**Decision (One scenario owns the common vocabulary)** · `dec:harness:single-scenario`

One Assayer-owning scenario type carries the common instance, names, time and barriers: `World` owns the real engine, the clock it shares with that engine, the seeded generator, the name registry and the tolerance bundle, and `Scenario` is that world bound to a tracing span it dereferences through. The single steward remains the only owner of the working copy (`dec:concurrency:single-steward`) under the layer's one-writer choice (`dec:structural:write-serialisation`) and the specification's concurrency contract (`chap:spec:concurrency`); the scenario coordinates access to that subject and does not become another owner.

The rejected alternative is a parallel scenario model for each caller surface. The existing scenario and multi-channel arrangements show why that loses (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-scenario`) (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-multi-channel`): a second model carries a second vocabulary for shared queues, while the common choice exists precisely to make those claims singular (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-one-scenario`).

The landed skeleton supplied the common starting point (`entry:assayer:harness-stage-skeleton`), and the publication-barrier residue showed the cost of leaving one model outside it (`entry:assayer:harness-stage-clock-residual-race`). The migration those readings established has since run: fixture-side construction in crate, integration, inline and benchmark coverage enters through that one type, and the parallel configuration, entity, report and wait vocabularies are retired (`entry:assayer:harness-scenario-unification`) (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-record-audit-architecture-gap`). Convergence is therefore descriptive as well as contractual, on the terms the adoption caveat states (`cav:harness:incremental-adoption`).

**Decision (Tests use declared barriers and author no waits)** · `dec:harness:no-ad-hoc-waits`

A test waits only by calling the declared barrier for the queue whose completion it needs, and that set is closed: six barriers stand, one for every asynchronous queue a test can reach, each documenting what its return covers and what it excludes (`entry:assayer:harness-closed-barriers`). The label queue and deferred identity drain retain their specified meanings (`req:publication:label-queue`) (`alg:publication:identity-draining`); the harness names how a test observes their completion under the same single-writer discipline (`dec:structural:write-serialisation`) (`dec:concurrency:single-steward`).

The rejected alternative is a test-authored sleep, poll, deadline or compound wait. It loses because elapsed time is not evidence that a queue drained, and a poll can turn a stopped worker into a stale-value assertion. The liveness architecture gives the failure a named home (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-liveness`), while lifecycle operations that require two-phase visibility retain that existing order (`dec:construction:two-phase-visibility`).

The barrier rule is the closed-set choice argued for the scenario (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-time-and-barriers`). The earlier residual-race evidence is decisive about the diagnostic difference: a missing publication barrier is reported as missing completion only when the harness owns the wait (`entry:assayer:harness-stage-clock-residual-race`). The authored waits have since retired behind those barriers, each removed site naming its replacement, and what the census left standing is six raw sites that are stimulus or measurement rather than completion waits, each beside its own ruling — a monotonic expiry horizon a test measures, a paced fixed workload, yields that widen an interleaving — so the rule binds what it always bound and now describes the tree (`entry:assayer:harness-polling-retirement`).

**Decision (Long stimuli are declarative playback)** · `dec:harness:declarative-playback`

A long stimulus is a sequence of typed rows interpreted by one runner. The row type belongs to the test subject and reaches the runner as a `PlaybackRow`; the runner owns barrier policy, checkpoint callbacks, bounded batching and progress reporting, which stand as `PlaybackBarrierPolicy`, `PlaybackCheckpoint`, `PlaybackBatchSize` and `PlaybackProgress` around one `playback` entry point (`entry:assayer:harness-tape-runner`). Assessment, label and report-reception meanings remain the specification's (`req:runtime:assessment-interface`) (`req:runtime:label-interface`) (`alg:runtime:report-reception`), and playback merely drives the assessment and learning paths (`dec:operational:assessment-path`) (`dec:operational:learning-path`).

The rejected alternative is a handwritten stimulus loop per test. It loses because each loop would separately decide when state is settled, where a failing row is named and whether a checkpoint reads completed work. A playback batch inherits the existing cost boundary without gaining a shared view: timestamps remain batch-wide and published snapshots remain per-request (`dec:ordering:batch-timestamp`) (`dec:concurrency:per-request-load`).

The common scenario is the execution home (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-scenario`), and the playback contract is the shared choice (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-tapes`). The runner waited on measured cost rather than on argument, and that measurement landed: the recorded enrichment-grid distribution and its rerun rule released it (`entry:assayer:process-grid-runtime`) (`entry:assayer:harness-tape-runner`), and the staged umbrella that once held this work owns none of it now (`entry:assayer:harness-stage-tapes`).

**Decision (State readers obey one probe contract)** · `dec:harness:probe-contract`

A permitted test-support probe crosses the relevant barrier, performs one published load, returns owned copies, exposes no mutation and reads time only from the scenario. Three projections stand under those clauses — `PublishedModelBlock`, `PublishedSlotMoments` and `PendingEntryView` — and the first two cross the label-publication barrier before taking exactly one published load. The third departs from that wording: pending insertion completes on the assessing thread, so it names its lack of an asynchronous dependency and takes one non-consuming lookup instead of a barrier and a published load (`entry:assayer:harness-probe-contract`). The clause binds the version boundary a reading depends on, and where a reading depends on none there is no wait that would settle anything. Those clauses preserve the definition of a published snapshot and the structural-exactness guarantee (`def:publication:model-snapshot`) (`inv:guarantee:structural-exactness`) under the representation choices for published and in-flight state (`dec:representation:published-composition`) (`dec:representation:pending-retention`).

The rejected alternatives are local state readers with unstated rules and a wider production surface. The first loses because repeated readers can mix versions or retain borrows; the second loses because guidance is intentionally opaque and precision is intentionally excluded (`dec:surface:guidance-opacity`) (`dec:retention:precision-excluded`). A test need does not create a host promise.

The testing gate already separates the reachable support surface (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-reach`). The contract formalises that boundary (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-probes`), including the hard consequence that a witness of excluded state needs a direct crate-level home rather than a nonconforming probe (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-probe-versus-retention`) (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-record-audit-probe-universal-conflict`). Owned readings are now the largest single group of surfaces the intent plans still ask this harness for, and every one of them lands inside that limit or does not land here at all (`obs:assayer:testing-architecture-intent-demand`).

**Decision (Shared oracles declare an independent route)** · `dec:harness:oracle-tier`

A small shared oracle tier recomputes published quantities by declared routes and compares them under named budgets. Each oracle declares whether it implements a specification formula, an independent derivation or a reference chosen by the harness; that declaration stands as an `OracleProvenance` constant beside its route, and the tolerance register supplies the corresponding discipline for the budgets the comparison is made under (`tab:assayer:harness-scenario-tolerances`). Four routes stand — `pairwise_rank`, `regularised_schur_complement`, `decay_recurrence` and `dimension_width` — each declaring a specification formula and each deliberately written against the production route it checks, so the three-valued vocabulary is exercised at its strictest value alone (`entry:assayer:harness-oracle-tier`).

The rejected alternative is repeated local recomputation, including an expected value obtained through the production route it purports to check. It loses because duplication multiplies interpretations while route reuse checks only self-consistency. The per-observation and precision invariants state what the comparisons must witness (`inv:guarantee:per-observation-exactness`) (`inv:guarantee:precision`), the numerics layer owns their mathematics (`spec:numerics:model-mathematics`), and the retained debug oracle is the narrow precedent (`dec:posterior:debug-oracle`).

The existing assertion tier remains a reader and comparator rather than being renamed an oracle (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-assertions`). The shared tier is the distinct second-computation choice (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-oracles`), bounded by the fact that independence mitigates but cannot eliminate oracle error (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-oracle-trust`).

**Decision (Fixtures establish and report their preconditions)** · `dec:harness:guarded-fixtures`

A state-establishing fixture returns only after checking its named precondition, and a failure reports the measured state that failed the check. This makes non-vacuity the setup's obligation before a result assertion applies. The evidence-authority invariant remains the source of what assessment may establish (`inv:guarantee:evidence-authority`), while convergence remains diagnostic and never a runtime gate (`dec:operational:convergence-diagnostic`) (`dec:health:reports-never-gates`).

The rejected alternative is unchecked setup followed by an assertion that assumes training, separation or activation occurred. It loses because a vacuous pass is indistinguishable from a working fixture. The current assertion architecture provides the reporting shape (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-assertions`), and the guarded-fixture choice makes it general (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-guarded-fixtures`).

Measured baselines are preferred to hoped-for thresholds, as the identity-separation reframing demonstrates (`obs:assayer:reframe-identity-threshold`). A guard may use independent arithmetic, but it does not thereby become the result oracle (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-guard-and-oracle`). The guarded population is at a fixpoint and was settled item by item: the construction guard covers the builder that carries an expected layout, the cold world and the three scenario constructors, the shared trained state carries its own measured baseline, and the specialised seam guards its dimension setup and both harness spawns, while command primitives, the learning cycle verbs and the pure payload builders stay out because they establish no reusable post-state for a second guard to measure (`entry:assayer:harness-guarded-fixtures`).

**Decision (Fixture guards and result oracles stay separate)** · `dec:harness:separate-validation`

Fixture guards validate that setup produced the state a test requires; result oracles validate what the system did from that state. The distinction preserves evidence authority (`inv:guarantee:evidence-authority`) and the rule that convergence reports rather than controls behaviour (`dec:operational:convergence-diagnostic`) (`dec:health:reports-never-gates`).

The rejected alternative is one mechanism that reports every disagreement as setup failure. It loses because the remedies differ: a failed guard invalidates the fixture, while an oracle disagreement challenges the result. The assertion architecture can report both without conflating them (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-assertions`), and the proposed fusion is rejected explicitly on that diagnostic ground (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-guard-and-oracle`).

Guarded fixtures remain the default setup shape (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-guarded-fixtures`) and now measure what they build (`entry:assayer:harness-guarded-fixtures`), including comparisons against measured baselines rather than a threshold the setup may never have reached (`obs:assayer:reframe-identity-threshold`).

**Decision (Shared scenarios exercise the real engine)** · `dec:harness:real-engine`

Shared scenarios exercise a real Assayer instance through its assessment contract (`chap:spec:assessment-interface`) (`dec:contracts:assessment-surface`) and the infallible batch surface that real hosts receive (`dec:surface:batch-infallible`). The numerical paths remain subject to the precision guarantee and repair discipline (`inv:guarantee:precision`) (`dec:numerics:repair-cascade`), with an independent debug oracle where one already exists (`dec:posterior:debug-oracle`).

The rejected alternative is an engine mock or double. It loses because queue ordering, publication, decay and numerical repair are the behaviours the shared scenario exists to witness; substituting their implementation would make a passing test evidence about the substitute. The no-double choice is part of the rejected-alternatives argument (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-rejected`).

Specialised maintenance and multi-channel arrangements may drive the real subject through narrower seams (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-side-harnesses`) (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-multi-channel`). The seeded invariant packs broaden inputs through those same surfaces (`entry:assayer:harness-property-packs`), and neither they nor the narrower seams change what executes.

**Decision (Specialised side harnesses keep only genuine specialisation)** · `dec:harness:specialised-side-harnesses`

Maintenance and multi-channel harnesses remain local where their subjects, row shapes or paired-world ceremony are genuinely specialised (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-side-harnesses`) (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-multi-channel`). They reuse shared engine logic and barrier meanings, so specialisation does not become a second contract: the maintenance seam adopted the common identity-maintenance barrier in place of its sleeping flush, the support tree dropped its scenario-builder extension for the shared builder, and its longest label history is subject-owned rows driven by the shared runner (`entry:assayer:harness-scenario-unification`) (`tab:assayer:harness-implementation-support-roster`).

The rejected alternatives are absorbing every side harness into the common scenario and leaving each one wholly independent. Absorption loses because it would make single-caller machinery universal; independence loses because it duplicates the ordering claims shared with the real engine. The explicit boundary is the selected alternative (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-rejected`).

Both sides still exercise the real assessment surface and its precision discipline (`chap:spec:assessment-interface`) (`dec:contracts:assessment-surface`) (`dec:surface:batch-infallible`) (`inv:guarantee:precision`) (`dec:numerics:repair-cascade`). The debug-oracle precedent and the landed seeded packs remain available without moving specialised ceremony into the shared tier (`dec:posterior:debug-oracle`) (`entry:assayer:harness-property-packs`). One step is written on both sides — the challenge-routing step the paired-world verb cannot delegate to either single-world verb — and that duplication follows from the paired shape rather than from an unclaimed seam (`obs:assayer:testing-architecture-routing-duplication`).

**Decision (Invariant sweeps are seeded and framework-free)** · `dec:harness:seeded-sweeps`

Invariant packs use declared reproducible seeds and print the seed on failure; `run_seeded_sweep` is the one envelope that does it, reporting the declared seed, the zero-based case index, the complete drawn input and the witness's own reason (`entry:assayer:harness-property-packs`). They drive the real assessment surface (`chap:spec:assessment-interface`) (`dec:contracts:assessment-surface`) (`dec:surface:batch-infallible`) and test precision and repair through their existing contracts (`inv:guarantee:precision`) (`dec:numerics:repair-cascade`) (`dec:posterior:debug-oracle`).

The rejected alternative is adding a property-testing framework. It loses because the harness already has deterministic generation, while shrinking offers little for failures whose cause is queue order, publication or a numerical path rather than a minimal input. The framework rejection is explicit (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-rejected`), and the packs that landed under it added no dependency: twenty-nine declared-seed packs witness every claim the census found sweep-shaped, while the claims it ruled out say why they are not per-draw predicates (`entry:assayer:harness-property-packs`).

The maintenance and multi-channel subjects retain their specialised drivers (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-side-harnesses`) (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-multi-channel`); seeded sweeps change input coverage and do not absorb those harnesses.

**Caveat (The contract removes harness duplication, not product blockers)** · `cav:harness:limited-unblocking`

The contract can remove duplicate fixtures, readers and waits, and it did; it cannot settle missing production behaviour or a specification choice, and the finished harness settled neither (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-not-the-critical-path`) (`obs:assayer:harness-implementation-limited-unblocking`). The demand census measures what that leaves: of the hundred and fourteen things the intent plans say their witnesses still lack, eight name production code or a ruling the corpus has not made, twenty-seven name a surface this harness does not have, and the remaining seventy-nine are test-local and ask this contract for nothing (`obs:assayer:testing-architecture-intent-demand`). The one specification ruling still open belongs to the promise it would rewrite rather than to any harness mechanism (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-record-audit-six-rulings`).

The callerless helpers a common contract made retirable have since retired, by a caller census rather than by the compiler diagnostic a public test-support surface cannot give (`obs:assayer:testing-architecture-unreported-decay`) (`entry:assayer:harness-stale-helper-retirement`); the specialised support tree stayed, because it is retained by decision rather than awaiting retirement (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-multi-channel`) (`dec:harness:specialised-side-harnesses`). Enabling a retirement is the whole of what a decision record does for one, and the concurrency specification, one-writer layer choice and steward decision remain unchanged (`chap:spec:concurrency`) (`dec:structural:write-serialisation`) (`dec:concurrency:single-steward`).

**Caveat (Playback progress is liveness, not latency)** · `cav:harness:progress-not-latency`

A playback progress counter identifies the row before which execution stopped; it establishes no elapsed-time promise. `PlaybackProgress` is that counter: it advances once at each completed row boundary and reports the first incomplete row, without reading a clock (`entry:assayer:harness-tape-runner`). The existing deadlines already make that distinction (`obs:assayer:testing-architecture-deadlines-are-liveness`), and the same limit binds playback (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-progress-not-latency`). A latency claim requires its own measurement and budget.

This limit applies while playback drives assessment, labels and report reception (`req:runtime:assessment-interface`) (`req:runtime:label-interface`) (`alg:runtime:report-reception`) through the assessment and learning paths (`dec:operational:assessment-path`) (`dec:operational:learning-path`). Batch timestamp and per-request publication semantics remain independent of progress (`dec:ordering:batch-timestamp`) (`dec:concurrency:per-request-load`). The scenario supplies execution and the shared runner the sequencing (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-scenario`) (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-tapes`) (`entry:assayer:harness-tape-runner`), while the one maintained runtime figure is an informational budget recorded beside its suite that no test, gate, assertion or deadline reads (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-multi-channel-runtime-budget`).

**Caveat (Oracle independence mitigates but does not prove trust)** · `cav:harness:oracle-trust`

An independent route and a fails-before arm reduce the chance that an oracle repeats the production defect; neither proves the oracle correct (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-oracle-trust`). The tier remains deliberately small and read by several tests (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-oracles`), with provenance and budgets declared beside the existing tolerance discipline (`tab:assayer:harness-scenario-tolerances`); four routes stand, each separated from a deliberately defective arm, and no intent plan asks for a fifth (`entry:assayer:harness-oracle-tier`) (`obs:assayer:testing-architecture-intent-demand`).

The oracle compares against the per-observation and precision guarantees (`inv:guarantee:per-observation-exactness`) (`inv:guarantee:precision`) using mathematics owned by the numerics layer (`spec:numerics:model-mathematics`). The retained debug oracle is precedent, not proof (`dec:posterior:debug-oracle`), and the assertion architecture remains the comparison surface (`sec:assayer:testing-architecture-assertions`).

**Decision (The convergence subject splits by topic)** · `dec:harness:convergence-split`

The convergence subject is partitioned before the concentrated witnesses land into four integration-test binaries: `learning_convergence` for learning dynamics under labels, `lifecycle_identity_convergence` for lifecycle and identity, `posterior_convergence` for posterior geometry and conditioning, and `calibration_discrimination_convergence` for calibration and discrimination. A witness lands in the binary whose subject matches the area named by its promise; the standing convergence witness remains in `learning_convergence`. The concentration observation and integration-slice history supply the placement evidence (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-destination-concentration`) (`obs:assayer:harness-integration-slices`). All four targets now exist, the three new ones carrying honest empty indexes until their assigned witnesses land (`entry:assayer:harness-convergence-split`).

`learning_convergence` receives (`plan:assayer:intent-a-stream-with-no-adverse-outcome-converges-to-near-zero-risk`), (`plan:assayer:intent-the-leverage-bound-fires-at-cold-start-and-rarely-after`), (`plan:assayer:intent-the-importance-ceiling-and-not-the-data-sets-gradient-balance`), (`plan:assayer:intent-the-anchor-catches-a-regime-change-first`), and (`plan:assayer:intent-restriction-opens-a-gap-between-operational-and-sister-estimates`). Each promise makes a labelled trajectory, its update bounds, or its response to changed evidence the principal subject.

`lifecycle_identity_convergence` receives (`plan:assayer:intent-a-registration-lifts-the-blend-weight-and-labels-lower-it`), (`plan:assayer:intent-a-mis-standardised-arrival-bootstraps-back-into-scale`), (`plan:assayer:intent-marginalising-a-sentinel-leaves-its-information-behind`), (`plan:assayer:intent-retiring-an-uninformative-sentinel-costs-no-discrimination`), and (`plan:assayer:intent-entities-sharing-a-cell-are-separated-by-identity-alone`). Each promise makes admission, removal, retained information, or entity identity the boundary whose effect must converge.

`posterior_convergence` receives (`plan:assayer:intent-sentinel-specific-uncertainty-does-not-wake-the-anchor`), (`plan:assayer:intent-sparsely-reporting-sentinels-ill-condition-the-precision-matrix`), and (`plan:assayer:intent-cross-sentinel-correlation-is-caught-only-once-interactions-converge`). Each promise is decided by posterior projection, spectrum, covariance, or interaction geometry rather than by calibration or lifecycle state.

`calibration_discrimination_convergence` receives (`plan:assayer:intent-one-corrupted-label-in-two-hundred-costs-almost-nothing`), (`plan:assayer:intent-recent-discrimination-parts-from-the-lifetime-figure-under-drift`), (`plan:assayer:intent-a-systematically-mislabelled-entity-parts-from-the-aggregate-within-ten-labels`), and (`plan:assayer:intent-a-tenfold-base-rate-shift-trips-the-cumulative-sum-and-resets-calibration`). Each promise reads rank discrimination, concordance, drift, or calibration as its terminal result.

Retaining one binary is rejected. It puts seventeen long-running tests behind one runtime and review door even though their promise areas share the scenario contract rather than one subject; the existing concentration is the condition to remove, not a claim-cohesion benefit to preserve (`obs:assayer:testing-harness-concept-destination-concentration`).

Deferring the split until a runtime budget exists is also rejected. A runtime budget measures execution cost after ownership is chosen and cannot choose that ownership; waiting would make the first concentrated witnesses pay for a layout whose subject boundaries are already stated by their promises. Playback was separately gated by its own cost evidence, and that evidence has since released it (`entry:assayer:process-grid-runtime`) (`entry:assayer:harness-tape-runner`).

Each topic binary may rely on the shared scenario and its declared barriers under the single-scenario decision (`dec:harness:single-scenario`). No topic binary carries fixture code of its own: common fixture behaviour stays with the shared scenario, while genuinely specialised ceremony remains within the side-harness boundary (`dec:harness:specialised-side-harnesses`).

**Decision (Performance evidence uses the shared scenario)** · `dec:harness:performance-benchmarks`

Maintained performance evidence is Criterion benchmarks that consume the shared scenario, its guarded fixtures and the real engine (`dec:harness:single-scenario`) (`dec:harness:guarded-fixtures`) (`dec:harness:real-engine`). The benchmark groups follow the specification's assessment, label, construction, pre-seed, health and marginalisation cost subjects (`chap:spec:convergence-and-resources`), while the performance plan fixes their cases, measurement contract and implementation partition (`plan:assayer:performance-harness`). One Criterion target carries those six groups over six declared real-engine cases whose dimensions, populations, completion and health are guarded before a measurement can begin, and the scheduled route runs it on a recorded context without deriving a verdict from a wall-clock floor (`entry:assayer:performance-harness-fixtures`) (`entry:assayer:performance-criterion-target`) (`entry:assayer:performance-ci-route`).

Ignored stopwatch tests with asserted wall-clock budgets are retired. Their claims and historical budgets now stand beside the Criterion identifiers as documented comparison baselines that the generated indexes and matrices resolve (`entry:assayer:performance-benchmark-projections`): a baseline tells a reader how far an estimate has moved and never makes elapsed time a pass-or-fail assertion in the ordinary suite. Playback progress remains liveness rather than latency (`cav:harness:progress-not-latency`).

A benchmark may rely on `World`, the declared scenario, guarded fixture builders, read-only test-support probes and the real engine. It may not construct private models, ledgers, pending stores or channels; substitute an engine double; author an asynchronous wait; or assert a wall-clock floor in an ordinary test. Setup that establishes a measured state proves its precondition before Criterion starts the interval, and the barrier completing asynchronous measured work remains inside that interval.

Scheduling the stopwatch tests as written is rejected because one hand-timed aggregate and one machine-dependent assertion supply neither a distribution nor durable evidence. Retiring the witnesses without replacement is rejected because the specification makes resource claims that would then have no maintained measurement. Keeping benchmarks off the harness is rejected because it creates a second population of hand-built worlds and bypasses the queue, publication and lifecycle behaviour the shared contract exists to exercise.

**Caveat (The single scenario is descriptive, and one new caller can make it false again)** · `cav:harness:incremental-adoption`

The single-scenario decision is the contract, and the adoption that made it a description of the tree has run to its end: fixture-side construction on every caller surface enters through the one scenario type, the parallel configuration, entity, report and wait vocabularies are gone, and the recognizers find no retired declaration and no authored completion wait left (`entry:assayer:harness-scenario-unification`) (`entry:assayer:harness-polling-retirement`). What the claim rests on is that census rather than a compiler diagnostic: the test-support surface is public, so a helper whose callers disappear is found by a search and not by dead-code analysis (`obs:assayer:testing-architecture-unreported-decay`).

What would make the claim false again is one new caller that declares common scenario state, common barrier meaning or a wait of its own, which is why each of those is stated as a recognizable violation and why the checker that would catch them has no owner yet (`sec:assayer:harness-implementation-conventions`) (`obs:assayer:harness-implementation-convention-enforcement`). The end state remains one scenario around the specification's concurrency model (`chap:spec:concurrency`) and the one-writer decisions (`dec:structural:write-serialisation`) (`dec:concurrency:single-steward`). The common scenario choice names that destination (`sec:assayer:testing-harness-concept-one-scenario`); the landed skeleton and residual-race repair are its foundation (`entry:assayer:harness-stage-skeleton`) (`entry:assayer:harness-stage-clock-residual-race`), and the retained maintenance and multi-channel seams are declared exceptions rather than survivals of the vocabulary that retired (`dec:harness:specialised-side-harnesses`).
