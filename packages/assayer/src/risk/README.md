## Unit test matrix · `tab:assayer:risk-unit-test-matrix`

**Table (Unit test matrix)**

| Test | Area | Claim |
|------|------|-------|
| (`test:unit:prior-level-blend-weight-near-zero`) | risk | The anchor earns weight only by being more certain than the sister is on the features they share. Two models sitting at the same isotropic prior are equally uncertain, so the anchor gets essentially none of it and the sister's own view stands. At cold start, borrowing from a model that knows no more than you do would add nothing but its bias. |
| (`test:unit:time-correction-cancels-in-weight`) | risk | The blend weight is a ratio of the two models' uncertainties, and the staleness correction multiplies both of them, so it cancels exactly: doubling the correction leaves the weight bit-for-bit unchanged. Time passing makes both models less trustworthy, not one of them relatively more so, and the weight is computed from the raw forms to keep it that way. |
| (`test:unit:time-correction-affects-variance`) | risk | Where the staleness correction does land is the uncertainty: raising it moves the blended variance. A model that has not been updated in a while is not wrong in a knowable direction, so what ages is the confidence attached to its answer rather than the answer itself. |
| (`test:unit:projected-qf-matches-submatrix`) | risk | Reading the sister's uncertainty over just the anchor's features by scattered indexing gives the same number as physically cutting that submatrix out and working on it. The projection is a restriction of the full covariance, not an approximation of one, so the weight it feeds compares like with like. |
| (`test:unit:mixture-variance-non-negative`) | risk | The three-term mixture stays non-negative across a wide sweep of weights, component variances and disagreeing means. The cross-term that accounts for the two models disagreeing only ever adds, so no combination of inputs can produce a negative variance for a caller to take a square root of. |
| (`test:unit:qf-clamp-on-negative-eigenvalue`) | risk | A covariance that has drifted very slightly indefinite through accumulated rounding yields a negative quadratic form, and the blend clamps it to zero before treating it as a variance. The alternative is a negative uncertainty propagating into the weight ratio and the mixture, where it would be far harder to recognise as arithmetic noise. |
| (`test:unit:kappa-eff-soft-transition`) | risk | The calibration constant follows the blend weight through a smooth transition rather than a switch: at no weight it is the sister's, at full weight the anchor's, and around the regime boundary it sits near the midpoint. Because the two regimes are calibrated separately, a hard switch would put a step discontinuity in the probability an entity is assigned as its weight crosses. |
| (`test:unit:p-bad-uses-kappa-eff`) | risk | The risk probability is the sigmoid of the blended score divided by the blended calibration constant, and the division is not optional: at a constant of two the probability is visibly that of the halved score and demonstrably not that of the raw one. Skipping the division would return the model's uncalibrated confidence as though it were a probability. |
| (`test:unit:no-overflow-at-large-rho`) | risk | An extreme score saturates the probability instead of breaking it: a blended score far outside the ordinary range still yields a finite value, pressed up against one. An entity the model is overwhelmingly sure about produces an actionable near-certainty rather than a non-finite value that would poison every downstream computation. |
| (`test:unit:blended-estimate-is-convex`) | risk | The blended score is a genuine convex combination of the two models' own scores at the weight the blend chose — even when they disagree in sign. It can therefore never fall outside the interval the two models bracket, so borrowing from the anchor cannot produce a verdict neither model would endorse. |
| (`test:unit:symmetry-optimised-qf-matches-naive`) | risk | Computing the quadratic form as a diagonal plus a doubled upper triangle reaches the same value as iterating every element of the matrix. Exploiting the covariance's symmetry halves the work and changes nothing about what the uncertainty means. |
| (`test:unit:dot-product-correctness`) | risk | The linear predictor behind every score in this module is an ordinary elementwise-product sum over the feature vector and the model's means, with no scaling or reordering hidden inside it. |
| (`test:unit:quadratic-form-identity-gives-norm-squared`) | risk | Against an identity covariance the quadratic form reduces to the squared norm of the feature vector — the known answer for the case where the model has no correlations and unit variance everywhere. This anchors the column-major indexing: a transposed or mis-strided read would not land on this value. |
| (`test:unit:variance-diagnostics-populated`) | risk | The component variances reported alongside the blend are real quantities and not decoration: each is non-negative, they recombine into the blended variance through the same three-term formula the blend used, and the sister's variance restricted to the shared subspace never exceeds its variance over all features. A reader can therefore reconstruct why the weight came out where it did instead of taking the number on trust. |
| (`test:unit:anchor-only-w-near-one`) | risk | The converse of an anchor that earns nothing: an anchor orders of magnitude more precise than the sister over the shared features takes nearly all the weight, and the blended score lands next to the anchor's own. A sister that has learned almost nothing yet defers to the model that has, which is what makes the population-level anchor useful during a cold start. |
| (`test:unit:dimension-1-scalar-case`) | risk | In the degenerate single-feature case the machinery reduces to its closed form exactly: the weight is one minus the ratio of the two scalar variances, and the blended score is that weight applied to the two scalar means. The general matrix code is a generalisation of the scalar arithmetic and not a separate approximation that happens to agree in the usual dimensions. |
| (`test:unit:intervention-effectiveness-at-call-site`) | risk | The blend publishes the sister's own score separately from the blended one so a caller can measure intervention effectiveness against it: the gap between what the inherited model expects of an entity and what the operational model expects after intervention. Only a score computed from unintervened history makes that difference meaningful, which is why the inherited score survives the blend rather than being folded away into it. |
| (`test:unit:time-correction-does-not-affect-rho`) | risk | cites (`claim:risk:the-staleness-correction-widens-the-uncertainty-without-moving-the-point-estimate`) |
| (`test:unit:golden-vector-known-output`) | risk | Run end to end on small fixed matrices, every published quantity — both quadratic forms, the weight, both linear predictors and the blend of them — matches values worked out by hand. This is the pin that catches a refactor which keeps all the internal identities consistent while quietly changing what the blend computes. |
| (`test:unit:mixture-variance-identity`) | risk | The blended variance is exactly the variance of the two-component mixture the blend represents, recovered independently from the moment identity in a regime where the weight is strictly interior and the two models disagree. The blend is therefore not two models averaged with an uncertainty bolted on: the disagreement between them is itself part of the uncertainty reported, which is the only honest answer when the models are pulling in different directions. |
| (`test:unit:a-model-whose-floors-have-never-acted-borrows-nothing`) | risk | A model whose floors have never acted has borrowed nothing, and the reading says so along every direction rather than along the ones it was asked about. A fresh model's precision is its construction prior alone: no rebuild has applied a spectral floor and no clamp has bound, so both tracked masses are zero and the numerator of the share is zero whatever the direction. This is the property that keeps the whole mechanism off the path of a healthy deployment: a widening that fired a little on every verdict would be a systematic inflation of every uncertainty the package reports, which is exactly what a calibration refit would then spend its evidence undoing. |
| (`test:unit:the-borrowed-share-rises-as-the-direction-turns`) | risk | The share has an angle, and so does the widening it drives. A model that has clamped every coordinate and then learned about one of them has borrowed a small part of the precision along the direction it learned and a large part of the precision along the directions it did not, so turning the direction of interest from the first toward the second raises the share, and raises it monotonically rather than at some threshold. The variance the tempering reports rises with it over the same sweep, which is the end-to-end statement the ruling makes: two requests against one model, differing only in which direction they ask about, receive different uncertainties. Degradation is not equally applicable to every verdict, and which verdicts it applies to is decided by where the model's evidence actually is, not by an aggregate that has averaged that question away. The share stays strictly below one throughout, and that is a property of the engine rather than of this sweep. The prior precision a model is constructed at is not tracked as floor mass and so is not counted in the numerator, so a direction the floors hold up entirely is a limit the arithmetic must handle rather than a state a live model reaches. The saturation is tested against that limit where it can be reached, which is on the tempering itself. |
| (`test:unit:the-borrowed-share-blends-by-the-weights-the-variances-take`) | risk | Two models that have borrowed differently blend their shares by the same weights the blend gave their variances, and the disagreement between them dilutes the result rather than inheriting a share of its own. The three-term mixture's last term is the extra variance from not knowing which model is right; that is uncertainty their evidence produced, and crediting a floor with it would widen a verdict twice for one cause. The identity this test checks is the definition read back off the result: borrowed variance over total variance, with the components weighted as the blend weighted them. |
| (`test:unit:the-covariance-reading-is-the-definition-at-sigma-phi`) | risk | The share the assessment path reads from the published covariance is the share the model itself would report, because the two are one function evaluated along one direction. The definition divides the floors' contribution to the precision along a direction by the precision along it, and needs the precision matrix, which is not published. The identity `(Σφ)ᵀB(Σφ) = φᵀΣφ` supplies the denominator from the covariance alone, and `Σφ` is the direction whose precision holds that variance up — so the published reading is the definition evaluated at `Σφ`, and this test asserts exactly that by asking the model for its own reading there. The model whose two readings are compared has been rebuilt, because the identity is an identity only where the pair the model holds are actually inverses; between rebuilds they disagree by the clamp's own accumulated contribution, which the drift monitor reads and which no reading through the covariance can see. |
| (`test:unit:empty-buffer-returns-old-kappa`) | risk | A refit with nothing to learn from returns the calibration it was given, unmoved: both constants come back as they went in, the reported movement is zero, neither regime is marked fitted and both effective sample counts are empty. A refit triggered before any labels have arrived is a no-op rather than a reset to whatever the fit's default would be. |
| (`test:unit:synthetic-convergence-kappa-2`) | risk | Given outcomes generated by a known calibration constant, the fit recovers that constant from a starting point well away from it. The search is therefore doing statistics rather than tracking its own initialisation: it finds the scaling that the data actually implies. |
| (`test:unit:regime-separation`) | risk | One buffer holding entries from both regimes, each generated with its own calibration constant, yields two fits that both succeed and end up clearly apart. The regimes are fitted independently, so a sister-dominated score and an anchor-dominated one are not forced to share a scaling that suits neither. |
| (`test:unit:minimum-sample-guard`) | risk | Plenty of entries is not the same as enough of each outcome. A regime with fifty negatives but only two positives is left unfitted and keeps its previous constant, because the minimum is enforced on the positive and negative counts separately. Fitting a sigmoid to data that is one-sided would push the constant to a bound on the strength of two labels. |
| (`test:unit:stub-valued-entries-converge`) | risk | When every score in the buffer is identical the loss surface carries no information about the scaling, and the search still terminates with a finite, positive constant inside its bounds — it drifts to a boundary rather than diverging or returning something unusable. A stub-valued or saturated period leaves calibration harmlessly inert instead of breaking it. |
| (`test:unit:flat-scores-leave-successive-refits-bit-identical`) | risk | A buffer of zero scores is worse than uninformative — it is silent. Every candidate sharpness predicts a half for every row, so the cross-entropy is one constant across the whole search range, and what comes back is not a fit but the bound the iteration walks to while every comparison it makes is a tie. Two buffers sharing nothing else — different lengths, different base rates, different importance — return the same sharpness bit for bit, and the drift between them is not small but exactly nothing. That is what makes a drift bound applied to such a pair vacuous: it is not that the calibration held steady, it is that no calibration was performed, and the bound would pass at any width whatever. The neighbouring tests fit a fixture whose classes overlap for exactly this reason (´tab:assayer:harness-scenario-tolerances´). |
| (`test:unit:overlap-ladder-fit-lands-at-the-interior-optimum`) | risk | Classes whose scores genuinely overlap put the fit at an interior optimum rather than against a search bound: the overlap ladder's fitted constant lands on the root of the stationarity condition its own counts imply, and sits some four units of log-κ clear of either end of the search range. The preceding test is the contrast that gives this one its point — a buffer whose scores carry no information leaves the search resting on a bound, where the returned constant is an artefact of how the iteration breaks a tie and no later assertion about it can discriminate anything. An interior optimum is what makes the fitted constant a statement about the data, and it is the precondition the search's own unimodality argument rests on. |
| (`test:unit:overlap-ladder-drift-bound-admits-and-rejects`) | risk | Multiplying every score by a factor multiplies the fitted constant by that same factor, which is what makes the declared drift bound a two-sided assertion: a four-percent perturbation of the scores moves the constant and stays inside the bound, while a ten-percent one moves it past and the bound rejects it. Both halves are asserted because a bound that only ever sees movement it admits is indistinguishable from a bound that admits everything — the rejecting case is what shows the figure is deciding something (´tab:assayer:harness-scenario-tolerances´). |
| (`test:unit:search-within-bounds`) | risk | A fitted constant never leaves the configured search interval, even when the data was generated by a constant near its lower end. The search brackets the whole of log-space between the two bounds and can only ever narrow inwards, so the operator's limits are a guarantee about the output and not merely a starting hint. |
| (`test:unit:no-nan-in-loss`) | risk | The cross-entropy stays finite across the whole search range even for scores far outside anything the model would ordinarily produce, including a confidently wrong one. The loss takes a logarithm of the predicted probability, so a single saturated score would otherwise contribute an infinity and make every candidate constant look equally bad. |
| (`test:unit:sigmoid-clamped-never-zero-or-one`) | risk | This is why: the calibration sigmoid is held strictly inside the unit interval, at scores of a thousand in either direction as much as at zero. Neither endpoint is ever returned, so the logarithm the loss takes of it and of its complement always has an argument. |
| (`test:unit:precompute-newest-first-recency`) | risk | The recency discount is anchored at the newest entry, which carries full weight, with each older one worth a fixed fraction more of the discount — so the newest entry in a buffer outweighs the oldest. Anchoring at the newest rather than the oldest keeps the weights bounded by one however long the buffer has been running. |
| (`test:unit:buffer-push-evicts-oldest`) | risk | The calibration buffer is bounded and drops from the front: pushing beyond capacity leaves exactly the most recent entries, in order. Memory is therefore fixed however long the instance runs, and what is sacrificed for that is always the oldest evidence — the evidence recency weighting was already discounting most heavily. |
| (`test:unit:delta-cal-log-space`) | risk | How far the calibration moved is measured as the absolute difference of the logarithms, not of the constants themselves. Movement is therefore relative: a step from one to two counts the same as a step from ten to twenty, which is what a downstream threshold on "the calibration has shifted" needs, since the constant is a scaling. |
| (`test:unit:golden-section-terminates`) | risk | A tolerance so tight that floating-point arithmetic can never satisfy it does not hang the search: the iteration cap ends it and a finite constant comes back. The refit runs on the model owner's thread, so a convergence criterion that cannot be met has to cost a bounded number of iterations rather than the instance. |
| (`test:unit:anchor-refit-label-index-updated`) | risk | A refit that actually fitted the anchor regime is recorded against the label count at which it happened, moving the tracker's stamp off its initial zero. The reactivation trigger measures how long the anchor has gone unfitted from that stamp, so a refit that succeeded but was not recorded would make the anchor look permanently overdue. |
| (`test:unit:regime-transition-shared-with-blend`) | risk | Calibration and blending divide the regimes with one shared transition function, agreeing exactly across the weight range rather than approximately. Two copies that drifted apart would mean an entity was blended as one regime and calibrated as another, and the mismatch would show up only as a mis-calibrated probability with nothing pointing at its cause. |
| (`test:unit:delta-cal-exceeds-threshold`) | risk | The reported movement is large enough to be acted on when the calibration genuinely moves: data generated by a constant three times the one in force produces movement past the default drift-reset threshold. The threshold is therefore reachable by an ordinary recalibration, not a bar only a pathological one could clear. |
| (`test:unit:has-sufficient-anchor-samples-true`) | risk | The cheap pre-check admits a buffer that a real fit would accept: a hundred anchor-dominated entries carrying both outcomes clears the minimums. This scan is what decides whether a reactivation refit is worth running at all, so it has to say yes when the anchor really could be fitted. |
| (`test:unit:has-sufficient-anchor-samples-false`) | risk | The check counts each entry by its regime rather than by its existence, so fifty sister-dominated entries contribute almost nothing toward the anchor's minimums and the answer is no. Counting entries outright would report a busy sister-regime instance as having anchor evidence it has never gathered. |
| (`test:unit:sample-floor-reads-undiscounted-count`) | risk | The sample floor reads the undiscounted weighted count — the same quantity the cheap pre-check computes — so an anchor regime whose evidence is old but sufficient is fitted rather than abandoned. Under a recency-discounted floor the same buffer sits in a trap: the pre-check keeps saying refit, the fit keeps refusing, the refit bookkeeping never advances, and a full search runs on every label. Recency belongs to the objective, which tracks the current score distribution; the floor is an evidence census and takes no discount. |
| (`test:unit:snapshot-counts-weighted-below-threshold`) | risk | The published regime counts are weighted samples, not a census against a hard threshold: a buffer whose entries all sit just below the old 0.3 split still reports substantial anchor evidence, because each record enters the anchor count at its transition share times its importance weight. A threshold tally would have reported zero anchor evidence here — and, through the risk basis, no anchor maturity — while the fit's own floor read a weighted count near half the buffer. |
| (`test:unit:sigmoid-clamped-matches-stable-sigmoid`) | risk | The calibration sigmoid is the crate's shared stable sigmoid with a clamp applied, matching it exactly wherever the clamp is not binding. There is one definition of the logistic curve in the package and calibration adds only the log-loss guard on top — a second implementation would eventually disagree with the one deciding probabilities on the assessment path. |
| (`test:unit:golden-section-monotonicity`) | risk | Every iteration of the search strictly narrows the bracket, on real weighted data and in both of the branches the comparison can take. Because the interval only ever shrinks, the loop's progress toward its tolerance is guaranteed by the step itself rather than by the iteration cap catching a search that had stalled. |
| (`test:unit:boundary-convergence-all-positive`) | risk | Data that is adverse wherever the score is high and benign where it is very low drives the constant toward the steep end of its range, because the sharpest sigmoid is the one that best reproduces such a clean separation. The search leaning on a bound is the correct answer for near-separable data rather than a failure to converge. |
| (`test:unit:recency-weighting-dominance`) | risk | When the older and newer halves of the buffer were generated by different calibrations, the fit follows the newer one — landing well past the geometric midpoint the two halves would give if they counted equally. Recency weighting is what makes calibration track a shifting population instead of averaging over its whole history. |
| (`test:unit:calibration-property-decile-bins`) | risk | The probabilities the fitted constant produces mean what they say: sorted into deciles, every bin's mean predicted probability sits close to the rate at which its entries actually turned out adverse. Recovering the right constant is not the goal in itself — the goal is that a host reading a risk of one in five can act as though one in five is what it will be. |
| (`test:unit:new-initialises-to-prior`) | risk | A freshly constructed channel state holds exactly the priors it was given and nothing else: the live counts start equal to the prior counts, which are kept separately so the state always knows how much of itself is belief rather than observation. |
| (`test:unit:update-fail-increments-alpha`) | risk | A challenge the entity failed is one count of evidence that challenges do catch adverse actors, and it moves only that side of the Beta state — the pass count is left exactly where it was. Recording an outcome therefore shifts the estimate without inventing evidence for the other conclusion. |

| (`test:unit:estimate-returns-posterior-mean`) | risk | The challenge effectiveness a host reads back is the Beta posterior mean — the catch count as a share of all counts — so the prior and the observations are combined by the same arithmetic rather than the prior being a separate special case that has to be blended in later. |
| (`test:unit:estimate-variance-correct`) | risk | The estimate travels with the Beta posterior variance, which is strictly positive and strictly below the variance of a single coin flip at the same mean. That gap is the whole point of accumulating counts: a host reading the pair can tell a well-evidenced catch rate from a barely-evidenced one, which a point estimate alone would hide. |
| (`test:unit:effective-sample-size-computation`) | risk | Effective sample size counts observations and not belief: a state that has seen nothing reports zero however heavy its prior, and fifty recorded outcomes report fifty. | (`test:unit:sufficient-evidence-threshold`) | risk | Sufficiency is a verdict on the observed count against a host-chosen bar: a state fresh from its prior fails it, and the same state passes once enough real outcomes have accumulated. A host can therefore decline to act on an estimate that is still mostly prior. |
| (`test:unit:time-decay-reduces-effective-sample`) | risk | Time pulls the accumulated counts back toward the prior, so a run of observations left to age is worth less than it was — but never nothing: the effective sample falls and stays positive. Evidence fades rather than being discarded, which is what lets an estimate follow a changing challenge mechanism instead of being pinned by however busy the channel once was. |
| (`test:unit:time-decay-half-life`) | risk | The fade has a calibrated rate rather than an arbitrary one: at the default hourly factor, waiting the algebraic half-life leaves an accumulation worth half what it was. | (`test:unit:serde-roundtrip`) | risk | Companion state is the host's to persist, so it survives a trip through serialisation with both pseudo-counts and its decay rate intact. Losing the decay rate on restore would be the quiet failure: the counts would look right and would then age at the wrong speed. |
| (`test:unit:tracker-unknown-channel-returns-default-estimate`) | risk | A channel the tracker has never heard of still answers, with the default estimate rather than an absence. Derivation can therefore run from the first request on a new channel, before any challenge has been observed, instead of the host having to seed every channel it might route to. |
| (`test:unit:tracker-update-records-channel-result`) | risk | Recording an outcome on an untracked channel brings that channel's state into existence from the defaults and hands back the estimate the outcome produced, already moved above the neutral half. The host never has to register a channel before reporting on it. |
| (`test:unit:tracker-health-report-contains-channel`) | risk | The Companion reports on itself: every tracked channel appears in the health report with a finite estimate, a finite variance, the count of evidence behind them and the sufficiency verdict at the host's threshold. | (`test:unit:time-decay-preserves-mean-increases-variance-reduces-n-eff`) | risk | Ageing costs confidence, not opinion. Decay is therefore the right shape for a stale channel: the estimate is not silently rewritten, it is merely believed less. |
| (`test:unit:estimate-decays-on-read-without-mutating`) | risk | A read is the posterior at the moment of reading: asked about an idle channel six months on, the estimate comes back with the counts decayed to the present — the same numbers a mutating decay would produce — while the stored state itself has not moved by a bit. An undecayed read is unconservative in exactly one direction, a variance too tight for evidence that has aged; and a read that wrote would let a diagnostic pass erode the evidence it was only supposed to look at. |

| (`test:unit:importance-weight-balanced`) | risk | When the two classes arrive equally often there is no imbalance to correct, and both classes come out at unit weight. The correction is therefore inert on balanced data rather than being a constant tax applied regardless. |
| (`test:unit:importance-weight-rare-positive`) | risk | Each weight is the reciprocal of twice its class rate, and the point of that form is what it buys: at any class rate, each class contributes exactly half the total gradient weight — rate times weight is one half on both sides.
