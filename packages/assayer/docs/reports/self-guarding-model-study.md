# The floor is coordinatewise, so a refused factorisation is a verdict and not a condition to repair · `rep:assayer:self-guarding-model-study`

This report is evidence for a decision, not the decision. It was commissioned to answer a contract question about the regularised recompute: when the plain Cholesky factorisation of a model's precision matrix is refused and the cascade's second phase succeeds by adding a diagonal shift, is the shifted inverse an accepted answer or a violation of what the model promises. It argues that it is a violation, that the model should guard its own invariant, and that input which would break the invariant should be refused with an error that reaches whoever supplied it.

It changes no specification, no record and no implementation. Where it concludes that a record must change it drafts the replacement text here, in a fenced block so that nothing in it mints or cites, and leaves the record alone.

## What the model promises, stated once · `sec:assayer:selfguard-invariant`

**The promise is positive definiteness, and nothing in the engine bounds the spectrum.** The specification requires the marginalised precision to be positive definite by its minimum eigenvalue (`req:gaussian:positive-definiteness`), and the substrate carries symmetry as a type invariant restored after every mutating operation with no raw mutable access at all (`dec:representation:matrix-substrate`), (`dec:substrate:no-raw-access`). Definiteness is not carried the same way. `SymmetricMatrix` has two constructors and two trust levels: `from_computation` at ``src/linalg/symmetric.rs:111`` audits nothing outside a debug build, and `from_storage` at ``src/linalg/symmetric.rs:129`` returns a `Result` whose error is `AsymmetryError` — it validates the symmetry of a restored matrix and says nothing about its spectrum. The type therefore guarantees symmetry unconditionally and definiteness not at all, which is the asymmetry every entry in the next section inherits.

**The replenishment floor is coordinatewise and the specification says so in the requirement's own words.** The floor clamps each diagonal entry after each forgetting step at $\lambda_\text{floor}$ (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`), and the requirement states unprompted that this "arrests coordinatewise diagonal decay" and "does not bound $\kappa(B)$: equal positive diagonal entries can coexist with an arbitrarily small eigenvalue through the off-diagonal structure". The implementation is exactly that and no more: `SymmetricMatrix::clamp_diagonal_min` at ``src/linalg/symmetric.rs:219`` walks the diagonal and lifts each entry below the floor to the floor, comments that the change is diagonal-only, and touches no off-diagonal entry. It is called once, from the leverage-bounded update at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1078``.

**So the floor does not guarantee factorisability, and the answer to the commission's first question is negative.** A Cholesky factorisation succeeds exactly when the matrix is positive definite in the working arithmetic; a diagonal floor bounds the trace from below and leaves $\lambda_\text{min}$ free. The engine's own bridge module states the same distinction between the two readings it offers: the diagonal pair "bounds the spectrum without determining it, and only this one answers whether the matrix is positive definite", written on `eigenvalue_min_max` at ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:540``. The consequence for the ruling is the load-bearing one. A refused plain factorisation is not a borderline case the floor failed to cover; it is a *verdict* that the matrix in hand is not positive definite in the working arithmetic, which is to say that the invariant stated by the positive-definiteness requirement (`req:gaussian:positive-definiteness`) was already lost before the factorisation was attempted. The second phase does not restore it. It shifts the matrix by $\delta I$, factors a different matrix, and returns that matrix's inverse as though it were this one's — with the eigenvalue that failed raised by exactly $\delta$ and every other eigenvalue raised by the same amount (``src/linalg/bridge.rs:375``, the shift; ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:502``, the inverse taken from the shifted factor).

**One record states the floor as a spectral bound and is wrong on the corpus's own evidence.** The precision guarantee (`inv:guarantee:precision`) reads, in full, that "the prior replenishment floor bounds the posterior's condition number from below, and the synchronisation monitor detects the drift between the tracked precision and covariance before it becomes numerically material" — minted at ``docs/spec/reference-guarantees.md:89`` and mirrored at ``docs/spec.md:4867``. Its first clause contradicts the requirement it cites in the same breath. A package audit already found this and recorded the repair as made: the epsilon audit lists "a lower bound on precision diagonals bounds spectral condition number" among the claims it replaced, replaced by "the coordinatewise-only guarantee in (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`)" (``docs/schur-epsilon-audit.md:200``), and separately records that the floor "is not a spectral floor" (``docs/schur-epsilon-audit.md:18``). The requirement was rewritten; the guarantee that cites it was not. The two records disagree in their letter, but they do not leave the invariant unstatable, because only one of them is arguing: the requirement carries the argument for why a diagonal bound is not a spectral one, and the guarantee carries an assertion with no argument behind it. The invariant is therefore statable — and stating it obliges an amendment, drafted at (`sec:assayer:selfguard-records`).

**A second gap in the same requirement matters for the surface walk.** The floor is required "after each forgetting step", and the epsilon audit notes that bulk decay "also scales precision without applying the floor required after each forgetting step" (``docs/schur-epsilon-audit.md:18``). That is still true at this commit: `BayesianLinearModel::apply_time_decay` at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:237`` scales the precision and does not clamp, and the only `clamp_diagonal_min` call in the package is the one inside the label update. So the requirement's normative clamp is applied on the label path and skipped on the bulk-decay path, which is one of the entries the next section classifies.

**The invariant this report holds the model to is therefore the following, and it is narrower than the corpus's current letter.** Between recomputes, a model's precision matrix is symmetric — unconditionally, by the type — and is *intended* to be positive definite, with the diagonal bounded below by $\lambda_\text{floor}$ on the paths that apply the clamp. Positive definiteness is the property that makes every downstream consumer sound: the covariance is its inverse, so a non-definite precision matrix is a posterior with negative variance along some direction, which is the reading the conformance audit gave at high severity when the marginalisation verification was a diagonal test (``docs/plans/conformance-audit.md:2243``). That particular divergence has since been repaired — the verification is spectral at ``src/model/marginalise.rs:1098`` — and the audit's argument for *why* a non-definite precision matrix is severe survives the repair of the one site it was written about. It is not bounded by the floor, is not carried by the type, and is checked nowhere on the maintenance path. The commission's question is what should happen when the check that does exist — the factorisation itself — returns a verdict against it.

**The configuration the shift uses is not the configuration the invariant is stated in.** `RegularizationConfig` at ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:113`` documents `delta` as "held at the model's own replenishment floor (´req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor´)", and its `Default` at ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:134`` sets `delta` and `epsilon` to $10^{-3}$, which is the shipped $\lambda_\text{floor}$ (``src/config/types.rs:298``) under the shipped $\lambda_\text{prior}$ of $0.1$ (``src/config/types.rs:297``), the pair the configuration table also carries (``docs/spec/reference-configuration.md:29``, ``docs/spec/reference-configuration.md:30``). The two are equal by coincidence of defaults and not by construction: every construction site in the package takes `RegularizationConfig::default()` (``src/owner/label_path.rs:390``, ``src/owner/label_path.rs:1455``, ``src/config/types.rs:509``), so a host that raises `model.lambda_floor` and leaves the regularisation configuration alone gets a shift below the floor it declared, and one that lowers it gets a shift above. The coupling the doc comment asserts exists in the defaults only. This is recorded here because a report that measured the shift without recording which configuration produced it would be repeating a fault the desk already carries against the drift study.

## The mutation surface · `sec:assayer:selfguard-mutation-surface`

**The walk's result is the study's central finding, and it is not the one the commission's framing anticipated. No entry on the maintenance path can carry a positive-definite precision matrix off positive definiteness from anything a host supplies.** Every arithmetic entry either preserves definiteness by algebra or already refuses. The three entries that can install a non-definite precision matrix all take it from *storage* rather than from a caller, and the one remaining route to a non-definite matrix arising in flight is rounding on a matrix the engine has itself made ill-conditioned. The ruling's "inappropriate input" therefore lands on the persistence boundary and nowhere else, and the rest of the cascade's traffic is the engine's own arithmetic, which needs the separate answer the subsection below gives.

| Entry | Site | Can it break definiteness? | Mechanism, and whose fault the input is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Bulk time decay | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:237`` | No, for definiteness; yes, for the floor clause | A uniform positive scalar is isospectral up to the factor. It applies no clamp, so it can carry a diagonal below the floor the requirement mandates after a forgetting step |
| Extension | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:266`` | No | Block diagonal with $\lambda I$ and zero couplings; the spectrum is the union of the two blocks' |
| Hibernation restore | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:345`` | **Yes** | An archived block is written over positions whose couplings the extension zeroed. The block crosses the persistence boundary and its admission test reads its minimum *diagonal* |
| Permutation | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:390`` | No, under a valid index list | $PAP^\top$ is isospectral. A list that is not a permutation is caught by a debug assertion only |
| Marginalisation, general | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:449`` | No | $B'$ is verified against its spectrum before adoption, and the fallback is a principal submatrix of a definite matrix, which is definite by interlacing |
| Marginalisation, rank one | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:562`` | No | The same contract, discharged by a Gershgorin certificate where that carries and by the spectrum where it does not |
| Restore from a snapshot | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:788`` | **Yes** | $B \leftarrow \Sigma^{-1}$ through the cascade. A shifted inverse is accepted as though it were the inverse, and only the cascade's terminus errors |

| Leverage-bounded label update | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1045`` | No, for the precision; yes, for the covariance | Scale, clamp, and a positive-semidefinite rank-one addition. The covariance takes the reciprocal downdate and can lose semi-definiteness to rounding, which the next label's leverage reading refuses |
| Recompute-outcome application | ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1129`` | No, for the precision | It installs a covariance and never touches $B$. What it can install is a covariance that is not $B^{-1}$ |

**The label update cannot break the precision matrix, and the algebra is short enough to check.** The three mutations at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1075``, ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1078`` and ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1081`` are: scale by $\gamma_\text{eff}$, clamp each diagonal up to $\lambda_\text{floor}$, and add $w_\text{eff} \hat\varphi \hat\varphi^\top$. Scaling by a positive factor multiplies every eigenvalue by it. Clamping raises diagonal entries and therefore adds a non-negative diagonal matrix, which is positive semidefinite. The rank-one term is positive semidefinite for a non-negative weight. A definite matrix plus two semidefinite matrices is definite, so the composition preserves the property exactly, and no choice of feature vector, weight or target changes that. The one input that could break it is a negative or non-finite effective weight, and that is refused before any mutation at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1067`` — in every build profile, with the code stating why a debug assertion would have been the wrong instrument there: it "would have made the release build the one that carried the negative weight through, which is the build where nobody is watching".

**So the leverage-bounded update is the wrong place to look for the ruling's bite, and the commission's own candidate for the probe is refuted at the algebra.** The commission proposes "a label whose bounded update still drives a pivot below zero" as one shape the probe might take. No such label exists. What a label can do is degrade the *covariance*, whose Sherman–Morrison downdate at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1089`` subtracts a rank-one term and is therefore the one arithmetic on the path that can lose the property it is meant to keep. The engine reads that loss on the next label as a leverage that is no longer non-negative and refuses, which is the mechanism the surfacing section takes as its model.

**Marginalisation is the entry that already does what the ruling asks, and it is worth naming as the precedent rather than as an exception.** `verify_positive_definite` at ``src/model/marginalise.rs:1098`` reads the spectrum, floors the verdict at a resolution proportional to the largest eigenvalue and the width, and treats a non-converging eigensolver as a refusal, on the argument that there is then no verdict to adopt on. Its documentation states in its own words why the diagonal test it replaced was wrong: the Schur correction is subtracted from the diagonal and added to the off-diagonal structure alike, so a $B'$ can emerge with every diagonal comfortably positive and a negative eigenvalue carried between them. A refused $B'$ falls back to $B_{kk}$, a principal submatrix of a definite matrix and definite by interlacing. The path therefore never adopts a matrix it has not certified, at a cost of one eigendecomposition per lifecycle event, and the rank-one path pays a Gershgorin pass instead wherever that settles the question. This is the shape the ruling wants, already shipped, at the one entry that was audited for it.

**The three storage entries are where the ruling's inappropriate input actually is.** All three take a matrix from a serialised artefact, and every artefact crosses the boundary through one deserialiser: the matrix type's own, at ``src/linalg/serde_support.rs:121``, which calls `from_storage` with a tolerance of $10^{-12}$. That call validates symmetry, refuses a triangle disagreement above the tolerance, and returns `AsymmetryError` — the boundary's only verdict. Definiteness is not asked about, so a matrix that is symmetric to the bit and indefinite by a whole eigenvalue passes the boundary and is installed.

**Checkpoint restore installs the matrix with no numerical check at all, and it is the cleanest inappropriate input in the surface.** `BayesianLinearModel::from_checkpoint` at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:862`` is infallible: it takes the precision and the covariance as already-built values, debug-asserts that their widths match the mean's, and moves them into the model. A checkpoint that has been truncated, hand-edited, written by an older build with a different convention, or restored from the wrong deployment therefore becomes the model's live precision matrix. Nothing on the maintenance path will notice until the first recomputation, at which point the plain factorisation is refused and the shift returns the inverse of a matrix nobody asked to invert. The suite's own witness for the gap between the two tests exists already — a two-by-two matrix of ones on the diagonal and twos off it has a strictly positive diagonal and eigenvalues of three and minus one, asserted at both readings in `eigenvalue_min_max_parts_from_the_diagonal` — so a checkpoint carrying that matrix is admitted by every check standing between the file and the model.

**Snapshot restore is the entry that already has the error channel, and it declines to use it for the case the ruling is about.** `from_parameters` at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:788`` returns a result whose error arm fires on the cascade's terminus only. The clean and regularised arms are matched together on one line at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:810``, so a covariance that could not be factored is inverted after a shift and the resulting precision matrix is installed with no diagnostic and no flag. The code says so itself: a live marker at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:806`` asks for the regularised branch to be split "so revert logs the correction count before accepting the result, which is the two-phase cascade's own signal". The gap is disclosed rather than accidental, and the repair the ruling asks for is one step further than the repair the marker asks for: not a log before accepting, but a refusal instead of accepting.

**Hibernation restore admits a block on a diagonal reading, which is the same insufficiency one level up.** `survives_floor` at ``src/model/hibernation.rs:350`` refuses a non-positive or non-finite decay factor and then tests the aged minimum precision against the floor, where the minimum is over the block's diagonal. Its own comment is explicit that the test is "the replenishment floor read forward", which is the right test for the question it asks — has this entity been forgotten — and not a test of definiteness at all. `restore_self_structure_block` at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:345`` then writes the aged block onto positions whose couplings the extension left at zero, so the matrix is block diagonal there and its spectrum is the union of the block's with the rest's. An indefinite archived block transplants its negative eigenvalue directly into the live model. The block type derives the serialisation traits at ``src/model/hibernation.rs:73``, so the archive is a persisted artefact on the same footing as the checkpoint.

**Two further entries are guarded only in a debug build, which is worth recording even though neither is the ruling's target.** The permutation at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:390`` establishes that its index list is a permutation inside a `cfg(debug_assertions)` block; a release build handed a list with a repeat gathers the same row twice and produces a singular matrix. The decay factor at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:237`` is debug-asserted into the half-open unit interval; a release build handed a non-positive or non-finite factor scales the spectrum through zero or destroys it. Neither list nor factor is host input — both are computed inside the engine, the factor through the clock's shared functions (`dec:clock:shared-functions`) — so classifying them as inappropriate input would be a category error. They are recorded because a guard that exists only where nobody is watching is the pattern the label update's own refusal comment argues against, and the two entries answer that argument differently from the entry that made it.

### Rounding the engine accumulates is not input, and it needs its own answer · `sec:assayer:selfguard-rounding-is-not-input`

**The route to the regularised phase that the drift study actually measured is none of the three storage entries.** That study drove the shipped engine at a feature width above a thousand and recorded hundreds of regularised recomputations per run — 331 against 611 across six runs of one configuration (`sec:assayer:drift-run-spread`) — with no corrupt checkpoint, no hibernated block and no snapshot revert anywhere in the picture. It attributes the transition directly: the plain factorisation was "inverting a matrix whose smallest eigenvalues have gone far below anything the diagonal floor bounds" (`sec:assayer:drift-transient`). The precision matrix was built entirely by the label update from valid labels, and the update, as shown above, cannot subtract definiteness. What it can do is build a matrix whose least eigenvalue is many orders below its largest while every diagonal entry sits at or above the floor — exactly the state the floor's own requirement warns is compatible with it — and at that conditioning the rounding of the scale, the clamp and the rank-one addition can put the computed least eigenvalue on the wrong side of zero.

**That gives the ruling a boundary it must state, because the two cases want opposite dispositions.** A non-definite matrix arriving from a file is a contract violation by whoever wrote the file, and the error partition already says a caller's defect is a hard error because the caller can fix it (`dec:degradation:error-partition`). A non-definite matrix arising from rounding on a matrix the engine itself made ill-conditioned is nobody's input: erroring on it hands the host a failure it cannot act on, which is the argument the rejected fallible assessment lost on (`disc:degradation:fallible-alternative`), and it would arrive on the ordinary label path at whatever rate the geometry produces — hundreds per run at the width the drift study drove.

**The honest reading is that the two need different names, and that the cascade currently gives them the same one.** The regularised variant at ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:151`` is returned for both, and the caller cannot tell them apart, because the distinguishing fact is not in the matrix: it is in where the matrix came from. A shift on a matrix restored from storage is repairing someone's data; a shift on a matrix the update built is reporting that the engine's own conditioning has outrun its arithmetic. The call-site argument at ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:98`` already carries the distinction needed — periodic, marginalisation, revert — and its documentation says it is "used for structured logging only; failure policy is the caller's responsibility, because a site wanting different behaviour changes the configuration it passes rather than the function it calls". Making failure policy vary by call site is therefore not a new mechanism but a use of one already threaded through the shared utility and currently discarded at the point of decision.

**The consequence for the conditioning trigger has already been measured and is not re-argued here.** The drift study's second recommendation is that the condition estimate is pinned above its threshold by the floor itself, so the engine recomputes on every label indefinitely, and that taking the estimate below threshold cut recomputations over twelve hundred labels from 1100 to 11 and the wall for the same schedule from 410 to 331 seconds, with the synchronisation error one per cent *lower* (`sec:assayer:drift-recommendation-falsifier`). That is a live desk item rather than this report's to decide, but it bounds how often the rounding case can arise: at the shipped floor the cascade is entered about once per label per model at that width, so any policy that errors on the rounding case errors at that rate until the trigger is repaired. A bite that refuses the shift without the trigger repair standing in front of it would take a deployment down, and the ordering section treats that as a hard dependency rather than a preference.

## What rejection means, and where the error can surface · `sec:assayer:selfguard-surfacing`

**What each synchronous caller does with an error today is the first thing to establish, because three of the five already have an error channel and two of the three throw the news away.**

| Entry | Channel today | What the caller does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Construction | Panic on a non-positive or non-finite prior | Refuses to build; the builder validates the configuration first, at ``src/api/builder.rs:437`` |
| Extension | None; infallible | Nothing to do |
| Marginalisation | Diagnostics, never a result | The working copy folds each model's diagnostics into a ledger and takes a fallback flag from them, at ``src/snapshot/working.rs:1021`` |
| Hibernation restore | A refusal result | The lifecycle logs at info and extends at the prior instead, at ``src/owner/lifecycle.rs:861`` — a principled degradation with a stated alternative |
| Snapshot restore | A revert error, on the terminus only | Propagated through `from_snapshot` at ``src/snapshot/working.rs:471``, and its one caller logs at error, counts a revert failure, and synthesises a cascade terminus to halve every model's interval at ``src/owner/label_path.rs:1492`` — while keeping the working copy it failed to replace |
| Checkpoint restore | None; infallible | Nothing to do; the payload is installed at ``src/snapshot/working.rs:741`` |

**The snapshot-restore row is the one worth reading twice.** When the revert fails, the engine has just decided that the working copy is corrupt, has failed to rebuild it from the last published snapshot, and then continues with the corrupt working copy — logging, counting, and shortening the cadence so that the cascade gets another attempt sooner. The comment says as much: the halving gives the cascade "a chance to restore consistency". So the deepest existing failure path in the package ends by asking the cascade to repair what the cascade has just failed to repair, and the ruling removes the arm it is asking for. That is not an argument against the ruling; it is the clearest statement of why the present arrangement has no bottom.

**One configuration fact belongs beside that row.** `revert_all_state` builds its model configuration and its regularisation configuration from `Default` at ``src/owner/label_path.rs:1454`` and ``src/owner/label_path.rs:1455`` rather than from the running deployment's, so a revert rebuilds every model at the default prior precision whatever the deployment declared. It is recorded here rather than argued: it is a separate defect, it is not this report's to fix, and it matters to the ruling only because it means the revert path's shift magnitude is the default one under every configuration.

### Option (i): a synchronous guard before the acknowledgement · `sec:assayer:selfguard-option-synchronous`

**This option has nothing to guard, and that is a stronger objection than its cost.** `Assayer::label` at ``src/api/label.rs:125`` consumes the pending entry, sanitises, journals and enqueues; the model is not reachable from that thread at all. But the decisive point is the surface walk's: no label input can carry the precision matrix off definiteness, because the update's three mutations are a positive scaling, a positive-semidefinite diagonal addition and a positive-semidefinite rank-one addition. A guard placed before the acknowledgement would therefore be a guard on a violation the entry cannot commit.

**Its costs, if it were installed anyway, are three and each is structural.** It would need a completion variant, because the state the guard must test is the state at application time, which is after however many labels are queued ahead of this one — and there is no completion variant by decision (`dec:surface:no-variants`), on the argument that the label surface has one shape. It would change what the journal means: today a journaled label is a label that will be applied on recovery, and a guard evaluated before the journal would make it a label that may be refused after recovery too, which is a durability change and not a numerics one (`dec:durability:checkpoint-journal`). And it would break the third step's unconditionality, which the three-step update exists to secure precisely so that no mutation ever has to be undone (`dec:posterior:three-step-update`). The option is refused on the first ground and would be expensive on any of the other three.

### Option (ii): rejection at application, surfacing per label through the ledger and published health · `sec:assayer:selfguard-option-ledger`

**This option is buildable and its cost is a new retained surface the corpus has no policy for.** The host's per-label information ends at the acknowledgement; after that it reads aggregates. The health surface is tiered and keyed by model rather than by label (`dec:health:tiered-queries`), and the marginalisation ledger it would extend is a lifetime aggregate plus a per-event aggregate carried on a lifecycle result (`entry:posterior:skip-counter`) — neither of which is a per-label record. A per-label rejection record is a new structure keyed by an identifier the engine deliberately consumes at the boundary (`dec:surface:consume-at-boundary`), so it would need a retention policy, a bound, and an eviction rule, which is three decisions the ruling does not settle.

**It also inherits a defect the drift study measured.** The compact summary's synchronisation reading is pinned by one model's structural constant, so an aggregate on that summary "cannot report a real excursion on any other" model (`sec:assayer:drift-recommendation-falsifier`). A per-label rejection count published the same way would be dominated by whichever model is structurally worst, which is the model the rounding case fires on. The option delivers news at the rate the geometry sets and in a shape that hides which model it is about.

### Option (iii): rejection at application, the label dropped, a hard health flag · `sec:assayer:selfguard-option-drop`

**This option is already built, which is the most useful fact in this section.** CP5 reads the working copy after a label's updates and reverts the label's whole effect where any model refused its update or any model carries a non-finite mean or a non-positive precision diagonal (`tab:runtime:numeric-checkpoints`). The mechanism at ``src/owner/label_path.rs:1240`` reads a shared refusal tally, counts a revert on the published summary, rebuilds the working copy from the last snapshot, logs at error, and republishes. The table's own prose gives the argument the ruling would give: "an assessment must answer, so its four checkpoints substitute and disclose; a label may be refused whole, so its two checkpoints revert rather than repair, and the reverted state is the last state known good."

**Its cost is measured rather than estimated, and at the width the drift study drove it is fatal.** The cascade is entered about once per label per model there, because the conditioning trigger is pinned above its threshold by the replenishment floor itself and the counter arm is never reached (`sec:assayer:drift-growth`). A policy that drops the label whenever the cascade would have shifted therefore drops every label: the engine stops learning while reporting a revert counter climbing at one per label. That is not a degradation the host can act on either, because the host supplied nothing wrong.

**And CP5's precision reading is the diagonal test, so it would not catch the case anyway.** The checkpoint's stated reading is "a non-finite or non-positive precision-diagonal entry", which is the necessary-and-not-sufficient test the corpus's own requirement argues against and which the marginalisation path has already abandoned in favour of the spectrum. Option (iii) as written would reject the labels whose precision diagonal went wrong and admit exactly the matrices the ruling is about.

### Option (iv): refuse by provenance, and let the terminus the corpus already has carry the maintenance path · `sec:assayer:selfguard-option-provenance`

**The shape this study recommends splits the disposition by where the matrix came from, because that is the only place the distinction the ruling needs is available.** The two cases are a corrupt artefact and the engine's own conditioning, and the surface walk shows they arrive at disjoint entries.

**At the three persistence entries, a definiteness verdict is taken once, at restore, and a failure is a hard error on the restore call.** This is the error partition applied unchanged rather than extended: a checkpoint or an archive carrying a non-definite precision matrix is "a caller supplying something the contract does not admit", which the partition already calls a hard error "because it is the caller's defect and the caller can fix it" (`dec:degradation:error-partition`). The caller here is a host performing a recovery, and its available actions are real ones — restore an earlier checkpoint, refuse to start, re-derive from the journal. The verdict costs one eigendecomposition per model per restore, which is the cost marginalisation already pays per lifecycle event, on an event that happens once at start-up rather than once per label. Two of the three entries need a signature change to carry it: checkpoint restore is infallible today and would return a result, and hibernation restore already returns one and would gain a variant beside the refusal it has.

**On the maintenance path, the regularised phase is retired and the outcome the corpus already has takes its place.** This is the part that needs no new error type, no new metric, and no new surface. `CholeskyInverseResult` loses its regularised variant; `RecomputeOutcome` loses its regularised variant; what a refused factorisation produces is the cascade terminus, which already retains the maintained covariance, already emits a health event at ``src/owner/label_path.rs:2169``, already increments a per-model counter that reaches both health tiers, and already halves the effective interval under the adaptive cadence. The three-way outcome collapses into the two-way one the failure posture was written for. Retaining and flagging survives; repairing does not. That is precisely the distinction the commission asks be kept: the observability of a repaired state is worth keeping even when the repair is refused, and here it is kept by keeping the flag and deleting the repair.

**The same arithmetic is a modelling choice at one site and a misreport at another, and that asymmetry is the ruling's real content.** The diagonal shift raises every eigenvalue by its own size and does nothing else, which the bridge documents at ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:329``. Applied inside the label update, that is a declared weak prior with an exactly stated effect on the posterior — which is what the replenishment floor already is, on the diagonal, and what the requirement calls "equivalent to a weak coordinatewise prior that does not fully decay". Applied inside the factorisation, the same shift is a claim to have inverted a matrix that was not inverted. The first is a model the host can read; the second is a number the host cannot distinguish from an exact one except by a flag it must remember to check. Moving the shift from the second site to the first is therefore not a softening of the ruling but its implementation: the model guards its invariant by *maintaining* it, and stops being asked to repair what it should not have lost.

**That is the finding that makes the ruling expensive, and it must be stated plainly: followed through, the ruling forces a spectral guarantee.** If the model may not accept a shifted inverse, then a refused factorisation is a state the model must not reach, and nothing in the engine prevents it reaching that state, because the floor is coordinatewise. At the width the drift study drove, the plain factorisation is refused routinely on a matrix built entirely from valid labels. Retiring the second phase without first bounding the spectrum would leave those models with no recomputable covariance at all — the terminus retains the maintained one, which then drifts without a correction for ever, and the synchronisation monitor would read exactly that. So the ruling's implementation is not one bite but a chain: repair the trigger, install something that actually bounds the least eigenvalue, and only then retire the phase that has been standing in for that bound. The bites section orders them that way and says what happens if the order is broken.

**A spectral floor is the candidate, and its cost is named rather than assumed.** The cheapest form is the same $\delta I$ the cascade adds, applied in the update immediately after the diagonal clamp, which raises the least eigenvalue by $\delta$ per label and is therefore a declared prior rather than a repair. Its cost is that it is not free of modelling consequence: the floor bounds how much confidence a coordinate may lose, and a spectral floor bounds how much confidence any *direction* may lose, which is a stronger statement about the prior than the corpus has yet made. Whether that statement is acceptable is a modelling question and is not this report's to settle; what this report establishes is that some statement of that kind is required for the ruling to be implementable at all. The drift study drew the same line for a different quantity and left it there deliberately, noting that raising the floor "is a modelling change, since the floor bounds how much confidence a coordinate may lose" (`sec:assayer:drift-recommendation-falsifier`).

### Option (v): write the shift into the model, so that the inverse is honest · `sec:assayer:selfguard-option-declared-shift`

**Costing option (iv) turned up a fifth shape, and it removes the misreport without introducing an error the host cannot act on.** The defect in the present arrangement is not that a shift is applied. It is that the shift is applied to a temporary: `cholesky_regularised` at ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:375`` forms `mat.add_scaled_identity(config.delta)`, factors that, and the caller installs the resulting inverse while the *model keeps the unshifted precision matrix*. So the model ends up holding a $B$ and a $\Sigma$ that are not inverses of each other by construction, and it holds them that way permanently rather than transiently.

**The drift study's headline reading is that inconsistency, measured.** It records the synchronisation error settling near thirty on the affected model and states the mechanism directly: "the health surface reports its success as a persistent error, because the monitor compares the shifted inverse against the unshifted matrix" (`sec:assayer:drift-transient`). A monitor reading $\lVert B\Sigma - I\rVert_F$ against a $\Sigma$ that was never $B^{-1}$ is reporting a fault that is not drift and cannot be recomputed away. Writing the shift into the model makes $\Sigma = B^{-1}$ true again, and the monitor's reading on that model would collapse to the arithmetic's own error — which is a falsifiable prediction this report offers rather than a claim it has tested.

**Its cost is that the mutation is anti-conservative, and this is the sharpest argument in the study, because it also explains why refusing is the only fully sound answer.** Making a non-definite symmetric matrix definite by the smallest change to its spectrum necessarily *raises* the offending eigenvalue, and raising an eigenvalue of a precision matrix lowers the variance along that direction. Every repair of a non-definite precision matrix therefore claims confidence the evidence does not support, in exactly the direction where the evidence was worst. There is no conservative repair available: widening the posterior means shrinking eigenvalues toward zero, which moves a negative eigenvalue further from the definite cone rather than into it. The package's conservatism guarantee is stated as a direction rather than an absence of error (`inv:guarantee:per-observation-exactness`), and this is a mutation in the direction the guarantee excludes.

**So the three shapes are separated by what they make visible, not by what they compute.** Today's shift is anti-conservative, fires only when things have gone wrong, and is reported as a success carrying a flag. A declared spectral floor is anti-conservative, fires always, has a stated magnitude, and is part of the model the host reads. A hard error is conservative, fires at the rate the geometry sets, and hands the host nothing it can act on. The ruling as stated selects the third for input and leaves the maintenance path to be chosen between the first two; this report recommends the second and records that the choice is a modelling one and therefore the owner's.

**The declared floor's magnitude is derivable rather than chosen, which is worth stating because it removes a tuning decision from the bite.** Under decay alone a per-label additive floor $\delta_\text{spec}$ satisfies $x_{n+1} = \gamma x_n + \delta_\text{spec}$, whose fixed point is $\delta_\text{spec}/(1-\gamma)$. Setting that fixed point at the replenishment floor gives $\delta_\text{spec} = (1-\gamma)\lambda_\text{floor}$, so at the operational model's decay of $0.9995$ and the shipped floor of $10^{-3}$ the addition is $5 \times 10^{-7}$ per label, and at the sister and anchor rate of $0.9998$ it is $2 \times 10^{-7}$. The magnitude is therefore per model and follows from the two figures the configuration already carries, which is the same shape the synchronisation threshold's derivation takes (`entry:posterior:sync-visibility`). Two cautions belong with it: the shipped cascade's $\delta$ of $10^{-3}$ applied per label would settle the least eigenvalue at two, twenty times the prior precision, so the cascade's magnitude is emphatically not the right one at this site; and an addition is not idempotent where the diagonal clamp is, so the derivation above is the stationary reading and not a bound at any single label.

## The records argued the other way · `sec:assayer:selfguard-records`

**Fewer records contradict the ruling than the commission's list supposes, and that is the useful result of reading them.** Three of the six named argue the ruling's own posture already and need only their premise made explicit; two contradict it and must be replaced; one carries an assertion that was already superseded elsewhere. A seventh, unnamed in the commission, is in genuine tension with the recommended shape and is drafted here because a study that skipped it would be leaving the tension for a bite to discover.

**Every draft below is given unwrapped, one line per paragraph.** The records were authored hard-wrapped, and the repository's prose rule is that markdown paragraphs run and are reflowed when touched, so a bite applying these drafts reflows the paragraphs it replaces rather than matching the surrounding wrap. The drafts stand in fenced blocks, which participate in neither minting nor citing, so the labels and citations inside them are inert text until a record carries them.

### The two that contradict the ruling · `sec:assayer:selfguard-records-contradicting`

**The repair cascade is the record the ruling repeals, and what survives it is the ordering argument, not the second phase.** Its current argument is that ordering the phases "keeps regularisation exceptional and observable", because "a factorisation that always regularised would succeed more often and would make the distinction between a healthy matrix and a repaired one unavailable to everything downstream — including the recomputation policy, which reads exactly that distinction". Every clause of that is right and none of it survives contact with the measurement: the drift study recorded hundreds of regularised recomputations per run, so the second phase is not exceptional in the shipped engine, and the distinction the argument prizes is preserved better by refusing than by repairing — a refusal is unambiguous where a shift is a success the consumer must remember to interrogate. The replacement keeps the phase ordering as a diagnostic staging and deletes the repair.

```text
**Decision (Factorisation is attempted once, and its refusal is a verdict)** · `dec:posterior:repair-cascade`

Factorisation is attempted plainly. Its refusal is not retried under a diagonal shift: the refusal is the answer, and it says that the matrix in hand is not positive definite in the working arithmetic (`src/linalg/bridge.rs`).

The retired second phase added `δI`, factored the shifted matrix, and returned that matrix's inverse. The arithmetic was sound and the report was not: the returned quantity is the inverse of a matrix the caller did not supply, offered under a variant the caller had to inspect to distinguish from an exact answer. What made this untenable was not the risk of a consumer forgetting to inspect but the frequency — the shift was reached routinely rather than exceptionally at feature widths above a thousand (`rep:assayer:synchronisation-drift-study`), so a mechanism justified as exceptional and observable was neither.

The same shift is retained, at a different site and with a different meaning. Applied in the update it is a declared floor on the posterior's spectrum, with a stated effect on every eigenvalue and a magnitude the configuration carries; applied in the factorisation it was a claim about which matrix had been inverted. The arithmetic is identical and the epistemic status is opposite, which is why the move is a repair of the record rather than a weakening of it.
```

**The cascade's infallibility is the second contradiction, and it is only half wrong.** Its current argument is that "the cascade has no failing exit", that "nothing upstream is handed an error from a factorisation", and that the flag "is what keeps the specification's precision statement a bound rather than a precondition". The first two clauses go; the third is the one the commission asks about specifically, and it goes with them, because the statement it protects was never a bound the floor delivered. What survives is the terminus and what it retains.

```text
**Decision (The maintenance path never fails the call; the restore paths do)** · `dec:posterior:cascade-never-fails`

A factorisation refused on the periodic recomputation path does not fail the call. The terminus retains the maintained covariance and flags it; the call returns. A factorisation refused while restoring a model from a persisted artefact does fail the call, and the error reaches the host performing the restore.

The split is the error partition rather than an exception to it (`dec:degradation:error-partition`). A refusal on the maintenance path is the engine's own conditioning outrunning its arithmetic on state the engine built from valid input, and a host handed that error has no repair available — which is the ground the fallible assessment was rejected on (`disc:degradation:fallible-alternative`). A refusal on a restore path is a corrupt artefact, which is a defect of whoever wrote it and one the writer can act on: an earlier checkpoint, a refusal to start, a rebuild from the journal.

What this record no longer claims is that the flag keeps the precision statement a bound rather than a precondition. The statement it referred to was a spectral claim the replenishment floor does not support (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`), so the flag was standing in for a guarantee that was not there. The guarantee is now made where it belongs, by a floor on the spectrum rather than by a note attached to a repaired result.
```

### The three that already argue the ruling's posture · `sec:assayer:selfguard-records-agreeing`

**The conditioning guard is the ruling, stated in the corpus, one record above the cascade.** Its argument is that the guard "declines a computation it expects to fail rather than attempting it and handling the failure", and that "the difference is not performance: an ill-conditioned solve does not reliably fail, it returns a number, and the failure modes that produce a plausible wrong correction are the ones no downstream check would catch". Substitute "factorisation" for "solve" and that is the case against the second phase, written by the corpus about a neighbouring site. It needs no replacement text. What it needs is to stop being read as a local convenience: the amendment is one sentence added to it, naming the cascade as the site where the same reasoning was not applied and now is.

**The infallible correction survives whole, and its last sentence is why.** It argues that the uncorrected fallback "is a valid, conservative posterior over the surviving dimensions, not a damaged one", and closes: "The positive-definiteness the specification requires holds on both paths, which is what makes declining an option at all." That is exactly the ruling's structure — declining is available because the alternative is sound — and the record is the model the other entries should be brought to. Its one gap is a premise it leaves implicit: the fallback is definite because a principal submatrix of a definite matrix is definite, which fails if the parent is not definite. Under the ruling the parent's definiteness becomes something the model guarantees rather than assumes, so the premise is worth writing down.

```text
The fallback is sound rather than merely convenient: the uncorrected block is a valid, conservative posterior over the surviving dimensions, not a damaged one. What it loses is the sharpening the correction would have supplied. The positive-definiteness the specification requires (`req:gaussian:positive-definiteness`) holds on both paths, which is what makes declining an option at all — and it holds on the fallback path by interlacing, because a principal submatrix of a positive definite matrix is positive definite. That premise is the parent's definiteness, which the model now guarantees rather than assumes (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`), so the fallback's soundness is a consequence of the invariant rather than a separate hope.
```

**The numerics face of the failure posture is a remark rather than a decision, and the ruling narrows it rather than repealing it.** It currently says the cascade "is this posture at the numerics layer, not a second decision", and that it "ends by retaining the best available state and flagging it, which is (`dec:degradation:retain-and-flag`) applied to a factorisation". Retaining and flagging is exactly what the terminus continues to do, so the remark's substance stands. What must change is the scope: the posture governs the maintenance path and not the restore paths, and the remark is the place a reader looks to learn that.

```text
**Remark (The numerics face of this posture, and where it stops)** · `rem:degradation:numerics-face`

The refused factorisation on the posterior maintenance path is this posture at the numerics layer, not a second decision. It ends by retaining the maintained covariance and flagging it, which is (`dec:degradation:retain-and-flag`) applied to a factorisation. Reading it as an independent choice is what previously let the corpus carry the same posture in several places with drifted wording; the record that owns the maintenance path decides how a refusal is reported, and inherits from here what happens when the state cannot be recomputed.

The posture stops at the persistence boundary. A factorisation refused while restoring a model from a persisted artefact is not a numerical pathology arising in flight but a structural contract violation by whoever wrote the artefact, and the partition sends that to a hard error rather than to a flag (`dec:degradation:error-partition`). The two dispositions are the partition doing its work rather than an inconsistency, on the same reasoning that sends observation-queue overflow to a degradation and label-queue overflow to an error (`rem:degradation:queue-face`).
```

### The one whose assertion was already superseded · `sec:assayer:selfguard-records-superseded`

**The precision guarantee's first clause is false and its second is unaffected.** It reads that "the prior replenishment floor bounds the posterior's condition number from below" — a claim the requirement it cites refutes in its own text and which the package's epsilon audit lists among the claims it replaced. The amendment is not a consequence of the ruling; it is a repair the corpus already made in one file and did not propagate to the other. What the ruling adds is the replacement's second half: under a spectral floor the clause becomes true, so the amendment is worth writing once in the form it will hold rather than twice.

```text
**Invariant (Precision is maintained within its bounds)** · `inv:guarantee:precision`

A spectral floor bounds the posterior precision matrix's least eigenvalue from below, so the matrix is positive definite at every observable point and factorisable in the working arithmetic (`dec:posterior:spectral-floor`). The replenishment floor bounds each diagonal entry and does not bound the spectrum (`req:gaussian:prior-replenishment-floor`); the synchronisation monitor detects the drift between the tracked precision and covariance before it becomes numerically material (`alg:gaussian:synchronisation-monitor`).

The three are separate guarantees and the distinction is the whole content of the statement. The spectral floor is what makes factorisability a precondition the model maintains rather than an outcome it hopes for, which is what lets a refused factorisation be read as a verdict against the invariant rather than as a condition to repair. The coordinatewise floor is a modelling statement about how much confidence a single feature may lose and bounds no eigenvalue. The monitor catches the drift neither floor addresses.
```

### The record in tension with the recommended shape · `sec:assayer:selfguard-records-tension`

**The one inversion utility is argued against varying behaviour by call site, and option (iv) varies behaviour by call site.** Its argument is that "a call site that wants different behaviour changes the configuration it passes, not the function it calls", and the reason given is the failure it was written to prevent: two records specified the utility's interface incompatibly while each certified its own side as verified, "not a wrong tolerance but two right ones that disagree". That argument is about *implementations* and *tolerances*, and the recommended shape adds neither — it keeps one utility, one signature and one set of tolerances, and makes the disposition of a refusal the caller's, which the utility's own documentation already says it is: "failure policy is the caller's responsibility". So the tension is smaller than it looks, but it is real enough that a bite would trip over it, because the natural implementation is a second entry point for restores.

```text
**Decision (One shared inversion utility, with the disposition of a refusal left to the caller)** · `dec:posterior:one-inversion-utility`

Every site that needs an inverse from a factorisation calls one utility (`src/linalg/bridge.rs`). There is no second implementation, and in particular no second set of tolerances: a call site that wants different arithmetic changes the configuration it passes, not the function it calls.

What a call site does decide is the disposition of a refusal, and it decides it outside the utility. The utility reports that the factorisation was refused and which pivot refused it; whether that report becomes a retained-and-flagged covariance or a hard error is the caller's, because the answer depends on where the matrix came from and the utility cannot know that (`dec:posterior:cascade-never-fails`). The call-site tag the utility already takes is diagnostic and does not select behaviour, which is the line worth keeping: a tag that selected behaviour would be the second implementation this record exists to forbid, reintroduced as a parameter.

The alternative is what the corpus had. Two records specified this utility's interface incompatibly while each certified its own side as verified, which is the failure a shared utility with two owners produces — not a wrong tolerance but two right ones that disagree. The interface is now one interface, recorded at (`reg:posterior:collapsed-interface`).
```

### Two tables and one requirement move with the records · `sec:assayer:selfguard-records-tables`

**The posterior's guard table loses a row and the degradation table is unaffected.** The row "Regularisation pivot threshold | ``src/linalg/bridge.rs`` | a non-positive pivot passing as positive in the second cascade phase" names a guard on a phase that no longer exists, so it leaves with the phase (`tab:posterior:guards`). A row for the spectral floor takes its place, sited in the update. The degradation record's magnitude table (`tab:degradation:guard-magnitudes`) carries no cascade row and needs no change; the spectral floor's magnitude belongs in the configuration surface beside the replenishment floor rather than in that table, because it is a declared modelling parameter with a constraint rather than a guard whose magnitude no specification fixes.

**The numeric checkpoints table's CP5 reading is the last amendment, and it is the one that would otherwise silently defeat the ruling.** CP5's stated reading is "any model with a non-finite mean entry, or a non-finite or non-positive precision-diagonal entry" (`tab:runtime:numeric-checkpoints`). That is the diagonal test whose insufficiency the corpus argues twice — at the positive-definiteness requirement and at the marginalisation verification it caused to be rewritten. Under the ruling it is the checkpoint standing between a non-definite precision matrix and the published snapshot, so it has to read what it is guarding. The replacement is a row rather than a paragraph, and its cost is the reason it is raised here rather than left implicit: a spectral reading at CP5 is an eigendecomposition per model per label, which is cubic work on the hot path, where the present reading is linear. ## The bites · `sec:assayer:selfguard-bites`

**Nine bites, in an order two of whose edges are hard dependencies rather than preferences.** Every estimate below is a gate chain rather than a work estimate: at the last measured walls in this loop a package chain is clippy at all targets and features near forty-five seconds warm, the featureless test run near a hundred and ninety seconds, the all-features run near two hundred and fifteen, an empty formatter check in seconds, and the corpus check near ten seconds warm against thirty-one cold, the difference being the linter's own release build. A full chain is therefore about eight minutes of gate wall per bite, and a bite that needs two chains because a writer proposal regenerates a projection needs about sixteen.

**Two bites are blocked on a decision and are marked so rather than estimated as though they were not.** The spectral floor is a modelling change and the failed-revert disposition is an open design question that predates this study. Nothing downstream of either can be scheduled until they are answered, and a plan that hid that behind an ordering would be presenting a dependency as a sequence.

**Bite B lands first and is the one that implements the ruling for input that is actually input.** `from_checkpoint` becomes fallible and `from_checkpoint_payload` propagates. The verdict is a Cholesky attempt rather than an eigendecomposition, on the argument the marginalisation path already found: a factorisation is the cheapest available witness of definiteness, and the restore has no factorisation of its own to reuse. The error is a variant added to the existing build error, since a restore happens while the instance is being constructed and the builder's enum is the one that already reaches a host at that moment (`dec:construction:eager-validation`). Its own test extends the suite's existing shape — `from_parameters_with_bad_sigma` already asserts that an unfactorable covariance is refused — and the new case is the matrix the suite already carries as its witness that the diagonal and the spectrum disagree. Nothing else in the plan depends on it and it depends on nothing, so it is the cheapest demonstration that the ruling is implementable.

**Bite C is the same repair at the archive and is cheaper still, because both the channel and the alternative already exist.** `survives_floor` stays exactly as it is — it answers whether an entity has been forgotten and is not a definiteness test, and conflating the two would be the mistake this study spent its first section refusing. The verdict goes beside it, and the lifecycle's existing refusal arm at ``src/owner/lifecycle.rs:861`` already does the right thing with it: it logs and extends at the prior, which is a definite block by construction. So bite C adds a variant to a refusal enum whose consumer needs no change.

**Bite D is the open question, and it is worth stating plainly that the ruling did not create it.** When `from_snapshot` fails during a revert, the engine keeps the working copy it has just judged corrupt and shortens the cadence so that the cascade can try again at ``src/owner/label_path.rs:1492``. Under the ruling the arm it is asking for is gone, so the loop has no exit. The dispositions available are a hard stop on the label path, a fall back to a fresh prior-initialised working copy, and a refusal to publish while retaining the last published snapshot as the read surface. Each is a different answer to what a host is owed when the engine cannot recover its own state, and none of them is a numerics decision. Bite E is one line — splitting the clean and regularised arms at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:810`` — and it cannot land in front of D, because it makes D's arm reachable far more often.

**Bite F is the modelling change and its cost is the anti-conservatism the surfacing section derives.** The addition goes immediately after the diagonal clamp at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:1078``, using the matrix operation the package already has for the retiring shift. The magnitude is derived per model from the decay rate and the replenishment floor rather than configured as a free parameter, which keeps it out of the class of figures the corpus dislikes; what the configuration carries is the floor it targets. The second chain is the configuration projection: a new binding row is a generated surface, so the projection is regenerated by proposal and its bytes applied verbatim rather than hand-edited. The bite is blocked on a decision because it declares confidence the evidence does not support in the worst-conditioned direction, and no amount of implementation care changes that.

**Bite G is the largest, and its cost is not in the numerics but in two published surfaces.** Retiring the phase deletes the regularised variant from the cascade result and from the recomputation outcome, and with them the arms in `apply_recompute_outcome_with_threshold`, the marginalisation inverse helper's combined match at ``src/model/bayesian.rs:94``, and the health event at ``src/owner/label_path.rs:2159``. The surfaces are the problem. `regularised_recomputes` reaches the detailed health tier at ``src/health/published.rs:224``, the compact summary carries `regularisation_count` at ``src/health/summary.rs:330``, and the checkpoint state persists the per-model counter at ``src/persistence/checkpoint.rs:250``. A retired phase leaves those three fields able to hold only zero, which is the fault the corpus names elsewhere as reporting a constant where a measurement is promised. The honest disposition is to repurpose rather than retire them: under bite F there is a declared spectral repair to count, and a counter that has always meant "this model's precision needed help" continues to mean it. That keeps the checkpoint format and both health tiers unchanged, which is why it is worth doing even though repurposing a field is normally worse than replacing it.

**Bite H is nearly free, and the reason is an irony worth recording.** CP5 needs a definiteness reading rather than a diagonal one, and a spectral reading per model per label would be cubic work on the hot path. It does not have to compute one: `check_cholesky_recomputation` runs at ``src/owner/label_path.rs:1001`` and CP5 at ``src/owner/label_path.rs:1241``, so the recomputation's verdict is already in the model by the time the checkpoint reads it, and CP5 pays a factorisation only for models that did not recompute this label. At the shipped floor, before bite A, every model recomputes on every label, so the marginal cost is zero — the trigger defect that makes the rounding case frequent is the same defect that makes this reading free. After bite A repairs the trigger the cost returns, which is the one place in this plan where two repairs work against each other, and the resolution is that CP5 reads the stored verdict and its staleness bound rather than re-deriving it.

**Bite I is documents only and is gated by the corpus check rather than by the test suite.** It applies the drafts from the records section, retires the guard-table row, amends the checkpoint row, and reflows the paragraphs it touches. It lands last because a record amended before the code it describes would leave the corpus describing an engine that does not exist yet, which is the failure the census recorded as records certifying their own side while the code adjudicated otherwise (`reg:posterior:collapsed-interface`).

**What a bite must not do, stated once because the ordering makes it tempting.** No bite retires the second phase before something bounds the spectrum. At the width the drift study drove, a refused factorisation on the maintenance path is routine, and the terminus retains the maintained covariance without correcting it — so G before F leaves the affected models with a covariance that drifts unboundedly and a synchronisation monitor reporting exactly that, which is worse than the misreport the repair is repealing. That edge is the plan's only genuine hazard, and it is why F carries a decision rather than an estimate.

## The probe, and what it changed in this report · `sec:assayer:selfguard-probe`

**The probe earned its place by asserting two facts this report depends on that nothing in the corpus asserted.** The commission offered three candidate shapes and the surface walk refuted the first two: no label can drive a pivot below zero, because the update's mutations are definiteness-preserving, and no marginalisation can leave the block indefinite, because the general path verifies the spectrum and the rank-one path verifies a certificate of the matrix it is about to adopt. The third candidate — a checkpoint that restores a non-definite matrix — is the one the walk found, so that is the seam the probe observes.

**It observes and repairs nothing, and it is ignored because its input deliberately violates the invariant.** It stands at ``src/tests/linalg_bridge.rs`` as `the_storage_boundary_and_the_cascade_both_admit_an_indefinite_matrix`, and it asserts four things in order: that the witness is indefinite by more than round-off, that the witness passes the diagonal test it exists to defeat, that the storage boundary admits it, that the plain factorisation refuses it, and that the cascade nonetheless answers with a success carrying a non-zero replacement count. The witness is the matrix the suite already carries for the two tests disagreeing — ones on the diagonal, twos off it, spectrum three and minus one.

**Its output, run once on the shared server, is three lines.**

```text
the witness spectrum: lambda_max 3.000000, lambda_min -1.000000
the witness diagonal: max 1.000000, min 1.000000
the cascade reported success on an indefinite matrix: 1 pivot(s) replaced, plain refusal at pivot 1, delta 1.000e-3, epsilon 1.000e-3
```

**Every quantity in the report's account of this seam stands in those three lines.** The witness carries a negative eigenvalue of exactly minus one and a diagonal whose minimum and maximum are both one, so the diagonal test finds nothing wrong with it and the spectral test refuses it. The plain factorisation refused at the second pivot, which is where the algebra puts it: the first pivot is one and the second is $1 - 4/1 = -3$. And the cascade answered with a regularised verdict at a replacement count of one, under a shift and a threshold both of $10^{-3}$ — so a pivot was replaced, and the report's account of which device carried the factorisation is read off that count rather than inferred from the source.

**Its central finding sharpened this report rather than confirming it, and the sharpening is about which device fabricates the answer.** The second phase has two devices and the report had been treating them as one. The diagonal shift is applied before any pivot is computed and raises every eigenvalue by its own size; beneath it the backend's per-pivot replacement stays armed, writing the shift's magnitude wherever a pivot still comes out at or below the threshold, and the returned count is the count of those replacements (``src/linalg/bridge.rs:113`` through ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:131``). A shift of $10^{-3}$ cannot lift an eigenvalue of $-1$, so on this witness the shift accomplishes nothing and the backstop is what carries the factorisation. That means retiring "the regularised phase" is retiring two devices with different failure modes, not one, and it means the misreport is worse than the report first stated: the returned inverse is the inverse of neither the matrix supplied nor the matrix shifted, but of a third matrix formed by replacing one of its pivots mid-factorisation.

**The backstop's own documented failure mode is the reason this matters beyond the probe's two dimensions.** The bridge records that the replacement "does not touch the column beneath it", so a replacement well below the value it replaced over-subtracts from what follows and "condemns the next pivot by a wider margin than the last: the sequence doubles its exponent at every step, and across a width in the thousands it reaches the format's ceiling, produces an infinity, and then a NaN" — and it records that this was reached rather than imagined, on a matrix "whose entries were all finite and none larger than a few hundred" (``src/linalg/bridge.rs:336`` through ``src/linalg/bridge.rs:357``). So the device the probe catches absorbing a two-by-two is the same device whose behaviour at production width is a divergence to a value the cascade cannot correct. The shift was installed in front of it to keep legitimate floored entries away from its threshold, which is a repair of the backstop rather than a replacement of it.

**One STOP condition was checked against the committed suite and is not met.** The commission requires a stop if the regularised phase is reached in the committed test suite today by a test that passes only because of the shift. Every committed test that reaches the second phase has the cascade as its own subject: the three regularisation tests and the two cascade tests in the bridge's own module assert what the phase does, and one of them accepts a clean verdict as readily as a regularised one; the threshold test asserts that a matrix with its whole diagonal at the replacement threshold factors under the shift with no pivot replaced, which is a test of the shift; and the recomputation tests that name the regularised outcome construct the outcome value directly rather than driving a factorisation, so they exercise the cadence bookkeeping and not the cascade. No test whose subject lies elsewhere depends on the shift, so nothing here is a finding that must be ruled on before a bite. The probe is not part of that reading: it is ignored by default and therefore not part of the committed green chain.

## Method and limits · `sec:assayer:selfguard-method-limits`

**Everything in this report is read from the source at the base commit and from the records, and the one measurement it relies on is another study's.** No engine behaviour was changed, no record was edited, no error type was touched, and no test expectation was altered.
