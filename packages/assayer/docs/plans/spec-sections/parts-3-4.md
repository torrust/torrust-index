# The Assayer Specification Sections: Parts III and IV · `reg:assayer:core-landscape-register`

This is the section-depth register for Part III (Chapters 10–12, the core models) and Part IV (Chapters 13–17, the decision landscape) of `docs/spec.md`, together with Appendix A, the rendering layer Part IV demotes to an appendix. It completes [the specification audit](../spec-audit.md) at section depth: every subsection audited individually — subject, the environment kinds it will yield, whether it binds, and how it stands against the implementation — with evidence rather than sampling.

The two Parts are audited under different rules, and the difference is the point of this file. Part III describes a system the code implements, so its sections take ordinary drift verdicts. Part IV describes a system the code does not implement: the specification is authoritative and the shipped derivation surface is the legacy party (`dec:assayer:spec-authoritative`), so Part IV sections record what the specification requires against what the legacy surface ships, and the specification is never marked wrong for diverging from the code.

The document practices the discipline it serves. The label at each heading or environment head is that environment's mint; a parenthesized label in running text is a same-owner citation; material in tables is displayed without participating. Every label minted here has area `assayer`, and every kind is drawn from a fixed registry of environment kinds, under the grammar of the label calculus. Tag forms of the superseded scheme (`dec:assayer:tags-superseded`) are written as plain text throughout, never in backticks, so that no tag of the old system can mint under the new one; section numbers appear in code font in the tables' first column only, as locators of the audited text, and carry no identity.

## Method · `sec:assayer:s34-method`

**Convention (The two verdict vocabularies)** · `conv:assayer:s34-verdicts`

Part III sections (Chapters 10–12) carry one of three verdicts. **SOUND** — the section makes a checkable claim, the claim was read against the named code, and it holds. **DRIFT** — the claim was read against the code and does not hold; the divergence is stated in full in the chapter disposition or in a finding below. **EXPOSITORY** — the section states no claim an implementation could falsify: motivation, interpretation guidance, a convergence narrative, or a derivation of a result stated elsewhere. A drift already held open by the audited deferral register is marked SOUND (deferred) and cited by its label, never re-described.

Part IV sections (Chapters 13–17) and Appendix A carry a different three. **SPEC-ONLY** — the specification requires a surface the implementation does not carry at all; the row records the future implementation burden. **LEGACY-DIVERGES** — the implementation carries a surface that answers the same need differently; the row names the legacy symbol and its location. **SHARED** — specification and legacy surface agree, in substance if not always in spelling. No Part IV row is a criticism of the specification.

**Convention (What counts as evidence)** · `conv:assayer:s34-evidence`

Every non-EXPOSITORY row cites ``path:line`` under `packages/assayer/src`, and every citation was read rather than name-matched. Where a section's claim is a default value, the evidence is the constant's definition site, not its use. Where a section's claim is a procedure, the evidence is the function that performs it. A row with no citation is EXPOSITORY by construction: there was nothing to cite.

**Data (Measurement basis)** · `data:assayer:s34-basis`

Audited 2026-08-19 against `docs/spec.md` as found, Chapters 10–17 at lines 1600–3058 and Appendix A at lines 5175–5338: 91 subsections across the eight chapters and nine in the appendix, matching the chapter-level inventory (`tab:assayer:spec-inventory-chapters`). The pre-rewrite counterparts were cross-read at `docs/spec.old.md`, concentrating on the restructured region the divergence table (`tab:assayer:spec-divergence-editorial`) maps: old Chapter 15 The Resonance Model, old Chapter 16 Resonance Derivation, old Section 17.1 Information Value, and old Appendix A Resonance Mathematics.

**Entry (Chapter 15, The Crossover Landscape)** · `entry:assayer:spec-sections-ch15`

The chapter the rewrite created, promoting the crossover structure from a step inside the old resonance derivation to Part IV's primary object, and the chapter where the audit's most interesting result sits: **the mathematics is already implemented; only the object is missing.** The legacy derivation computes each crossover as one logarithm equivalent to the specification's decomposed form — the same quantity, algebraically rearranged — and computes each crossover's variance as the risk term plus the squared challenge sensitivity times the challenge variance, which is precisely the diagonal of the rank-2 covariance (`thm:landscape:crossover-covariance`) states. The four rows that follow from that structure but are never surfaced are the chapter's real burden: the decomposition is not exposed as u and b_j, the crossovers are not returned at all, dominance is reported as an infinite crossover rather than a probability, the off-diagonal covariance is uncomputed, and credible intervals do not exist because the moment-only challenge input cannot supply the quantiles they push through (`obs:assayer:s4-posterior-flattened`). The chapter also absorbs two results the old text carried elsewhere — the catching-forfeiture threshold from old Section 16.11 and the p-independence result from old Section 16.2.5 — and both restatements are stronger than their sources.

**Entry (Chapter 16, Worked Landscapes)** · `entry:assayer:spec-sections-ch16`

## Findings · `sec:assayer:s34-findings`

**Warning (The training-eligibility table is not the implemented table)** · `warn:assayer:s3-eligibility-drift`

**New finding.** The eligibility table (`tab:eligibility:training`) is the specification's causal-inference contract: it fixes which observations inform the inherent-risk estimate. The implementation's eligibility predicate disagrees with it on two of the six rows, in opposite directions. The table says Slow is causally confounded and must not train the sister or anchor; `determine_eligibility` (`src/risk/challenge.rs:640`–`658`) returns true for Slow unconditionally, with the comment "Allow/Slow: always eligible (we observe the outcome)". The table says Challenge outcomes train the sister by default — that is the whole content of the `challenge_fail_is_unconfounded` flag, whose default the section states as true — while the same predicate admits a Challenge label only when it carries the ground-truth flag. The policy field exists (`src/config/types.rs:239`), defaults to true (`:245`), and is read by no eligibility path in the crate; the only references outside its own definition are two construction assertions (`src/tests/builder.rs:95`, `:139`) and four TODOs naming the gap (`src/risk/challenge.rs:637`–`639`, `src/owner/label_path.rs:471`, `src/risk/challenge.rs:1014`, `tests/multi_channel.rs:1511`). The consequence is not cosmetic: the sister model, whose entire purpose is to estimate risk on the unconfounded population, is trained on the confounded Slow population and starved of the default Challenge population — the opposite of the specification on both counts. This is the largest Part III drift the audit found, and the one whose correction most changes model behaviour.

**Warning (The importance-weight formulas are not the specified formulas)** · `warn:assayer:s3-importance-weights`

**New finding, and the audit's one silent divergence.** The balancing weights (`def:weighting:balancing-weights`) are specified as the reciprocal of twice the class rate: the positive weight is the smaller of the ceiling and one over twice the positive rate, the negative weight the smaller of the ceiling and one over twice its complement — a form whose defining property is that each class contributes exactly half the gradient weight, which is what the section's "Gradient balance" column asserts at 50% down to the ceiling. `compute_importance_weight` (`src/risk/challenge.rs:603`–`610`) computes the odds ratio instead: the positive weight is the complement over the rate, the negative weight the rate over its complement. The two agree only at a rate of one half, and diverge fast: at the section's own tabulated rate of 0.10 the specification gives 5.0 and 0.56 for an effective ratio of 8.9, and the code gives 9.0 and 0.111 for an effective ratio of 81 — the square of the specified ratio, and a gradient balance far from the section's stated 50%. Every row of the section's representative-rates table is therefore wrong about the shipped system, including the binding-regime analysis, which computes the ceiling's onset from the specified form. Unlike the eligibility drift this one carries no TODO and no comment: the function's documentation cites the section it does not implement. The audited deferral register formerly held the *reporting* of ceiling binding open; that reporting is now completed and retired at (`entry:assayer:defer-importance-ceiling-health`). It remains a different matter from this historical finding about the mechanism.

**Warning (The P-plus trackers share one rate, and it is a third rate)** · `warn:assayer:s3-p-plus-rate`

**New finding.** The class-rate trackers (`tab:weighting:trackers`) are explicit that the two valence-distribution trackers decay at the rates of the models they serve — the global tracker at the operational rate, the eligible tracker at the sister rate — and closes the paragraph with "No new parameter is introduced." The implementation introduces one: a single `gamma_p_plus` field defaulting to 0.999 (`src/owner/label_path.rs:215`, `:246`) is passed to both tracker updates (`:489`, `:494`). The value matches neither model's rate, and its half-life of about 693 labels is roughly a fifth of the sister's 3,500 and a half of the operational's 1,400, so both trackers react several times faster than their models retain evidence — precisely the "tracker faster than its model over-reacts to transient class-balance fluctuations" failure the section names as the reason for matching them. The section's convergence table, which derives its horizons from the two model rates, describes no tracker the code runs.

**Observation (The anchor projection selects a different feature set)** · `obs:assayer:s3-anchor-projection`

**New finding.** The per-feature table is the anchor model's definition (`def:risk:anchor-model`): fifteen named features in a fixed order, with features 13 and 14 computed inline "rather than occupying additional aggregate indices." The implementation gathers all fifteen from the feature vector (`src/feature/dimension_map.rs:423`–`441`), taking slots 1–6 as the *first six* aggregate features and slots 13–14 as aggregate positions 13 and 14. Against the aggregate block's documented layout (`src/feature/aggregate.rs:23`–`31`) the two lists agree on slot 0 (bias) and slot 1 (max cell-level max z) and on all six identity slots 7–12, which map to the named cross-dimension features exactly; they disagree on slots 2 through 6 and on 13 and 14. Where the specification asks for the standard deviation of cell-level max z, the code supplies the mean; where it asks for the cross-axis product, axis breadth, coverage and max CUSUM at slots 3–6, the code supplies the standard deviation and three per-axis max z values; and where it asks for the max raw z across Sentinels and axes and the log count of reporting Sentinels at slots 13–14, the code supplies coverage and max CUSUM. The projection is fifteen-wide in both readings and both are Ledger-free, so the contamination-resistance property that the contamination analysis (`sec:ledger:contamination`) and the anchor-floor design note hang on the measurement-only feature survives — but it survives at slot 1, not slot 13, and the feature the specification names at slot 14 is computed nowhere. The site carries a TODO naming the divergence (`:415`–`:416`): the canonical projection structure the specification calls for (map:anchor-proj) is not built, and no type of that name exists in the crate.

**Observation (Step 7 of the update is correct only under an unstated reading)** · `obs:assayer:s3-sm-step7`

**New finding, and a specification defect rather than a drift.** The leverage-bounded update (`alg:update:sherman-morrison`) lists the mean update as the seventh and last step of an ordered procedure whose sixth step has already replaced the covariance. Read in the order the section gives, step 7's covariance is the *posterior* covariance, and the formula is correct; read with the prior covariance the first two steps computed, it is wrong by the factor the Sherman–Morrison identity supplies. The section never says which, and its own step 1 defines the covariance-vector product from the prior, which invites the wrong reading. The implementation resolves it correctly and explicitly, computing the mean increment as the weight times the residual over the denominator, times the *prior* covariance-vector product (`src/model/bayesian.rs:777`–`781`) — algebraically the posterior form, and documented as such at `:717`–`:718`. The two agree; the specification is simply ambiguous where the code is not, and the rewrite should state the step against the prior product, as the code does, or say which covariance step 7 means.

**Observation (The Ledger absence threshold is named for a behaviour it does not have)** · `obs:assayer:s3-ledger-absent-naming`

**New finding, minor.** Entry deletion (`alg:ledger:entry-deletion`) specifies deletion after a threshold of consecutive absent report cycles, and is emphatic that deletion is the only correct operation because a merge would double-count. The configuration field implementing the threshold is documented as "Number of consecutive absences before cell hibernation" (`src/config/types.rs`). Hibernation is a distinct concept in this corpus with a lifecycle contract of its own (`entry:construction:hibernation`), covering deregistered Sentinels and axes, not Ledger entries. The behaviour is deletion as specified; only the field's documentation misnames it. Recorded because the outline wave will mint a label for this threshold and the code comment citing it should be migrated to that label with the wrong word corrected, per the early-conversion clause of the burn-list ruling (`dec:assayer:burn-lists`).

**Observation (The laundering exposure is new text and unverifiable by construction)** · `obs:assayer:s3-laundering-added`

The laundering discussion (`disc:ledger:laundering`) has no counterpart in the pre-rewrite text; the chapter-level divergence table records it as an addition (`tab:assayer:spec-divergence-editorial`). It is worth a section row of its own because of what kind of addition it is: not a mechanism but an accepted exposure, arguing that the temporal decay's symmetry is exploitable in the forgiving direction and that the trade was resolved deliberately toward curing staleness. Nothing in it is checkable against code — its claim is that *no* mechanism distinguishes an earned-benign label from a manufactured one, which the code confirms by absence — so it is EXPOSITORY in force but normative in consequence: it is the specification declining to defend a surface, which the outline should carry as a `cav` at full strength rather than folding into the contamination discussion around it.

**Observation (The challenge posterior is flattened at the derivation boundary)** · `obs:assayer:s4-posterior-flattened`

**New finding, and the structural reason three Part IV sections are SPEC-ONLY rather than merely renamed.** The challenge posterior (`sig:companion:posterior`) declares the challenge input as a two-variant posterior whose conjugate variant "additionally supports exact quantiles, used for credible intervals and exact dominance probabilities," with the moment-matched variant as the fallback. The Companion tracker does maintain the conjugate posterior — the pseudo-count pair is its state (`src/risk/challenge.rs:99`–`108`), incremented per challenge outcome (`:242`–`243`) and decayed toward its prior (`:228`–`229`) — but the derivation boundary accepts only a mean-and-variance pair (`src/types.rs:177`–`182`), so the conjugate structure is discarded one call before the two consumers that need it. This is why the incomplete-beta dominance probability (`thm:landscape:dominance`) and the quantile push-through (`def:landscape:credible-intervals`) are not merely unimplemented but *unimplementable at the current boundary*: they are blocked by a type, not by missing arithmetic. Recorded separately from the chapter-level Part IV finding (`warn:assayer:drift-landscape-unimplemented`) because it identifies the single smallest change that unblocks the largest share of Part IV's SPEC-ONLY surface.

**Observation (An undeclared reward field rides with the channel policy)** · `obs:assayer:s4-gamma-qt-extra`

**New finding, minor.** The legacy reward parameters carry a challenge-effectiveness time-decay field (`src/resonance/channel.rs:61`–`63`, default 0.9998) that the specification's reward struct — the derivation function (`sig:landscape:derivation-function`) and the extended rewards (`def:landscape:extended-rewards`) — does not declare, and that Chapter 14 does not describe among the policy fields. The quantity is real and belongs to the Companion Tracker, as the Core's own temporal configuration notes (`src/config/types.rs:276`–`278`); what is recorded here is that it travels inside the *channel policy* in the shipped surface, so a host configuring rewards is configuring a Companion decay rate at the same time. The specification's placement — Companion parameters with the Companion — is the better one, and the outline should not import this field into the reward environment when Part IV is written.

**Observation (What ships in place of decision fragility is the thing fragility replaced)** · `obs:assayer:s4-fragility-replacement`

**New finding.** The divergence table records decision fragility's (`sec:fragility:decision`) replacement of the old information-value sections as deliberate (`tab:assayer:spec-divergence-editorial`), and the definition (`def:fragility:definition`) argues the case at length: entropy over a rendered distribution "weights uncertainty by nothing," whereas fragility weights it by decision sensitivity. The shipped exploration surface is exactly the entropy gauge the section argues against — three Shannon entropies over the tag kernels at a single reference posture (`src/resonance/exploration.rs:52`, `:64`–`:82`), carried on the derivation output as `exploration` (`src/assessment.rs:548`–`549`). The rewrite's own accommodation of this is the ambiguity gauge (`sig:rendering:ambiguity-gauge`), which keeps the function under its old name while renaming its return type to sever the value-of-information association; in the code neither the rename nor the required disclaimer has happened — the type is still the metrics record and the module's note (`:9`–`:11`) warns only about subadditivity. The two surfaces are not variants of one idea: one ranks by decision sensitivity at a host's posture, the other by display ambiguity at a configured constant, and no host can migrate between them without changing what it asks for.

## Verdict · `sec:assayer:s34-verdict`

**Summary (What this register found)** · `summ:assayer:spec-sections-b`

Ninety-one sections across Chapters 10 through 17 and nine in Appendix A, audited individually. Part III's tallies, over 52 sections: 30 SOUND (two of them SOUND with an open deferral cited), 4 DRIFT, and 18 EXPOSITORY. Part IV's tallies, over 48 rows covering 39 sections and Appendix A's nine — the derivation function (`sig:landscape:derivation-function`) is split into three rows because its three inputs diverge differently: 17 SHARED, 11 LEGACY-DIVERGES, and 20 SPEC-ONLY. Ten findings are new and stated in full above: three warnings and four observations against Part III, and three observations against Part IV, beside one register and one caveat.

The Part III result is that the core models are implemented close to the specification and the four exceptions are concentrated in one chapter and in one theme. Chapter 11 is clean, Chapter 12 is clean, and Chapter 10 carries all four drifts — three of which are about *which observations train which model with what weight*, which is the chapter's causal contract rather than its arithmetic. The eligibility predicate trains the sister on confounded Slow labels and starves it of default Challenge labels (`warn:assayer:s3-eligibility-drift`); the importance weights use the odds ratio where the specification uses the balancing reciprocal, so the effective class ratio is the square of the specified one (`warn:assayer:s3-importance-weights`); and the class-rate trackers share a third decay rate that matches neither model they serve (`warn:assayer:s3-p-plus-rate`). The fourth, the anchor projection's feature assignment (`obs:assayer:s3-anchor-projection`), is about which features the coarse model sees. Two of the four carry TODOs at the drifting site; the importance-weight divergence carries none, and its function cites the section it does not implement. The chapter also produced the audit's one specification defect that is not drift at all: step 7 of the leverage-bounded update (`alg:update:sherman-morrison`) is correct only under a reading the section does not state (`obs:assayer:s3-sm-step7`), and the code resolves the ambiguity correctly.

The Part IV result is the inversion the rewrite did not anticipate. The rewrite demoted the resonance rendering to an optional appendix over a presentation-free landscape; the implementation ships the appendix and not the landscape. Seven of Appendix A's nine sections are SHARED, several exactly — the configuration struct matches field for field with every default identical (`src/resonance/derivation.rs:72`–`104`), the crossover-matching placement formula is implemented with the specification's own symbol (`:506`–`514`), and the kernel, bandwidths, magnitudes and dominated-tag treatment are all present — while the object they are supposed to render does not exist. And the divergence within Part IV is not uniform: Chapter 14's numbers are all correct in the shipped code, Chapter 15's crossover arithmetic and two-source variance are already computed and merely unexposed, the risk basis (`schema:risk:basis`) is SHARED field for field, the purity guarantee (`pf:landscape:purity`) holds and is tested, and Core label guidance (`sec:guidance:core`) is SHARED down to the field names and the starvation formula. The genuinely unbuilt surface is narrower than the chapter-level finding suggests, and one type change unblocks a disproportionate share of it (`obs:assayer:s4-posterior-flattened`).

**Data (The SPEC-ONLY surface, per chapter)** · `data:assayer:s34-spec-only-surface`

The future implementation burden Part IV specifies, stated per chapter so the burn register under the Assayer adoption (`dec:assayer:burn-lists`) — whose checked policy `profile.legacy-conform` and burn family `legacy.implementation` hold the legacy implementation's unlabelled remainder at zero — can track it and the outline wave can decide, per environment, whether it describes shipped work or unbuilt work, per clause 4 of (`req:assayer:spec-outline-inputs`).

**Chapter 13.** The whole output type and its two utilities. Concretely: a landscape record carrying the shared risk term, its uncertainty, the challenge posterior, a fixed-shape crossover vector of one entry per adjacent declared action pair, and a per-action regime vector; an optimal action function computed from the upper envelope of the per-action cost curves rather than by bracketing; and a fragility function over the landscape. The function signature itself must change shape — the shipped derivation takes four arguments and returns a tag profile — and the rendering-as-optional clause (`dec:landscape:rendering-optional`) requires the display configuration to stop being a required argument of the primary call. The two inputs that do not need building are the risk basis, which is SHARED, and the channel policy, which needs re-shaping rather than writing.

**Chapter 14.** Almost nothing structural: the named posture constants and the posture-as-cursor read-point, which presuppose Chapter 13's utilities, and the reporting of the challenge regime width and its domination probability, which presuppose Chapter 15's regimes. The reward surface, its defaults and its arithmetic are shipped. The one clean-up is placing the sensitivity exponents beside the rewards rather than inside them, and not importing the Companion decay field into the reward environment (`obs:assayer:s4-gamma-qt-extra`).

**Chapter 15.** Split sharply. Unbuilt outright: the dominance probability as a regularised-incomplete-beta evaluation with its per-action thresholds, and the credible intervals as a quantile push-through — both blocked by the flattened posterior boundary (`obs:assayer:s4-posterior-flattened`) rather than by missing mathematics. Computed but unexposed: the rigid-translation decomposition into its shared and per-transition parts, the crossover records themselves, and the diagonal of the rank-2 covariance. Genuinely new but small: the off-diagonal covariance and the zero-risk-variance width corollary, which follow in closed form from quantities already computed. And one behavioural change: the catching-forfeiture condition currently produces an infinite crossover where the specification wants a continuous inversion depth.

**Chapter 16.** No implementation burden of its own — the chapter is four worked examples and a summary — but the largest *verification* asset in Part IV. Its four landscapes are a complete numeric acceptance oracle for Chapters 13 through 15, stated to three decimals, and the two properties they were built to isolate (the regime table's independence of the risk estimate, and the 5.2× widening of the challenge regime when Slow is removed) are exactly the properties a first implementation would get wrong. It should be built as tests before the chapters it exercises are written.

**Chapter 17.** The whole fragility half: the fragility record with its modal action, flip probability and two evidence shares; the flip probability computed under the rank-2 covariance with the dominated-region special case; and the interpretation bands. The Core guidance half is SHARED and needs nothing. The composition pattern (`ex:fragility:composition`) needs no code at all — it becomes possible the moment Chapter 13's two utilities exist. The migration is not additive: the shipped entropy gauge is the surface decision fragility (`sec:fragility:decision`) deliberately retired (`obs:assayer:s4-fragility-replacement`), so the exploration output changes meaning, and hosts consuming it must be told.

**Appendix A.** The smallest burden in Part IV and the most misleading if read from the chapter-level finding alone. Unbuilt: the rendered examples (`ex:rendering:examples`), which depend on Chapter 16's landscapes. Re-framing rather than building: the rendering contract (`sig:rendering:contract`), which must become a function over a landscape and a risk basis rather than the derivation entry point, and the utilities (`sig:rendering:ambiguity-gauge`), which must adopt the renamed return type and carry the disclaimer the section requires. Everything else — the spectrum, the kernel, the placement formula with its existence test, the bandwidths with their floor, the magnitudes, and the dominated-tag suppression — is shipped and SHARED. The appendix is the one place in Part IV where the migration's work is to *subordinate* an existing surface rather than to build a missing one.
