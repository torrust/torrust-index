# The Assayer Specification, Parts I–II: Section Register · `reg:assayer:foundations-data-model-register`

This register audits `docs/spec.md` Parts I and II — Foundations and the Data Model — section by section, completing the chapter-level audit ([spec-audit.md](../spec-audit.md)) at the depth the campaign backlog's section-audit entry requires. One entry per chapter carries a disposition and a table with one row per section: what the section is about, the kinds the rewritten specification will mint there, whether the section binds the implementation or explains it, what checking it against the code found, and where that check was made. Every section stating a claim the code can answer was checked by reading the named symbol; sections that state nothing checkable are marked as such and carry no invented evidence.

The document practices the discipline it serves. The label at each heading or environment head is that environment's mint; a parenthesized label in running text is a same-owner citation; material in double-backtick spans is displayed without participating. Every label minted here has area `assayer`, and every kind is drawn from a fixed registry of environment kinds, under the grammar of the label calculus. Legacy forms — the specification's section numbers and the tag forms of the superseded scheme — appear only in double-backtick spans, never in running prose and never in a span that could mint.

## Method · `sec:assayer:sections-a-method`

**Convention (How this register was built)** · `conv:assayer:sections-a-method`

Measured 2026-08-19 against `docs/spec.md` at 6,084 lines and `docs/spec.old.md` at 6,589 lines, with code read at `packages/assayer/src`. The section inventory is the heading ladder itself: Chapters 1 through 9 carry one hundred subsection heads between `spec.md:24` and `spec.md:1589`, which is the figure (`tab:assayer:spec-inventory-chapters`) records chapter by chapter. The wave brief's figure of one hundred and eighteen matches neither the document nor that table, and the count taken from the heading ladder is used here instead.

Divergence against the pre-rewrite text was taken from a full normalized difference of both editions' Chapters 1 through 9 — tag annotations and status grades stripped from both sides so that substantive edits stand out — rather than by structure and distinctive string, which is the granularity (`cav:assayer:spec-audit-limits`) declares this register inherits. Drift was checked by reading the named symbol. A drift the audited deferral register already holds is cited by its label in prose beneath the table and never re-described; a drift held in the chapter audit is cited the same way; anything else stated as new is stated in full in (`summ:assayer:spec-sections-a`).

**Convention (Reading the tables)** · `conv:assayer:sections-a-columns`

The **section** column carries the specification's current number as a displayed legacy form; it locates the section for a reader of the present document and carries no identity. **Kinds** names the registry kinds the outline is expected to mint for that section's material, which is often more than one and is occasionally none. **Binds** is normative where the implementation must satisfy the section, expository where the section argues or illustrates, mixed where both, and structural where the head is a division carrying no material of its own. **Drift** is one of SOUND (checked and agreeing), NEW (a disagreement stated in full below), KNOWN (a disagreement already held as a deferral or an audit finding, cited beneath the table), or EXPOSITORY (nothing the code can answer). **Evidence** cites the file and line read; an expository row carries none.

**Entry (Chapter 3, The Valence Asymmetry)** · `entry:assayer:spec-sections-ch03`

## Part II — The Data Model · `sec:assayer:sections-part-2`

**Entry (Chapter 5, Registries and Lifecycle)** · `entry:assayer:spec-sections-ch05`

The longest chapter of Part II and the one where the specification and the implementation part company most concretely. The lifecycle algebra itself is sound in every direction checked: Sentinel, outcome-axis and identity lifecycle events extend and marginalise the right model sets, the anchor stays fixed-dimensional and moves only its projection, the removed partitions are gathered where they are not contiguous, and the axis registration computes its new dimension count by exactly the specified formula. What is not sound is the registration surface. The Sentinel registration record specified here — five fields, of which three carry the encoding contract — is two fields in the code, and every downstream claim that rests on the missing three fails with it, including the chapter's own statement that validating the declared width is the only structural validation the Core performs. Separately, the axis registration protocol under-extends the standardisation vectors whenever more than one identity dimension is registered, which breaks an invariant Chapter 9 states unconditionally, and does so on the reference configuration's own shape.

**Entry (Chapter 8, Host Signals)** · `entry:assayer:spec-sections-ch08`

The shortest chapter in Part II and, per section, the most exactly implemented. Both sections check out completely. The seven signal shapes exist with the same names and the same payload fields, the two persistence modes exist, the declaration record has the three declared fields, and the schema computes the signal block width as the sum of the declared widths with per-signal positions assigned in declaration order — which is exactly what the chapter says and exactly what the dimension-map chapter later relies on. Nothing here is deferred and nothing drifts. The one thing the chapter leaves unstated is that the shapes have different widths: the cyclic shape occupies two positions, and a reader computing the block width from the chapter alone has no way to know that.

## Verdict · `sec:assayer:sections-a-verdict`

**Summary (What the Parts I–II section audit found)** · `summ:assayer:spec-sections-a`

One hundred sections audited across nine chapters — six, seven, four, five, twenty-seven, eight, fifteen, two and twenty-six respectively. Of these, seven are division heads carrying no material, twenty-four state nothing the code can answer and are marked expository without a check, and sixty-nine were checked against the implementation by reading the named symbol. Of the checked sections, thirty-eight agree with the code, seven disagree only in ways the audited deferral register or the chapter audit already holds, and twenty-four carry disagreements new to this register. The soundness is very unevenly distributed: Chapters 6 and 8 are sound in every section, Chapter 4 is sound but for two undeclared surrogates, and Chapter 9 has more new drift than the rest of Part I and Part II combined.

The new findings, in full.

**The Sentinel registration record does not carry the encoding contract.** The specification's registration record declares five fields — identifier, name, description, declared domain width, and a coordinate-semantics record with an encoding description and a hierarchical-structure assertion. The implementation's record has two: identifier and name (`packages/assayer/src/owner/commands.rs:223`). Neither the coordinate-semantics record nor the encoding-description enumeration exists anywhere in the package. Four dependent claims fail with it. The registration protocol's first step, validating the declared width, and its statement that this is the only structural validation the Core performs, describe nothing: the actual Sentinel-registration validations are a non-empty name and a unique identifier (`packages/assayer/src/api/lifecycle.rs:65`). The width chapter's statement that the Core rejects registrations above the internal width at registration time is false for Sentinels — the range check on the declared width exists only on the identity-dimension path (`packages/assayer/src/api/lifecycle.rs:276`), and its error is worded for an identity dimension (`packages/assayer/src/error.rs:257`). The protocol's fourth step, which records the declared width alongside the routing indices so cell depths can be interpreted and coordinates normalised, records nothing. And the three stated purposes of the declarations — forcing engagement with the encoding question at registration, operational auditability in health reports, and diagnostic support for an uninformative Sentinel — have no surface at all. This is the largest single gap in Parts I and II, it is the mechanism by which the encoding contract was to be made real, and the outline must decide explicitly whether the record is specified-but-unbuilt or whether the contract is to be re-sited.

**Outcome-axis registration under-extends the standardisation vectors when more than one identity dimension is registered.** The registration handler computes the new dimension count as two per Sentinel when spatial features are enabled plus three per registered identity dimension (`packages/assayer/src/owner/lifecycle.rs:438`), and extends every full-dimension model by that count. It then builds the list of feature classes for the new positions, and pushes the three identity classes exactly once rather than once per dimension (`packages/assayer/src/owner/lifecycle.rs:450`), before pushing two per Sentinel. The extension helper appends exactly one entry per class and pads nothing (`packages/assayer/src/feature/standardisation.rs:304`). So with one identity dimension the model growth and the standardisation growth agree, and with two or more they diverge by three per extra dimension. Chapter 9 states as an unconditional invariant that the map, the model parameters and the standardisation statistics share the same dimension at every published version; this path breaks it. The reference configuration has two identity dimensions, so the divergence is on the documented reference shape, and the test that verifies the reference dimension builds the map directly rather than through the lifecycle handler and would not catch it. This was found by reading, not by executing; it should be reproduced before it is acted on, but the arithmetic is not ambiguous.

**Two of the five interaction template types have different semantics in the code than in the specification, and they are swapped.** The specification's second type generates one feature for a specific named pair of Sentinels and scales as a constant; the code's second type generates one feature for every pair of registered Sentinels (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:357`), which is the specification's *third* type. The specification's third type is the wildcard over all pairs and scales quadratically; the code's third type generates one feature per Sentinel — a Sentinel-by-aggregate product (`packages/assayer/src/feature/interaction.rs:193`, `packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:368`) — which the specification does not describe at all. The consequence is that the specification's explicit named-pair template has no implementation, the specification's warning that the wildcard type produces a large number of features applies to the code's second type rather than its third, and the convergence table's rows for the second and third types are attached to the wrong mechanisms. The first, fourth and fifth types match. Notably the lifecycle section's own prose — one matched feature per already-registered partner Sentinel — describes the code's second type rather than the specification's, so the chapter contradicts itself as well as the code.

**The interaction compilation pipeline has two stages, not three.** The specification describes declaration, resolution and compilation, with a symbolic template record carrying typed feature selectors, an interaction identifier enumeration with five variants, and a flat computation graph of resolved index triples. The code has an offset-based template enumeration with no selectors and no names (`packages/assayer/src/feature/interaction.rs:164`), a resolution map, and no compiled graph at all; the assembly path interprets templates directly. The code marks both gaps itself (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:199`, `packages/assayer/src/feature/interaction.rs:159`). The practical consequence for the specification is that the default interaction set's named templates — the products written with symbolic feature names — cannot be expressed in the implementation's vocabulary at all.

**No default interaction set exists in the library.** The specification gives a default set of five per-Sentinel templates, eight aggregate templates and one competitive template per identity dimension, and derives the default interaction dimension from it. In the code the templates are supplied by the host at construction, and the only place the specified shape appears is a test fixture (`packages/assayer/src/tests/dimension_map.rs:141`). The default interaction dimension formula is therefore a property of the reference configuration rather than of the library, and it does hold there: the interaction block measures exactly the specified count (`packages/assayer/src/tests/dimension_map.rs:150`).

**The slot-width field holds the extraction width, not the slot width.** The specification states that the map's per-Sentinel width field holds the uniform slot width including the occupancy indicator. The code's field of that name holds the extraction width without it (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:210`), and the slot width is a local computed by adding the occupancy width (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:327`). Both of the derived slot offsets the specification names — the base-Ledger offset and the per-axis Ledger offset map — are absent from the record entirely; the offsets are recomputed at each use site instead. Conversely the record carries a per-axis offset map for the *identity* blocks (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:179`) that the specification's record does not declare, although the specification does define the convention it implements. The base-Ledger offset the specification asserts is nonetheless arithmetically correct: the extraction order puts the base Ledger group after the four preceding groups (`packages/assayer/src/extraction/mod.rs:233`), and the slot prepends one.

**The anchor projection's two computed features are gathers.** The specification separates thirteen direct gathers from two computed features: one taken as a maximum over four aggregate positions and one computed from the assessment context as a function of the reporting-Sentinel count. The code gathers all fifteen, taking the last two directly from aggregate positions (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:440`), and marks the gap itself (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:415`). The projection record type the specification names does not exist. The six identity-sourced gathers, however, are exactly right: every one of the six cross-dimension features the identity chapter assigns to a named anchor position is gathered at that position (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:432`).

**The per-Sentinel standardisation bootstrap is unimplemented, and its two constants are documented as something else entirely.** The accumulator type exists (`packages/assayer/src/feature/bootstrap.rs:89`) and the Sentinel slot has a hook to start it (`packages/assayer/src/report/slot.rs:94`), but nothing outside a test ever calls the hook, and the two constants the soft blend needs — the accumulation count and the blend weight — are declared at exactly the specified values and read nowhere in the package (`packages/assayer/src/feature/standardisation.rs:187`). Worse, their documentation describes them as the replicate count and confidence level of a statistical bootstrap resampling procedure, citing an unrelated specification section; a maintainer tuning them by their documentation would be tuning a procedure that does not exist. The batch initialisation of the neighbouring section, by contrast, is genuinely wired (`packages/assayer/src/lib.rs:552`).

**The concordance recalibration window is half the specified size.** The specification calibrates the per-axis thresholds from the last ten thousand sub-scores; the configured window capacity is five thousand (`packages/assayer/src/config/types.rs:400`). The percentile and the recalibration cadence are as specified (`packages/assayer/src/config/types.rs:402`).

**The synchronisation error is a diagonal maximum, not a Frobenius norm.** The specification defines the synchronisation error as the Frobenius norm of the residual between the precision-covariance product and the identity. The code computes the maximum over the diagonal of the absolute deviation of that product from one (`packages/assayer/src/model/recompute.rs:367`). These are different quantities with different scales, which matters because the specification's threshold is expressed as a constant times the dimension — a form that only makes sense for the Frobenius norm. The absence of any threshold and of the interval-shortening rule is the deferral already held; the definition mismatch is not.

**The Schur positive-definiteness check tests the diagonal, not the spectrum.** The specification requires verifying that the marginalised precision is positive definite by its minimum eigenvalue. The code tests the minimum diagonal entry (`packages/assayer/src/model/marginalise.rs:260`), citing the specification section as though the two were the same. A positive diagonal is necessary but not sufficient, so the fallback can fail to fire on a matrix the specification would reject. The two regularisation constants are, by contrast, exactly right: the delta resolves to the prior precision times the specified epsilon (`packages/assayer/src/model/marginalise.rs:66`, `packages/assayer/src/config/types.rs:207`), and the condition guard is at its specified value.

**Two of the eight assumed Sentinel properties are enforced, not assumed.** The specification frames all eight as properties of the interface the Core depends on. Report ingestion validates two of them structurally and rejects violations with named errors — a missing root cell and a non-dyadic or duplicated interval (`packages/assayer/src/report/validation.rs:70`, `packages/assayer/src/report/validation.rs:113`, `packages/assayer/src/report/validation.rs:138`). This is the code being stricter than the specification, which is the benign direction, but it changes the binding force of the section: two rows are preconditions the Core checks, not assumptions it makes, and the outline should mint them accordingly.

**Smaller findings.** The identity budget record carries an observation-capacity field the specification does not declare (`packages/assayer/src/types.rs:237`), alongside the three declared fields it is missing. The measurement-state smoothing factor the identity chapter gives a default for has no configuration surface; the value appears only as a local constant in a test (`packages/assayer/src/identity/cell_state.rs:379`). The extraction is computed and stored in single precision (`packages/assayer/src/extraction/mod.rs:191`) while every model consuming it is double, a split the specification never states. The width decomposition recorded beside the extraction-width constant mislabels which group is which — it names the second group for coordination, the third for the chain, and the fourth for outcomes, where the specification's order is chain CUSUMs, chain structure, coordination (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:78`); the total is right and the ordering in the extraction routine is right, only the summary is wrong. And the competitive range's temporal entry ordering, which the specification specifies in detail down to a tie-break on the canonical dyadic key, is not implemented: cells take the order of the vector the caller supplies (`packages/assayer/src/feature/dimension_map.rs:401`).

**What the outline must not miss from these Parts.** Six things. First, the encoding contract needs a decision before Chapter 2 or Chapter 5 is written: it is the specification's most emphatic host requirement and it has no implementation surface, so the outline must record it as specifying unbuilt work or re-site it, exactly as (`req:assayer:spec-outline-inputs`) requires for Part IV. Second, Chapter 9 must be outlined against the code's taxonomy, not the document's: three of its interaction sections describe a template system that does not exist, and transcribing them would carry the error into the rewrite. Third, the version-consistency invariant should be minted as an invariant with a stated check, not as a bullet inside a prose list, because it is currently breakable by a path in another chapter and nothing detects that. Fourth, the sections that are sound are sound in a checkable way and should be minted so they stay that way — the extraction widths, the block layout, the anchor projection's six index assignments and the signal shapes are all tables of independent claims that a tool could verify against the constants they mirror, and the campaign's standing rule (`goal:assayer:tools-over-discipline`) argues for exactly that. Fifth, the two numeric losses of Chapter 3 and the deleted paragraph of Chapter 1 want a deliberate ruling in the outline review rather than silent inheritance. Sixth, the specification's own fenced code blocks carry tag forms of the superseded scheme inside Rust comments — the dimension map record's field comments cite them throughout — and a lint over participating prose will never see them, so the burn-list machinery (`dec:assayer:burn-lists`) must count fenced regions of the specification if the tag census is to reach zero.

**Caveat (What this register did not do)** · `cav:assayer:sections-a-limits`

Drift was checked by reading, not by executing: no test was written and nothing was run beyond the repository check, so the standardisation under-extension and the interaction-type swap are read from the source and should be reproduced before anything is changed on their account. Sections whose claims belong to a later Part — the model update path, the assessment pipeline, the concurrency tiers, the alarm summary — were followed only far enough to confirm that the Part I or Part II section naming them is consistent, and their own audit belongs to the parallel lanes. The divergence pass is complete at section granularity for these chapters, taken from a normalized difference of both editions, but it is not a sentence-level reconciliation: a rewording that preserved the substance was not recorded, and only edits that changed a number, deleted a statement, or moved a claim are noted above.
